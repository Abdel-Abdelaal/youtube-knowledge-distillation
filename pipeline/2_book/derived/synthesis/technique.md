# Technique

**Bottom line**
- Execution is half of results: technique is how tonnage gets directed to the target muscle, so every rep must be controlled and replicable, first rep to last, with form never altered to lift more. Mechanical failure (form breakdown) means you did something wrong.
- The lumbar spine stays neutral and rigid under load: it is a bridge between legs and upper body, never a crane. Upper-back rounding is tolerable later (and even used on purpose in some back work); lumbar flexion is not.
- Control the negative (at least ~1 s, never let gravity drop it), slow down most near the stretch; the positive is the fatigue gauge. Tempo matters but ranks below load and range of motion; time under tension is a by-product, not a target.
- Range of motion: travel distance under load in the lengthened range builds muscle; the lockout and the top squeeze add almost nothing. Cut range that only stresses tendons or causes pain (full elbow extension on curls, butt wink, dead hangs), never range that stresses the muscle to add weight.
- No universal form: leverages (height, limb length, hips) dictate stance, grip, depth and lift choice; but beginners need rigid rules first, and exotic tweaks are earned.
- In recent years he calls cheat reps and body English ego lifting and prefers strict reps, after years of preaching (and doing) cheat curls.

### Form breakdown, weight selection and ego lifting
- **Bodybuilders must own technique from rep one to the last: the 10th rep must look as crisp as the first, only slower; form breakdown is acceptable in strength sport, not for size** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2025; confidence: high; near failure only speed changes, never form {{UzTfF1VduxM@320}} {{Z35bJjrc_MY@64}} {{pUpQypTV3Ck@61}} {{waeSP7DBmig@730}}

- **Control the negative; a negative not resisted (gravity does the work) doesn't count; it is the most important half of the rep** — P; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "the weight should not lead you"; cables/machines invite dropping it, free weights force control {{YtNUZjBxdtE@378}} {{VB0LzTb3SQM@470}} {{O5xVdqgt8Uo@1880}} {{uOAia_t5c_I@438}}

### Range of motion and partials
- **ROM for hypertrophy = distance the load travels under tension, and it's relative to leverages; there is no objective "full ROM" (standards come from competition)** — P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high; a tall lifter's parallel squat can travel more than a short lifter's ass-to-grass {{-wk3jSpm434@0}} {{-wk3jSpm434@65}} {{GH8tqzGTgH8@829}} {{1apseBbt0-o@439}} {{GH8tqzGTgH8@911}}
