# Injuries

**Bottom line**
- In his view injuries don't "just happen". Nearly all come from mismanaged load, fatigue, volume or intensity, from grinding reps you knew were bad, or from repeating the same pattern too often. He treats them as the lifter's responsibility, and a natural should get warning signs before anything snaps.
- Most acute injuries happen in the "red zone", where the negative turns into the positive. The last grindy reps of heavy sets are the most dangerous. Risky lifts belong in higher rep ranges (roughly 6-15+) and on fresh days, with no grinding.
- Muscle should always give out before tendon, joint or spine. Tendon pain, SI-joint "burn" or next-day low-back pain means the programming or technique is wrong, not that you trained hard.
- Tendinitis is treated by training, never by rest or drugs. Take at most 1-2 days off, avoid painkillers and anti-inflammatories, and keep training in the pain-free "valley" with blood-flow work. Change joint angles and variations, strengthen the surrounding muscles, and use ice if needed.
- Prevent overuse by rotating variations, grips and stances. Pull more than you press, favour posterior chain and vertical pressing over frequent heavy flat bench, and keep low-cost prehab (strength restoration, face pulls, band work, dead hangs) inside the session.
- Pain is a messenger, not a measure of damage. Learn to tell muscle pain from tendon/joint pain, don't feed nocebo fear, and rebuild slowly and conservatively after an injury.

- **Never train through joint/tendon pain; pain is a warning, and pushing through turns minor issues into lifelong ones** — P; ~14 videos, 2020 → 2025; confidence: high; joint pain never goes away by itself, it piles up. ### Tendinitis: diagnosis and treatment
- **Tendinitis is the most common lifting injury: a spectrum from occasional pain to pain at rest, ending in rupture if ignored** — P/D; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; severe chronic tendinitis is a deep all-day pain. Ruptures (biceps, patellar, hip labrum) are usually preceded by months or years of ignored tendinitis {{9c7O0jjeR5Q@126}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@64}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1398}} {{yoROLGGUPXU@2074}} {{yoROLGGUPXU@0}}
- **Rest does not cure tendinitis; it only seems to, then returns because the movement cause is unchanged** — P/O; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "being told to rest is the worst tendinitis advice". - **Take at most 1-2 days off when pain is bad, mainly to get over the emotional hump, then return** — R; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: high; longer builds a fear association {{vHf2ZoHWHQs@312}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@583}} {{9kIIW251uiI@568}}
- **Avoid painkillers and anti-inflammatories: they mask the signal you need, and (in his view) anti-inflammatories weaken tendons and block adaptation** — O/P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "not medical advice". He gives a hip-labrum tear story about a powerlifter on painkillers. If you are on them anyway, avoid max singles and stay high-rep {{vHf2ZoHWHQs@160}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@852}} {{UgafGUt-VBc@440}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@641}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@978}}
- **Train around the pain: work in the "valley" between inactive moderate pain and acute pain, which widens the pain-free window** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; end the session before pain becomes acute. - **Tendinitis is "a game of angles": find which movements load the tendon and switch to variations that work the muscle without pain** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2025; confidence: high; reintroduce the painful movement unloaded. ## How his view changed
- **Pain handling: absolutist → graded.** 2020: "never lift through pain; if a movement hurts, stop" {{1R42snjAX4I@0}} {{IahVc0kbGOU@501}}. 2021: graded occurrence/aftermath scales, with minor pain to be pushed aside, nocebo awareness, and level-2 pain where you may push through {{Z90VsPDV__U@1528}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@2113}}. Later (2022-2025) he still treats tendon/joint pain as a stop signal {{qrBLykvU_EE@0}} {{waeSP7DBmig@370}}. The later view is graded, but the stop rule still holds for tendons.
- **Front delts and shoulder health.** 2020: front-delt isolation instead of pressing fixed his shoulders, and a strong front delt prevents injury {{KMcJAERb9Dk@1123}} {{KMcJAERb9Dk@1630}} {{KMcJAERb9Dk@1817}}. 2022: overdeveloped front delts versus weak rear/side delts invite injury; he shrank his front delts, built rear delts/traps, and the injuries stopped {{yoROLGGUPXU@710}}.
