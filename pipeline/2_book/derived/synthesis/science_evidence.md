# Science Evidence

**Bottom line**
- He is experience-based, not "science-based". Your own body's data is the most valid evidence, studies are group generalisations that are worthless until tested on yourself, and he deliberately doesn't cite studies.
- He isn't anti-science but anti-"scientism": treating single papers as fixed truth, cherry-picking studies to back a preference, reading only abstracts, and using "science-based" as authority or marketing.
- Hypertrophy is simple and ~90% has been known for a century. The main drivers are volume, intensity and frequency. Pump, burn, soreness, metabolic stress, tempo and TUT are minor or irrelevant. EMG is a poor predictor of growth.
- The ideal is a middle ground: experience pooled across many lifters ("experience-based ×10"), with science used only insofar as it improves your training.

## How his view changed
- **Stretch/eccentric versus concentric.** 2020-2021: muscle is gained mostly via weighted stretch, the negative is the key, and weighted stretching is "now backed by studies" {{6sFaB3rAY1A@1194}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@1986}} {{XweCS5RdqNk@318}}. 2023-2024: he cites a ~100-man study where ~90° training outgrew lengthened-only. He calls the stretch-focused trend contrarian and believes the concentric builds more than the eccentric, proposing a partner-removes-the-negative experiment. He notes Milo Wolf says lengthened partials help only a little {{CGhPZ9AxBJE@379}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@1141}} {{vVQmYGw7Kj8@2205}}. The later view is current.
- **Rep range and load.** 2020: some studies favour heavy weights, and heavy loads are needed for damage {{lJyrBDvtQFU@319}} {{gjZ6qGRxS10@110}}. 2025: rep range is "mostly dead" as long as you're close to failure {{ACFKXT44BzM@1694}}. This is a refinement.
