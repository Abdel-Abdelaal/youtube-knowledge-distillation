# Volume

**Bottom line**
- Volume only matters inside a relevant intensity window. Sets that don't challenge your baseline are junk volume, so "tonnage" for him always means hard-set tonnage, never raw reps.
- His later position is to do the least volume that keeps you progressing. Add sets (about 2 at a time) only when load and reps stall, cut liberally, and keep most exercises at 2-4 sets (3 for novices and intermediates, 2 for advanced; 2 per taxing lift in 2025).
- Weekly targets he gives: about 3-6 sets per muscle for novices, 6-12 for intermediates (12 as a cap), and 8-14 as the general answer. Back can go to 12-24 and even 14-28; legs get little (6 quad + 6 posterior sets/wk for himself in 2024).
- Per session: 2-4 sets per muscle, at most 2 exercises per body part. Upper days carry more exercises than lower days (6-8, up to 10, vs 3-5). Spread weekly volume across days, because frequency exists to fit volume in.
- He moved from 2020 maximalism (2-3 h sessions 6 days/wk, "more work always better", nucleus overload) to a low-volume, hard-set approach by 2024 ("closer to HIT than to 18-20 sets"). He still rejects HIT's single set and GVT/10x10 as the two extremes.

## Positions

### Core concepts: tonnage, intensity windows, baseline
- **Hypertrophy is driven by tonnage (reps x weight) accumulated over time; volume is reps x sets and counts only when the load sits in a relevant intensity window** — P; ~25 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "135 lb bench for 1000 reps won't make you big"; 10 reps at 315 beats 2 at 350; 300x5 beats 350x1 {{RaGX9rPQr4k@435}} {{UQguwgxTC9E@255}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@960}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@10631}}
- **Volume, intensity and frequency form one trinity; none works alone, and stressing just one breaks the system** — P; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high; in his view volume and intensity can't exist apart, frequency spreads them; "volume without intensity means nothing" {{lJyrBDvtQFU@62}} {{V4aBuYpdY34@55}} {{lmhW2W2UQto@1514}} {{1apseBbt0-o@62}}
- **Only relevant tonnage from hard sets counts; warm-ups and very light work count as tonnage but are low quality** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; count working sets only; 4000 lb of NO is worth about 400 lb of quality work; he defends warm-ups as still mildly useful {{s8mGWwTGLOo@360}} {{dpwvdQqKxQI@308}} {{LNggSj0WBd8@1071}} {{9y2s6kyFkPc@2253}}
- **Intensity floor: stay above about 40% 1RM; within the right window, 76% vs 98% doesn't matter, tonnage does** — P; 3 videos, 2020; confidence: medium; 40% vs 75% may equal out in theory but takes far longer {{VVckf4vyYPE@940}} {{QiOh8SF-UxQ@238}} {{VVckf4vyYPE@1003}}
- **End a muscle's work when it can no longer return to a baseline relevant to the absolute intensity set by the opening strength work** — P; ~12 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; e.g. a 300 lb squatter who ends up doing a variation with 150 lb has "dug too deep"; a 135 lb load is an irreducible baseline and pointless; he prefers this in-session cue to MRV guesses {{4uTKFMZVgSs@230}} {{UprNxHue9d0@187}} {{4uTKFMZVgSs@300}} {{QfHfxLYRu88@1321}}
- **Judge junk volume against absolute intensity, not relative; relative intensity keeps dropping, so it would have you train for hours** — P; 4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium; fatigue can disguise junk sets as hard ones; program from fresh 1RM percentages {{4uTKFMZVgSs@470}} {{4uTKFMZVgSs@270}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@1556}} {{ZPps_9IGdhc@400}}
- **Muscular endurance is the real cap on sets and sets your maximum recoverable volume; set multiplication means being able to return to baseline and repeat** — P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium; bigger muscles have more endurance, so adapt set counts per body part {{82CguFy4piw@314}} {{WyxeMHovPJ8@330}} {{LYBdXCcmOO0@1130}} {{82CguFy4piw@125}}

### Junk volume
- **Junk volume is work outside a relevant intensity window: unchallenging loads, pump chasing, sets ended by cardio or pump rather than muscular failure** — P; ~20 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; typical case: a 30-rep weight stopped at ~20 by pump; BFR/pump drop sets; 25-rep machine sets {{T0QddSpwwdc@380}} {{-banYSTnkq4@1330}} {{gjZ6qGRxS10@330}} {{O5xVdqgt8Uo@1507}}
- **Junk volume is relative to your muscular endurance and is mainly bad when it replaces hard sets or takes time from other muscles** — P/O; 4 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium; "one man's junk volume is another's treasure"; he called the concept "overblown" in 2021 {{C86TMVYGu0M@554}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@3452}} {{Zv9QAIb5nGk@1764}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1953}}
- **Calling every set after the top set junk is Mentzer logic; a second set costs about 3-4 minutes, so don't be dogmatic** — O; 2 videos, 2022; confidence: medium {{Zv9QAIb5nGk@1701}} {{Zv9QAIb5nGk@2016}}
- **Typical junk patterns: 10x10/GVT, 5x10 and 5x20 schemes, 25 chest sets, 13+ biceps sets in a session, 10x20 triceps cables, 8x10-20 light laterals** — O; ~15 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; on 25 chest sets, 60-70% of reps do nothing; on a ~300-rep push day, 65-75% is junk {{k9_dDbGLFZQ@248}} {{wLx5BRx8AaU@379}} {{gizylGr0ebo@2682}} {{uYRIWAKlA0c@816}}
- **10x10 is a band-aid: if you can repeat 10 reps for 10 sets the load is uninteresting, early gains are cardiovascular, and true GVT forces ~55-60% 1RM instead of 70-75%** — O/P; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; 3 sets in a relevant window may beat it; 2024: "10x10 is sandbagging" {{s8mGWwTGLOo@688}} {{s8mGWwTGLOo@1100}} {{l9iNYJtRrD8@124}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@767}}

### How much: maximalism vs minimalism
- **Do as little as needed for as much growth as possible: the minimal sets that keep you progressing and injury-free** — P; ~10 videos, 2021 → 2025; confidence: high; leaves room to add later; "volume only serves the purpose of progression" (2025) {{QL9Dh9CGOBQ@114}} {{BnzVYV9_IQc@253}} {{43F9unmSc_I@1945}} {{ACFKXT44BzM@751}}
- **Both extremes fail: HIT's 1-2 sets is too little, GVT/10x10 too much; naturals grow in the middle** — O; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; one set to failure "far too little for naturals"; 2024: HIT still beats GVT because it leaves room to add {{MIHPylz3f7Q@290}} {{gizylGr0ebo@2682}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@461}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@11980}}
- **Doing more always gives more, as long as you recover (2020-2022 maximalist stance)** — O; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium (later softened); one extra set won't hurt; sit at the brink of overtraining; most people undertrain; he prefers a maximized approach over ~80% of the gains for half the work {{tZ9CSu2DMIE@61}} {{tZ9CSu2DMIE@312}} {{Zv9QAIb5nGk@188}} {{z2uzpl_smSg@26}}
- **Minimalism is a tool, not an ideology; it fits strength, athletes, beginners and busy people, but builds unbalanced physiques for bodybuilders** — P/O; ~8 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: medium; the maximalist's last ~20% decides things at an advanced level; cuts hurt maximalists more in absolute tonnage (50,000 → 25,000 vs 10,000 → 5,000) {{w4-2c3iWvDQ@2605}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@376}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@502}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@1280}}
- **By 2024 he describes himself as low on the volume spectrum, closer to HIT than to 18-20 sets per muscle; cutting volume often improves performance and overload** — A/O; 3 videos, 2023 → 2024; confidence: medium; 2023: prefers fewer quality sets made possible by high frequency {{okm6bZHKHI4@5998}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@9295}} {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1006}}
- **Under- vs over-doing: constantly too little gives plateaus, too much gives injury or regression; when guessing, err slightly high** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium; "too much is worse than too little" (2020); 2022: too many sets costs at worst some junk volume and recovery, so err high, but add conservatively {{B4aHn6pXfTo@63}} {{MIHPylz3f7Q@568}} {{iDua_rNsBO8@695}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@6290}}

### Maximum recoverable volume (MRV)
- **MRV is the most work you can recover and grow from; the target is to train between medium and maximum recoverable volume** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium; below minimum you regress, around medium you progress or stall, above maximum it's useless or risks injury; MRV is individual and found by experiment {{tZ9CSu2DMIE@0}} {{B4aHn6pXfTo@0}} {{p-kR3RfGBHw@285}} {{p-kR3RfGBHw@317}}
- **MRV can only be diagnosed after the fact; most people don't overshoot; ~25% above minimum without passing the max is fine** — O; 2 videos, 2021; confidence: medium {{UprNxHue9d0@375}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@3301}}
- **MRV rises with training age and age; it keeps changing, so keep adapting** — P; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: low {{tZ9CSu2DMIE@375}} {{ADLnarIVojI@315}}
- **Office workers can aim at MRV; manual laborers should stay lower (e.g. 2 heavy sets of a compound instead of 4-8)** — P; 2 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: medium; athletes must count volume from their sport {{kCVudEfVU0c@313}} {{lyj1BFZ85Dg@5118}} {{kCVudEfVU0c@400}}
- **Signs of too much: soreness lingers or you can't repeat performance session to session; at 15-20 weekly sets with slow strength gains, try less volume, more intensity** — P; 1 video, 2022; confidence: medium {{QfHfxLYRu88@1384}} {{QfHfxLYRu88@1822}}

### Adding and cutting volume
- **Add volume only when progress (load, reps, tonnage) stalls, and in small steps of about 2 sets per body part (2-4 max) per adjustment** — R; ~6 videos, 2021 → 2025; confidence: high; past a point, progress should come from intensity/complexity rather than more sets {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@760}} {{16fjHEtZTCA@1336}} {{7uBT8vp0lNw@1576}} {{7n8RMhsxEB4@818}}
- **Never jump volume: spikes cause no results, sickness or overuse injury, and people then wrongly blame volume** — P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; can't jump from 9 to 16 sets; don't double volume on becoming intermediate {{Z1xF6eFUdBQ@63}} {{p6WcFTOBq3s@40}} {{16fjHEtZTCA@1336}} {{lyj1BFZ85Dg@4866}}
