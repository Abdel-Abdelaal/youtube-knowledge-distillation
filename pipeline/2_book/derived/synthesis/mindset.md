# Mindset

**Bottom line**
- Discipline carries a natural lifter. Motivation is not enough: it spikes and fades. Make training a scheduled, non-negotiable habit that you never debate with yourself. Discipline and willpower are skills you train, not genetic traits.
- Adherence matters most. Choose training and lifts you enjoy, because consistency over years beats intensity, optimisation and talent. Set no deadlines. Lifting is for life and has no finish line.
- Aim for an ideal so high you will never reach it, and choose it yourself. Don't compare yourself to others or idolise anyone, NH included. Learn to program and to think for yourself.
- In his view, "bad genetics", "natural limits" and the intermediate plateau are mostly mental or intellectual problems. Take full responsibility and reject the blackpill.
- Lift calm and to a plan, not angry or hyped, and treat failure as a teacher. His 2024 videos add balance: don't make the gym your whole life, rigid discipline becomes a straitjacket, and self-love works better than self-hatred as fuel.

## How his view changed
- **Place of the gym in life.** 2020-2022: serious lifting means 8-10 h/week that replaces hobbies and social time {{kh--k3genZA@755}}. Training should be so central that you "would sooner lose your nails" than stop {{YHm-qSHgGSk@946}}. Strip other hobbies so lifting is your one hobby {{3W-yE2pgTec@1135}}, and hobbyists stay mediocre, so make it a lifestyle {{ZPBwBrOktgA@1393}}. 2024 (later view): ~5 h/week with no lifestyle change; don't centre your life on the gym {{bRKQIOYo2u4@843}}. Gymcelling is a net negative {{lGbT0aT2hOk@270}}, and the gym won't fix your life by itself {{wBct3Pl8OY4@6136}}.
- **Rigidity.** 2020-2022: training is rigid, done as planned regardless of feeling, and taking a week off for low motivation is dangerous {{PuIp9cGKMHo@602}}. Grad-school paper lists where an undone item counted as failure {{lyj1BFZ85Dg@907}}. 2024 (later): discipline taken too far is a straitjacket {{lGbT0aT2hOk@825}}, planning every day makes you hyper-rigid {{fPU3nRIH8sM@7873}}, he keeps no rigid time schedule {{wBct3Pl8OY4@2857}}, and willpower fluctuates {{qYaWxvA4fKg@3531}}.
- **Harshness vs positive reinforcement.** 2020-2022: praise breeds stagnation and blame drives growth {{WdFEEkLfBRc@2776}}. Lazy people get the mediocre gains they deserve {{1ZgJ8kMFd9s@125}}. He felt pity, not sympathy, for blackpillers {{eeK8e2KIvwY@64}}. 2024 (later): his harsh inner voice turns most people off, and empathy and positive reinforcement work for the majority {{qYaWxvA4fKg@2207}}. Pure willpower ideology "creates one hero and breaks nine" {{qYaWxvA4fKg@2771}}. Being average and a happy dad bod are fine {{loDNjTHZjG4@3404}} {{qYaWxvA4fKg@3722}}.
- **Solitude vs community.** 2020-2022: personal development is lonely and company breeds comfort {{ZdcKQXMmmPY@313}} {{U_4FtEW5nh0@66}}. Destroying a weak identity requires isolation {{JNBgBfmTqx8@566}}. 2023-2024 (later): a man's biggest breakthroughs come among other men and the lone-wolf myth is false {{sgQD0WIfuvc@4057}}. Monk mode makes most people worse, and his own instinct to isolate never helped him {{lyj1BFZ85Dg@1469}}. Seek community {{o40xCjIzl14@6064}}.
- **Fuel: dysmorphia and self-hatred vs self-love.** 2020: lifting motivation may be an expression of dysmorphia, and you must collaborate with that drive {{MuQvx5dNpno@89}} {{MuQvx5dNpno@230}}. 2024 (later): self-hatred as fuel works at first then destroys you; love and acceptance kept him going {{qYaWxvA4fKg@1644}}.

## Misfiled but useful
- Very high reps (40-50) are boring for most lifts; he uses them only for neck and calves {{VVckf4vyYPE@1066}}. Calves are only boring if you don't push them to a burning pump {{6KA80BQ9W3k@943}}.
- 5x5 heavy deadlifts aren't hard training because tonnage is low {{Cn38kgCdOhw@2449}}. Tonnage is fully under your control {{Z1xF6eFUdBQ@379}} and builds slowly with patience {{V4aBuYpdY34@230}}.
- A 1RM is 1 second of stress; an 8-rep squat set is mentally harder, and staying in low reps is a comfort zone {{CTMwuqNsY9Q@247}}. Jumping from 20 to 40 reps in one session reflects mental fortitude, not doubled strength {{uTHCzF7Exck@384}}.
- Staying near ~85% keeps you in "flow" between anxiety (overtraining) and boredom (undertraining) {{z2uzpl_smSg@2587}}. A set range gives freedom on low-motivation weeks while enforcing a minimum {{iDua_rNsBO8@1506}}.
- Most lifters undertrain rather than overtrain {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1252}}. Advanced naturals burn out mentally or get bored before overtraining {{PLgCL_rizPU@63}}. Mental stamina limits upper days, physical stamina limits lower days {{cGzlMRCxvxo@1804}}.
- Some lifters are "diesel engines" who need long sessions; he can train 4 h without noticing {{D3mJh0ZA5nc@212}}. He needs variety, long sessions and lots of tonnage {{RaGX9rPQr4k@1989}}.
- Training matters more than diet for size {{XASWAdsSu0M@1207}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@1451}} {{43F9unmSc_I@2200}}. Eat things you like daily instead of cheat meals {{2sIS1fZYqrc@191}}. Dirty bulking is the lazy path {{a02c10s3Pjk@568}}.
- Ramadan or Lent: keep "cruise control" training momentum {{YKCNJS5KNvw@1820}}. Gym closure: aim to mitigate loss, not build mass {{kY88aqcwRqI@14}}.
- On a full-body program, sneaking in a one-day body-part session when you don't feel like the plan helps free spirits stay consistent {{wJTax5ziJUY@125}}. People who plan abs or calves at home rarely do them {{CFlRdyLiWOo@874}}.
- Obsessing over a weak point with excessive frequency creates frustration and keeps it weak {{yRRXCYcgzJs@317}}. When obsessed with a muscle group, give in, because the best gains come where the passion is {{43F9unmSc_I@124}}.
- Never abandon the basics that gave most of your gains {{ABTeMrXdgI4@692}}. A methodology should structure, not limit, so use free weights, machines and cables {{ACFKXT44BzM@1506}}.
- The fear of leaving gains on the table drives unnecessary protein and supplement stacking {{S_EF3ebs5Jg@2970}}. Buying a supplement is "action faking" {{L_aq5x4lom8@3443}} {{IzUEOsA4nIw@2390}}.
- Patience prevents injury ("the train slows on curves") {{qrBLykvU_EE@1318}}. Impatience forces patience later through time off {{-T8qWCI4_No@1169}}. The lifter who grinds every rep often drops out injured {{QiOh8SF-UxQ@427}}.
