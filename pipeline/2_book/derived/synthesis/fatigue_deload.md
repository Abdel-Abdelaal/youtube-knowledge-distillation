# Fatigue & Deload

**Bottom line**
- He rejects scheduled deload weeks and weeks off for bodybuilders (2020 → 2024): deloads are a powerlifting tool for peaking, and bodybuilders never peak. Needing one means the program overshoots recoverable volume.
- His alternative is a "bodybuilding deload" built into every week: evolving reps/sets, rotations of lifts and muscles, small daily trims. Tonnage stays stable and every muscle keeps being stimulated.
- Fatigue is not the enemy. Stimulus and fatigue are the same thing, so aim for the most fatigue you can recover from. He ranks SFR low (B tier 2023, 5th in 2024) and CNS fatigue lower still (D tier 2023).
- At the session level, stop when performance or the mind drops out (junk volume) and leave the gym with energy left (~20% in the tank, ~40% on arm days).
- Lower-back and axial fatigue from squats and deadlifts is the main thing to manage. Never do heavy squat and heavy deadlift on the same day, and he finds squats the more taxing of the two.

## Positions

### Against deload weeks and time off
- **Scheduled deloads are unnecessary or harmful for bodybuilders; a program that needs one is badly programmed** — O/P; ~16 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; deloads mean you are "digging a hole" (too much frequency, volume or strength work); "emergency response to a programming mistake" or, if scheduled, hurts gains; useless unless injured {{7ZS-mGePFXw@312}} {{CRXE2gFfwcs@188}} {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@1133}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@691}}
- **Deloads exist for peaking (strength athletes); bodybuilders never peak, so only the recovery half could matter** — P; ~9 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; the term spread ~20 years ago with the internet and powerlifting, and is absent from bronze/silver/golden era bodybuilding; current YouTube deload fashion is powerlifting-centric {{7ZS-mGePFXw@62}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@255}} {{GNJkAcgUJ_0@5585}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@691}}
- **Trim the program yourself rather than be forced into a deload: cutting 40-50 min from your week beats a deload** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; tonnage lost in a deload exceeds voluntary trimming; doing slightly less means you never need a week off and get ~25% more work across two weeks (2024); reduce workload *before* the deload would be needed {{7ZS-mGePFXw@350}} {{7ZS-mGePFXw@374}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@444}} {{wBct3Pl8OY4@10055}}
- **A week off (or 3-4 days fully off) is detraining, not deloading** — O/P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; "catastrophic"; time off only for injury or when away from the gym; needing 3-4 days off every 2 months means intensity/volume is wrong; a 13-day "strategic deconditioning" break is planned detraining; you can only deload from a load {{7ZS-mGePFXw@0}} {{CRXE2gFfwcs@126}} {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@1071}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@4399}}
- **Time off costs performance and work capacity, not muscle** — P; ~5 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: high; size regresses slowly, neural adaptations detrain fast; example: three-plate squat (315 lb) drops to ~255 lb after 2 weeks off; a 10% loss multiplied across 6+ movement patterns is huge; "you shrink during a deload" is an exaggeration {{PuIp9cGKMHo@851}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@788}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@3335}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@567}}
- **Deload fatigue clears in ~3 days; the rest of the week nothing grows** — P; ~3 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium; a week off every 6-12 weeks adds up to a lot of lost work over years; on a 3-day schedule one missed day = ~30% of the week {{PuIp9cGKMHo@2143}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@567}}
- **Coming back stronger after rest proves nothing, or proves you were under-recovered** — P/O; ~6 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: high; 5 days off then stronger means the program was bad; post-rest PRs are expected and only show full performance; bodybuilding is about maintaining performance, not PRs; strength may even rise after a break while volume capacity falls {{8nUneZclRp0@436}} {{z2uzpl_smSg@2525}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@2080}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@754}}
- **Deload weeks backfire behaviourally** — P/O; ~3 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium; knowing one is coming makes people overzealous beforehand; classic mesocycles pile low-intensity volume far from failure, then overshoot into junk volume; deloads are a cop-out that stops lifters learning day-to-day fatigue management {{BJXFeQWGUuY@507}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@2080}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@567}}
- **Deloads produce confirmation bias** — O; ~2 videos, 2022; confidence: medium; lifters credit the deload for preventing CNS fatigue that never came; he compares it to sacrificing a chicken nightly to keep away a wolf that never existed {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1127}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@1955}}
- **Mesocycles, low-volume phases, resensitisation breaks and "mTOR resets" are unnecessary; a good program runs indefinitely** — O; ~4 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium; muscle is never desensitised; a plateaued muscle needs more work, not time off {{P89o8FBSWDI@1871}} {{JnWWva91QUM@63}} {{yUecX6qONXg@400}}
- **Tolerant caveat: if deloads help you, keep them** — P/O; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; some top naturals grow with them but "can't justify it"; if deloads feel wasted, skip them but manage fatigue another way {{7ZS-mGePFXw@500}} {{yUecX6qONXg@379}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@691}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@754}}
- **If you must deload, the better form is your usual lifts at low intensity and low volume; 2 sets at 2 RIR is reasonable** — P; ~2 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: low; keeps blood flow and movement patterns; contrasts with his 2024 view that even an active deload far from failure is wasted for hypertrophy {{CRXE2gFfwcs@64}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@6800}}

### His built-in "bodybuilding deload" (autoregulation)
- **The deload is built into every session and week: evolving reps/sets and rotations vary volume, intensity and frequency slightly while tonnage stays stable** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; "I never deload" means not the powerlifter way, since he deloads "constantly" through set/rep management; small dips from rep ranges are the only variation he allows; he opposes blast/cruise, polarized volume/intensity days and clear hard/easy weeks {{-banYSTnkq4@1210}} {{MIHPylz3f7Q@697}} {{A55c7v1yMn4@937}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@3712}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@6605}}
- **Micro-deloads target muscles on the day: picture each body part flashing red (needs work) or green (needs recovery)** — P; ~3 videos, 2022; confidence: medium; reduce a muscle that needs rest to one evolving-rep set or rotate it out; train fresh biceps even if a rotation planned rest (non-arbitrary); keeps each muscle continuously stimulated {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1766}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1828}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1890}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@951}}
- **Volume, intensity and frequency are one thing: tools to manage fatigue** — P; ~2 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium; any program is a block of fatigue, so get the most from the least {{o05wgvQtSMc@379}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@2203}}
- **Autoregulation needs mechanisms built into the program (a menu of rotation options) and suits advanced lifters; beginners should work towards it** — P; ~3 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium; small recovery shortfalls can accumulate into under-recovery, so the method is not for novices {{7n8RMhsxEB4@689}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1828}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@1768}}
- **Training must dictate recovery and the deload, never the reverse** — P; ~3 videos, 2022; confidence: medium; set optimal work first, then see what recovery it needs (not a fixed 5+1 week block); bodybuilders let training dictate recovery, strength athletes the opposite {{BJXFeQWGUuY@191}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@255}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1937}}
- **Pre-plan easier swaps for bad days** — R; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium; back extensions or light RDLs for heavy deadlifts; on bad deadlift days pick the shrug version and skip rack pulls; on hard weeks skip secondary movements, though keeping the secondary preserves tonnage better than keeping strength work; take 1 set off the secondary movement on the week's 2nd upper day {{mXLMkXmIeW8@568}} {{cGzlMRCxvxo@2175}} {{MIHPylz3f7Q@887}} {{MIHPylz3f7Q@824}}
- **Use an 8-day program over 6-day weeks so heavy back squats or conventional deadlifts drop out every few weeks** — A/P; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low; rests body and mind, and the next exposure gives PRs {{cHYOYTgiRlM@250}}
- **Partial recovery is fine: 90-100% recovered is enough to train again** — P; ~3 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium; a deload gives absolute, not optimal, recovery; a muscle can only be restored to 100, never beyond, so emptying it fully first gains nothing; no extra recovery from something already recovered {{p-kR3RfGBHw@470}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@699}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@1706}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@4274}}

### Fatigue = stimulus
- **There is no stimulus without fatigue; stimulus and fatigue are the same thing seen two ways** — P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2025; confidence: high; if you're not fatigued you didn't push enough; target "the maximum fatigue you can recover from"; calling fatigue "the bad stuff" demonizes it {{boPKiKE26Rw@507}} {{sFMQj4Lflk0@944}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@5473}} {{ACFKXT44BzM@876}}
- **Accumulate fatigue rather than flush it** — P; ~2 videos, 2022; confidence: medium; fatigue decays fast and fitness slowly, so a pure flush risks fitness; his formula: performance − fatigue = fitness, and performance exists to let you accumulate fatigue {{PuIp9cGKMHo@5599}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@5913}}
- **Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio is overrated (B tier 2023, ranked 5th in 2024)** — O; ~4 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: high; it is an excuse to avoid heavy free-weight compounds, and machine/high-rep-only lifters avoid stimulus too; compounds give more of both and are only worse if excess fatigue outruns excess stimulus {{nmgidpn3DNM@571}} {{nmgidpn3DNM@634}} {{SxqjF2oPasg@943}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@1203}}
- **The deadlift's "bad SFR" is a programming or form problem** — O/P; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium; it easily overshoots recovery, so undershoot slightly ("fill the glass without overflowing"); too much volume or poor form makes it fatiguing {{3IMgRqcRN4Y@1316}} {{lG5SZbRdOjA@627}} {{nmgidpn3DNM@948}}
- **SFR does matter where fatigue is low-value** — P; ~6 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium; machines rest tendons/stabilizers (illustration: bench 100 fatigue points, 60 to muscle; chest machine 50, 45 to muscle); squats/deadlifts for abs is terrible SFR; heavy deadlift singles aren't optimal (keep to ~1 rep at RPE 9); chasing full ROM adds needless fatigue; matters most once many small muscles are isolated {{O5xVdqgt8Uo@806}} {{-izcpmcGs-c@382}} {{mMA3KF3qmlQ@1008}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@1266}}
- **Fatigue ≈ reps × weight, modulated by the lift** — P; 1 video, 2025; confidence: low; 10-15 reps at 50 lb is a lot for laterals and nothing for good mornings; bigger muscle groups added/removed matter more (3 sets of curls adds no real fatigue, 3 sets of deadlifts does) {{o05wgvQtSMc@566}} {{ACFKXT44BzM@627}}
- **Specify the type of fatigue: muscular, cardiovascular, mechanical, tendon, CNS, mental** — P; ~3 videos, 2020; confidence: medium; muscular and cardiovascular fatigue must not be confused, and many "fatigue-seeking" methods really target cardio; muscular fatigue = lost fiber force, rebuilt bigger with food and rest {{boPKiKE26Rw@62}} {{ADLnarIVojI@62}} {{ADLnarIVojI@0}}

### CNS fatigue
- **CNS fatigue barely matters for bodybuilders; most people can't reach CNS burnout (D tier 2023)** — O/P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high; 1.5 years of heavy squats/deadlifts with no burnout; supposed burnout is usually injury or mismanaged volume/intensity; you don't need a rest day for the CNS {{ZScs2L9rero@2140}} {{ZScs2L9rero@2210}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@437}} {{SxqjF2oPasg@2015}}
- **If your muscles can move a load, it can't fry your CNS; strength loss blamed on CNS is usually deficit, sleep, stress or a bad day** — P; 1 video, 2022; confidence: medium {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1003}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@940}}
- **The CNS is trainable; undertraining it out of fear limits gains and future work capacity** — P; ~2 videos, 2022; confidence: medium; bodybuilders should grow the CNS to serve the muscles, not plan around the big CNS bucket (that neglects small muscles); a slightly under-recovered CNS costs little because you don't test 1RMs {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1438}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@312}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@2000}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@2455}}

### Session-level fatigue: when to stop
- **Leave the gym with energy left; being wrecked is not a goal** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; ~20% in the tank with high frequency, ~40% after an arm day; arm day should leave you energized, not tired; being wiped every session means working dumb {{gVVj9QuCyFM@568}} {{wB-ydKSfQ5k@1769}} {{bJ7gfILR6RA@1190}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@10491}}
- **End the session when fatigue pushes sets out of the useful window (junk volume)** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; baseline idea: start at 100%, dig in each set; if under 50% of max feels hard, it's junk; if 400 lb squatter can barely do 180 lb ×3 late, you dug too deep; if the last 30-40 min of a 90-min session are drained, shorten it and raise intensity {{pUpQypTV3Ck@312}} {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@633}} {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@1635}} {{bJ7gfILR6RA@441}}
- **A good session is repeatable within ~5 days and for years** — P; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: medium; giving everything one day then being out seven loses to daily managed training; holding back to survive tomorrow is a scheduling problem {{MjeRI-baRF8@252}} {{bJ7gfILR6RA@1379}} {{DA7QvIAyptw@254}}
- **Lost reps at the same weight are the early warning** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; 300 lb ×4 then ×3 means do less volume that day; 7 instead of 8-9 is a milder under-recovery sign; for bodybuilders bar weight should never go backward, only volume dips temporarily; heavy lifts give an accurate read of recovery {{zCo31HKOsIY@127}} {{zCo31HKOsIY@190}} {{z2uzpl_smSg@2463}} {{z2uzpl_smSg@2400}}
- **Fatigue carries across exercises, even unrelated muscles; put the least important lifts last** — P; ~3 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: medium; by exercise 5 you underperform; on full body vary order since skipping the last quarter always undertrains a quarter of the body; over-focusing on compounds leaves arms with no direct volume {{G7rVTseabFU@400}} {{G7rVTseabFU@1400}} {{G7rVTseabFU@1430}} {{GyK1Y6WTR-g@887}}

- **Cardio fitness limits sessions; supersets with a lower-body compound wreck it** — P/O; ~3 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: medium; whole-workout giant sets sabotage performance; to stop fading before failure, build work capacity and eat before training {{G7rVTseabFU@400}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@5952}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@13610}}

## How his view changed
- **Wording on "never deload" refined.** In 2020 he said he never deloads ({{CRXE2gFfwcs@188}}). By 2022 he clarified that he deloads constantly through set/rep management, just not the powerlifter way ({{PuIp9cGKMHo@3712}}). Later view: deloading is built in.
- **Form of an acceptable deload.** In 2020 the "objectively better deload" was usual lifts at low intensity and volume ({{CRXE2gFfwcs@64}}). In 2024 he said even an active deload far from failure is wasted for hypertrophy ({{ZRNSc-xBVnc@691}}), yet in a separate 2024 video he called 2 sets at 2 RIR reasonable ({{6p2x4kDDjzs@6800}}). Later stance: harder against it, with that one exception.
- **SFR downgraded.** In 2022 he treated it as a simple valid concept ({{nmgidpn3DNM@571}}). He ranked it B tier in 2023 ({{SxqjF2oPasg@943}}) and 5th, "an excuse", in 2024 ({{6yW3GSskl8g@1203}}).
- **His own lower-body recovery.** In 2022 he trained legs every two days ({{o_x3Ij2GBXM@440}}). In 2025 he said he needs ~72 h because recovery lags as strength rises ({{ACFKXT44BzM@688}}). Later view: 72 h.

## Contradictions
- **Is feel a valid gauge?** In 2020 he said how you feel during and the day after a workout means nothing for judging volume ({{MIHPylz3f7Q@378}}). The same year he said to judge extra tonnage by sensations and soreness 4 days later ({{Z1xF6eFUdBQ@254}}) and to "let your body be the judge" for CNS ({{ZScs2L9rero@2100}}).
- **Is overtraining real?** In 2020 he called it real ({{PLgCL_rizPU@0}}) and also "a myth" ({{6sFaB3rAY1A@3206}}). He also said most people needn't worry about muscular fatigue ({{boPKiKE26Rw@127}}) but that most who think they overtrain are right ({{9M7QVlvFdQY@125}}).
- **Day-to-day interference.** In 2022 he said deadlift Monday then barbell rows Tuesday won't work ({{QfHfxLYRu88@690}}). In 2024 he said lats to failure then RDLs the next day doesn't hurt him, and doubted that one day's training hurts the next (garbled; {{okm6bZHKHI4@7431}}).
- Script flagged none. Squats vs deadlifts being "more taxing" is consistent across 2020-2022, so it is not a contradiction.
