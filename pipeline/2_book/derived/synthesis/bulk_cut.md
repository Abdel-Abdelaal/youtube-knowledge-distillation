# Bulk / Cut / Recomp

**Bottom line**
- Default for a natural who is not at an extreme: a very slow lean bulk on a small surplus (+200-300 kcal; 500 is pointless), aiming for roughly 0.5-1.5 lb/month and judged by gym progression, waist, mirror and calisthenics, not by the scale.
- The surplus only fuels what training creates. If lifts and volume are not rising, more calories just become fat. If you are progressing without a surplus, don't add calories.
- Cut only if you start fat (lift through it) or you're competing. Skinny-fat people recomp at maintenance and never cut. Skinny people bulk. Don't cut in your first ~5 years unless fat.
- Recomp (maintenance, trading fat for muscle) is slow and real but needs fat to trade. He ran one for 3 years (2019-2022) and then switched to slow bulking. Since 2024 he bulks in winter/spring and maintains or recomps in summer.
- He calls dirty bulks, GOMAD, crash/"suicide" cuts and yo-yo bulk/cut cycles his own biggest mistake (210→240 lb, mostly fat). Year-round shredded leanness is also a trap for naturals. His preferred range is ~12-18% body fat.

## Positions

### Surplus size
- **Keep the surplus small: he cited +200-400 kcal (2020), under 10% and "4% beats 40%" (2021), his own +300 (2022), 200 kcal "enough" (2024-09), 300 "plenty" and 500 "pointless" (2024-12)** — R/P; ~9 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; the body can only turn so much into muscle, and the rest is stored as fat {{vC9cKh9tFxs@1253}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@818}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@8320}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@8625}}
- **No muscle benefit from a surplus above ~10-15% (2020). A moderate surplus (~500 kcal) gets ~95-98% of possible gains, and 1000-2000 kcal does not double muscle (2022)** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium; also XASWAdsSu0M@2467 "well under 10%" {{VAblRcVMjqw@248}} {{XASWAdsSu0M@2467}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@318}}
- **Novices: maintenance calories, raised slowly as appetite grows; +500 kcal or more is a waste** — R; 1 video, 2021; confidence: medium {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1670}}
- **He bulks on the bare minimum surplus that still moves the scale; for him an extra bowl of rice is enough because his metabolism is slow** — A; ~4 videos, 2022 → 2024; he admits his instinctive method may not suit hardgainers {{uYRIWAKlA0c@127}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@943}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@3039}}
- **Exception: very skinny, fast-metabolism lifters who stall on a small surplus should eat more aggressively, perhaps double** — P; 1 video, 2022; confidence: medium {{h7D6CB4p1oU@2212}}
- **Raise calories slowly and hold them steady until the bulking intake becomes your new maintenance (proof you gained muscle), then raise again. Constant swings in intake disrupt training** — P; 2 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium {{U2WB_cYv2po@567}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@694}}
- **Small daily surpluses beat occasional huge days: a 3,000 kcal surplus day is mostly stored as fat; force-feeding +1000 kcal/day makes you lethargic and diet-fixated** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{6nKKGsCCqxo@695}} {{2sIS1fZYqrc@128}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@445}}
- **A big surplus doesn't build more natural muscle, only fat. A big deficit burns muscle as well as fat. Protein synthesis is limited, but your capacity to get fat is not** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high {{NpIo8tFRESg@700}} {{U2WB_cYv2po@186}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@322}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@941}}

### Rate of gain
- **Bulk slowly: under 4 lb/month (2020-08), 1-2 lb/month (2020-12), 0.5-1 lb/month or ~10 lb/year (2023-07), ~1 lb/month (2023-10), and 1.5 lb/month is "good" on a lean bulk (2024-11)** — R; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{mUAHGlseeyk@566}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@865}} {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1317}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@1387}} {{fPU3nRIH8sM@9349}}
- **The more advanced you are, the slower the bulk. He gained 5 lb in a year from 210 (<0.5 lb/month). An advanced lifter gaining 2 lb/month is just gaining fat. 10 lb of lean mass in 2-3 months at 200 lb is impossible** — P; 3 videos, 2023 → 2024; confidence: high {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1193}} {{43F9unmSc_I@2136}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@9103}}
- **Judge monthly gain as a percentage of bodyweight; at 180 lb, several lb/month is too much to be muscle for an intermediate. Fast gain is justified only for true beginners filling out** — P; 1 video, 2023; confidence: medium {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@248}}
- **Spread gains over years: gain your mass over 5 years rather than 8-10 months (2020). 20 lb over 3 years beats 20 lb in 1 year (2023). 14 kg in 4 years is good (2024). 50 lb in 3 years is too fast unless dangerously skinny (~20 lb of it was avoidable fat)** — P; 4 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{a02c10s3Pjk@443}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@1569}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@12773}} {{yZC3Nsmmzb8@8627}}
- **Never gain 20 lb "without fat" fast, even if skinny. Anyone claiming 20 lb of muscle with no fat is lying. No one bulks 100 lb and gets 90% muscle** — P; 3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{VAblRcVMjqw@0}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@598}} {{GsRa8YsVo00@126}}
- **10 lb in two months vs five-six months may give the same bench increase, but the slower gain is far more likely muscle. An inch on the arms in three months is fat** — P; 2 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium {{-T8qWCI4_No@1012}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@953}}
- **Each bulk after the first should be more conservative ("first bulk is like a first love"); lifters do the opposite** — P; 1 video, 2024; confidence: low {{ZLheQPgEfoA@8562}}
- **Teens have time: they can slow bulk for up to 10 years. Forcing +25 lb for a sport season at 18 backfires. Raising a teen from 3,500 to 4,300 kcal (+22%) for 1 kg/month, or +60% for 3 kg/month, is miscalculated, mostly fat, and fosters eating disorders** — P/O; 3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{TgPYObguEM0@187}} {{9M7QVlvFdQY@628}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@2800}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@3135}}

### Training drives the bulk
- **Calories can't become muscle by themselves. Training creates growth, and the surplus only fuels recovery. Extra calories must coincide with rising volume, workload and intensity, or they are stored as fat ("training dictates diet")** — P; ~9 videos, 2021 → 2023; confidence: high {{9mnLRQsrs9k@2836}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@126}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@1507}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@880}}
- **A bulk should raise work capacity and volume. If volume regresses on a bulk, why bulk? Eat to fuel progression, not to gain weight** — P; ~4 videos, 2022 → 2023; confidence: high {{yUecX6qONXg@252}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@628}} {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1193}}
- **If you are progressing without a surplus, don't add calories. Raising his surplus while still progressing was his "first mistake" in 2023** — P/A; 2 videos, 2023 → 2024; confidence: medium {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@186}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@8257}}
- **Bulking is not required for novices or intermediates to gain; it only makes progression easier. The main advantage of a surplus is faster visible gains, which help adherence** — P; 2 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium {{9WA6eQODjHQ@377}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@3330}}
- **This energy-demanding program (Doom Slayer) should be run on a bulk** — R; 1 video, 2023; confidence: low {{DA7QvIAyptw@629}}
- **Run a demanding strength program at maintenance vs eating everything and leverage and recovery differ "night and day". Many program results reflect noob gains and food, not the program** — O; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: low {{N8Dp4inzHog@506}} {{N8Dp4inzHog@441}}

### Bulk checks (is it muscle or fat?)
- **Calisthenics check: if bench rises while push-ups regress, or rows rise while pull-ups drop, you're gaining fat. Slow down or stop the bulk** — P; 3 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: high {{HrUPp573ki0@1684}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@565}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@502}}
- **Strength rising only in proportion to bodyweight (e.g. +20 lb bench for +10 lb bodyweight) is a bad trade and mostly not muscle. Scale weight and bar weight going up ≠ muscle ("mass moves mass", better leverage)** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high {{TgPYObguEM0@63}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@2870}} {{RryibwmuOWE@2006}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@312}}
- **Watch the waist: rising lifts and bodyweight with a growing waist means fat, a trap for bodybuilders using powerlifting principles. A rapidly growing waist means visceral fat; every inch there looks bad** — P; ~4 videos, 2021 → 2023; confidence: high {{f-XiVNy25tw@819}} {{Ug_FoOIpwxY@505}} {{pvpf7Yuayco@1080}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@1195}}
- **Check the mirror as often as the scale; "bulking hypnosis" or size goggles hide fat gain. On a cut, "lean goggles" hide size loss** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: medium {{JWwA9H6xUC0@318}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1015}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@376}}
- **Stop or slow the bulk when: bloat appears with no visible muscle (go to maintenance or a small deficit); extra calories no longer improve gym performance; your face gets chubby; you no longer like your body ("sign #5")** — P; 4 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium {{7Sp526pPxEU@503}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1770}} {{Q5ECkNZYZ58@317}} {{wBct3Pl8OY4@3367}}
- **Don't set a target bodyweight; it becomes an obsession and forces overeating. Set a time frame instead ("sign #4": he once aimed 180→225 to hit 100 kg)** — P; 3 videos, 2022 → 2023; confidence: medium; see Contradictions {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1708}} {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1380}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@691}}
- **Weigh only every 2-4 weeks (2023); earlier he weighed every month or two, or not at all, and judged by strength and mirror** — A/P; 3 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: medium {{Xx7A1dAAPDo@693}} {{qmpvEERwXVI@187}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@880}}
- **Calorie tracking: he has never tracked on a bulk because it feeds his obsessive tendencies. Tracking is still the easiest way to know you are gaining, suits people who need regimentation, may be needed to get shredded, and he would count if he cut** — O; 4 videos, 2023 → 2024; confidence: medium {{TxDUQPe3B0E@818}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@5545}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@3167}} {{yZC3Nsmmzb8@7921}}
- **Body-fat scales are inaccurate (always 12-14% for him). Nobody can eyeball body-fat % accurately, and knowing it is nearly useless** — O; 2 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{f0vdsC2yyhY@122}} {{RryibwmuOWE@1197}}

### Dirty bulking / GOMAD
- **Dirty bulking: mostly fat and leverage, not muscle. Junk food is low in protein and micronutrients, so you feel sluggish and bloated. Whatever muscle is kept would have come from any surplus. Big naturals got big in spite of it** — O/P; ~12 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{a02c10s3Pjk@65}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@730}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@768}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@7106}}
- **After a dirty bulk you must cut and lose the "size and strength" you thought you had. Fat you think is 20 lb is really 40. Some people never lose the love handles** — P/A; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{so6MbvvtM1w@714}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@901}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@439}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@7106}}
- **GOMAD is "stupid": a gallon of milk overshoots into a large surplus with no room to manage calories, too much milk fat and protein. At most it suits very skinny or very active athletes, and it can promote dysmorphia** — O; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{E783zHvVUdk@0}} {{E783zHvVUdk@380}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1170}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@4295}}
- **Excess fat lowers testosterone, so the fatter you get, the less anabolic you are; obese naturals might even lose muscle. Visceral fat and tendon load from extra bodyweight aren't fully reversed by leaning out** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{a02c10s3Pjk@568}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@1266}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@950}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@1012}}
- **Force-feeding can trigger eating disorders as crash diets trigger anorexia. If you hate your food, the bulk fails** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: medium {{TgPYObguEM0@63}} {{NpIo8tFRESg@630}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@3200}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@1003}}
- **Exceptions (2022): performance lifters (e.g. Alpha Destiny) who compare old numbers at the same bodyweight, and aesthetic lifters (e.g. Geoffrey Verity Schofield) who track measurements, can bulk hard. Most people don't track, so it fails** — O; 2 videos, 2022; confidence: medium; in his view their strength-driven mass doesn't prove you must dirty bulk {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1897}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@2022}} {{RryibwmuOWE@2164}}

### Bulk food and appetite
- **Base the bulk on whole foods, then top up with enjoyable calorie-dense foods. Pure clean is hard for hardgainers. Junk is OK only if very skinny. Eat more vegetables on a bulk than on a cut. Avoid foods that bloat you** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{6nKKGsCCqxo@314}} {{IeYLu4_hkZY@64}} {{SV5rA-iMHRE@631}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@9859}}

### Who should bulk, cut or recomp
- **Only extremes should bulk or cut (2020): very skinny people bulk, very fat people cut. - **Skinny naturals: slow lean bulk with a small surplus, never dirty or aggressive (they just get skinny-fat). ### Recomposition
- **A recomp is its own thing, not a bulk/cut hybrid: trade fat for muscle at a fixed bodyweight, like sculpting a statue. Heavy training at maintenance makes the body draw on fat reserves to make the work easier. Slow but real** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{qmpvEERwXVI@312}} {{hjb70fJ0MGk@150}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@565}} {{O9txIDHBBIk@183}}
- **Needs both muscle and fat to trade. Ideal: someone after bulk/cut cycles, happy with size, wanting to redistribute (best started when fluffier, after a bulk). Skinny people have nothing to recomp. The higher the body fat, the easier the trade** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{qmpvEERwXVI@0}} {{IeYLu4_hkZY@0}} {{IeYLu4_hkZY@179}} {{RryibwmuOWE@1356}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@814}}
- **Lower limit: don't recomp below ~14-15% body fat (2022); earlier, recomp from 18-19% down to ~12-13% (2020). Below ~8-9%, hormones tank and muscle is lost** — P; 3 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{qmpvEERwXVI@437}} {{XASWAdsSu0M@2151}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@690}}
- **Commit at least a year; two months then switching wastes time. 2% body fat (~4-5 lb fat→muscle at his size) takes 3-7 years. What a week of bulking or cutting gives can take a month or two on a recomp** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{qmpvEERwXVI@437}} {{kkbfHkyQ_q0@2831}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@124}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@250}}
- **Keep everything stable: no big diet or training changes before or during. The only changes are a gradual calorie increase as muscle is gained (muscle costs more to maintain, or you slide into a slow cut) and a small tonnage increase** — P; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{IeYLu4_hkZY@502}} {{IeYLu4_hkZY@628}} {{IeYLu4_hkZY@940}} {{UieXEWMNnfI@316}}
- **Judge a recomp by gym performance: tonnage and strength rise at the same bodyweight. Getting weaker means untracked weight loss or wrong diet/training/hormones. Feeling depleted means it's failing. Measurements may drop the first year; use the mirror (cuts, veins)** — P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{UieXEWMNnfI@62}} {{UieXEWMNnfI@126}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@1003}} {{O9txIDHBBIk@1377}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@3438}}
- **Monitor by weight every few months, not body-fat tests every two weeks** — R; 1 video, 2020; confidence: medium {{qmpvEERwXVI@249}}
- **You can't recomp without training; sedentary maintenance gives a "reverse recomp" (lose muscle, gain fat)** — P; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{qmpvEERwXVI@312}} {{YVxYG7FhlVE@1007}}
- **Recomp is slow when advanced and suits sculpting specific body parts, not chasing overall size; long-neglected muscles grow even without a surplus** — P; ~4 videos, 2022 → 2023; confidence: medium {{O9txIDHBBIk@470}} {{O9txIDHBBIk@1040}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@441}} {{d1eLAd5WvIA@815}}
- **When a recomp stalls, don't cut deeper. Run a very slow bulk ("widen the window"), then recomp again, even if the cycle takes 5 years. When strength stalls for a long time, the recomp is over** — P; 3 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{mUAHGlseeyk@758}} {{XASWAdsSu0M@2274}} {{9WA6eQODjHQ@1193}}
- **"You maintain to recomp, you don't recomp to maintain": a recomp that only holds mass is wasted time. A heavy lifter where more gaining adds only fat can recomp 1-2 years** — P; 2 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium {{9WA6eQODjHQ@1319}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@7870}}
- **Stalled on a cut and miserable below 1900 kcal → switch to a recomp at maintenance** — R; 1 video, 2021; confidence: low {{XASWAdsSu0M@1082}}
- **Set-point idea: each person has a body-fat window (~14-18% example) where they're most anabolic. Push it slowly. The window shifts with age, training and hormones and may shrink to a true set point** — P; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low {{mUAHGlseeyk@127}} {{mUAHGlseeyk@377}} {{mUAHGlseeyk@693}}

### Cutting
- **Cut slowly: 1 lb/week is too fast; 2-3 month cuts are too fast unless it's a mini-cut or a few lb. Got fat? Accept being fat a long time and cut slowly** — P/O; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{JWwA9H6xUC0@129}} {{JWwA9H6xUC0@192}} {{JWwA9H6xUC0@256}}
- **Crash/"suicide" cuts: dangerous to health, cost strength and muscle, feel awful. Only if body image is hurting your mental health, knowing it's not optimal** — O; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{JWwA9H6xUC0@256}} {{yenqGI_FVQ4@895}} {{RryibwmuOWE@1484}} {{O9txIDHBBIk@246}}
- **Don't preemptively cut volume, reps, sets or strength work on a cut (cutting tonnage 30% programs strength loss). Start the cut on your current program and let failure show where to adjust. More volume at lower intensity is a "fallacy"** — P; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{UieXEWMNnfI@443}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@150}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@254}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@270}}
- **Strength dropping on a diet means the deficit is too large. Losing strength from weight lost is normal, but not from the deficit itself. Early in a cut, glycogen isn't depleted, so big drops aren't diet-caused** — P; 3 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{e3KPnpnfbxc@0}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@127}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@9080}}
- **In a deficit the body prioritises organs, so some muscle loss may be unavoidable. Advanced lifters lose muscle mainly because they get weaker and can't produce the usual stimulus. Naturals avoid cannibalisation through patience** — P; 3 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{JWwA9H6xUC0@64}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@40}} {{vC9cKh9tFxs@880}}
