# Equipment & Home Gym

**Bottom line**
- Straps: bodybuilders should strap up whenever grip limits a back/pulling set, then train forearms directly (10-15 min/week). Hook and mixed grip are powerlifting dogma. He holds this from 2020 to 2025.
- He sees the barbell and rack (with safeties) as the core of a home gym. Buy quality ("buy nice or buy twice"), build the platform yourself, buy plates cheap, and only buy what you will use at least weekly.
- From 2020 to 2021 he argued that owning your gym unlocks progress and commercial gyms waste time. From 2023 to 2025 he trains hybrid (legs at home, overhead and isolation at a commercial gym) and calls commercial gyms optimal for hypertrophy. He now values Smith machines, cables and good machines more than he did as the 2020 "barbell purist".
- Gear such as sleeves, wraps and belts is optional and largely placebo for hypertrophy. He doesn't use a belt and stopped using the slingshot. In 2024 he squats in flat shoes.
- With minimal equipment you can still grow: a pull-up bar, rings and bands, weighted calisthenics, supersets to failure. Calisthenics alone makes legs and big size hard.

### Supportive gear (belts, sleeves, wraps, slingshot, shoes)

### Platform, floor, mats
- **Don't lift on bare concrete (drops damage it, and it's hard on knees and stability). Build your own platform: under $150 vs ~$1000 retail (2020); $100 at Home Depot vs $1000 Rogue (2023). His 16x8 ft platform cost ~$500 vs ≥$4000 custom (Feb 2020); his 16x9 ft one cost $800-900 vs $5-6k contracted (Dec 2020); about $3000 saved** — P/A; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high {{Lg0FY5wzMqw@248}} {{j0UMvyV80VY@0}} {{SbPaZ5giUNs@123}} {{6YC_gmRNhPo@1325}}
- **Build method: draw a paper plan 1-2 months ahead and do a mock assembly. Buy plywood in person (online stock is warped).
