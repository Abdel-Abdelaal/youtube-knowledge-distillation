# Exercise selection

**Bottom line**
- Build every program from compounds that cover the movement patterns (knee flexion, hip hinge, horizontal + vertical press, horizontal + vertical pull), then treat isolation as equally serious, not optional. Compounds alone leave arms, long-head triceps, rear/side delts, calves, neck, forearms and abs behind. Isolation-only programs keep naturals small.
- Pick a small, stable roster of lifts that suit your build and that you enjoy. Train each lift for months, rotate only close ("soft") variations, and never "shock the muscle" with novelty. Novices need 1-2 exercises per muscle, advanced lifters 3-5 at most.
- His 2023-25 favourites lean on the stretch, stability and a good stimulus-to-fatigue ratio: RDL over deadlift (he deadlifts about once a month), weighted dips and ring push-ups over bench, pull-ups plus pullovers for lats, heel-elevated/SSB/belt squats and split squats over back squats, cable laterals, preacher/EZ curls, JM press and overhead cable extensions, finger curls, decline sit-ups.
- Machines, cables and the Smith machine moved from "for advanced lifters only" (2020) to legitimate tools for stable, hard sets (2023-24). Free weights are still the base for naturals.
- Open every session with the heaviest, most demanding compound. Never open with isolation or put two similar heavy lifts back to back.

## Positions

### General principles: compounds vs isolation
- **A hypertrophy program is compounds for most of the tonnage plus isolation for whatever the compounds miss; neither alone works** — P; ~25 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high. Compounds give tonnage, time economy and warm-up. Isolation is a "sniper" that compounds (a "shotgun") can't replace. - **Isolation gets the same priority and seriousness as compounds, not tacked on at the end where it gets skipped** — P; ~8 videos, 2021 → 2024; high. Curls after squats don't hurt squat recovery. ### General principles: how many exercises, variations and rotations
- **Keep exercise selection limited: variety comes from what you do with a few lifts, not from many lifts** — P; ~15 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. Too many lifts means each is trained too rarely, technique and tonnage become unstable, and you are "a technician of new tricks rather than a master". Examples: 55 chest lifts or 45 lifts is too many, 20 exercises is too many, 4 curl variations plus 1 bench variation won't progress. {{uRWgz3nhqds@127}} {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@735}} {{dyeeKOQJhrM@620}} {{HXoY7xzHlWU@1198}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@4087}}
- **Number of exercises/variations by level** — R; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2024; medium-high. Values over time: 2020, keep 15-20 lifts per body part on a list to choose from (one method he describes). 2022, he keeps 6-7 favourite lifts per muscle in rotation. 2022, beginner 1 biceps exercise, NH 4-6. 2023, variations per lift: novice 0-1, intermediate 1-2, advanced 3-5, max 5 "hard" variations per pattern. 2024, novices 1-2 exercises per muscle, never more than 4-5, "small variations are unlimited". {{wJTax5ziJUY@125}} {{QfHfxLYRu88@3393}} {{BW-32tYoNxw@759}} {{1TKqwXCge9E@1960}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@1974}}
- **Hard vs soft variations: soft = same pattern, different implement/grip/pause (unlimited, near-full carryover); hard = different pattern (pull-up vs pulldown, squat vs leg press), max ~5, each a new skill** — P; ~5 videos, 2023 → 2024; high. A different rep range on the same lift counts as a variation. Rotate soft variations to prevent overuse without changing the pattern. {{1TKqwXCge9E@1899}} {{1TKqwXCge9E@2273}} {{43F9unmSc_I@1821}} {{43F9unmSc_I@695}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@6814}}
- **Only add a variation for a reason (a stall despite evolving rep ranges, technique, overuse, injury); never just for boredom or to "shock the muscle"** — P; ~12 videos, 2020 → 2023; high. You can't shock the muscle. Switch for boredom or injury, not growth. Constantly switching keeps you relearning neurologically. "Shocking the muscle" is D tier. {{FtwMg4iM5kA@0}} {{5ANLeP4QvX8@1767}} {{1TKqwXCge9E@1516}} {{SxqjF2oPasg@1008}} {{yUecX6qONXg@990}}
- **Rotations alternate close variations in one slot (week A/week B); options in a slot must hit the same muscle the same way** — P; ~15 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. - **Don't rotate a lift out just because it plateaus: that teaches you to quit and resets you to newbie neural gains** — P; ~3 videos, 2022 → 2023; medium. Grind through plateaus. Rotating back exercises every two months means never breaking through. {{9y2s6kyFkPc@1752}} {{4t72r8Q0vXQ@187}} {{SxqjF2oPasg@1069}}

### General principles: choosing and dropping lifts
- **No lift is mandatory for a bodybuilder; every lift (even squat, bench, deadlift) can be replaced by another in the same pattern** — P; ~12 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. Only powerlifters must do competition lifts. If a lift hurts after a fair try, ten others do the job. {{Eh0fnXIyDMc@188}} {{0RoULYC_tao@188}} {{sFMQj4Lflk0@756}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@883}}
- **Fit lifts to your build (leverages, height, mobility); the program adapts to the lifter, not the reverse** — P; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. Tall lifters: sumo, low-bar to parallel, RDL/stiff-leg, block pulls, OHP. Drop lifts that don't suit you instead of clinging to them (sunk cost). {{iCWUcBy7Vxs@187}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@740}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@816}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@8505}}
- **Enjoyment and adherence beat theoretical optimality: a 75%-optimal lift you do beats a 100% one you skip** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. Remove exercises you dislike (he found one he hadn't done in 3 months). Advanced lifters should refuse lifts they dislike. Machines are boring and get skipped. {{uL-pwl2nfa0@64}} {{BW-32tYoNxw@125}} {{gYyqqbs2SLI@252}} {{JW73oft8Q60@960}} {{7uBT8vp0lNw@509}}
- **Judge a new lift only after training it seriously for ~3 months; start light; accept a tuning phase up to 6 months** — R; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. Only program lifts you've done regularly for 3+ months. Keep a ranked "lifts done" data bank and a separate "to try" list. Compare two exercises over 3 months by progress, not mind-muscle feel. Drop failures into an "exercise graveyard". {{gWdAfpJIXbc@790}} {{rlWIogS2Qho@129}} {{9y2s6kyFkPc@1564}} {{DAZRYoex8_w@950}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@753}}
- **Lift-quality criteria (2023-24): stretch under load, high stability, good stimulus-to-fatigue, low cardio cost, quick setup (<3 min), progressable** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; high. ### General principles: specificity and the "tree"
- **Organise lifts as a tree of specificity: branches are movement patterns, the top of each branch is the king lift, and lower lifts must feed it** — P; ~12 videos, 2020 → 2021; medium (mostly early). Every lift needs neighbours feeding it; never include a lift with no close neighbours. ## How his view changed
- **Deadlift → RDL.** 2020-21: deadlifts are mandatory, ~85% of his back thickness, the best whole-body builder, floor pulls never optional {{cGzlMRCxvxo@1844}} {{ZScs2L9rero@695}} {{GClcGq2tIDU@753}}. 2022: the deadlift is an accessory to the RDL {{bPUrJ3CSqEA@256}}. 2023-24: RDL is king, the deadlift is B/C tier, an "energy vampire" done ~once a month, and bodybuilders don't need it {{43F9unmSc_I@944}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@8211}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1194}}. - **Bench → dips/ring push-ups.** 2020: bench is the best horizontal press by risk/reward; he wouldn't recommend dips over bench {{K4UBm1dV6WM@313}} {{K4UBm1dV6WM@375}}. 2023-24: weighted dips are S tier, "squat of the upper body", and he dropped bench after shoulder injuries {{DAZRYoex8_w@255}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@821}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@1205}}.

## Contradictions
- **Rotation scheduling.** 2020: rotations "should always be scheduled, not done by feel" {{z9MDwMO8K4Y@0}}. 2022: rotations should never be scheduled (e.g. every two weeks) or triggered by a plateau, only "when the muscle needs it" {{BJXFeQWGUuY@2078}}. 2023: scheduled for most people, autoregulated for advanced lifters {{1TKqwXCge9E@1389}}, which may reconcile the two.
- **Barbell rows in his programs.** 2021: "none of his programs, novice or otherwise, include barbell rows" {{Q5y4wM8zcWY@189}}. Yet many templates include them: swimming template rows 3x8-10 {{cNGPCn9Uupc@0}}, barbell/seal rows 4x8-12 {{_P0-kYyVMn4@505}}, 2023 barbell rows as the day's hinge {{7uBT8vp0lNw@509}}.
- **Leg curls/isolation for his own legs.** 2023-24 he rates the lying leg curl S tier and the leg extension Platz tier {{9L90ktKGPLw@2013}} {{_IBSNO56zuE@2954}}. But in 2024 he trains legs only with free weights and hip hinges "despite evidence" {{vVQmYGw7Kj8@1954}}. Same year, his staples include belt squats and Smith work {{Ft039H8jzXU@1695}} {{qvWxIzhT1QU@759}}.
- **Importance of exercise selection.** 2021: "exercise selection dictates how your physique turns out, so it is paramount" {{8ELQ2XZgpls@1891}}. Also 2021: for naturals "exercise selection is a by-product" of programming {{K1GVimJowoM@0}}. 2023: only B tier {{SxqjF2oPasg@3212}}.
- **Band curls.** "Mediocre, use for recovery" {{RwEJpHTwWm0@1699}} vs "excellent, the only curl he really feels" {{ZLheQPgEfoA@4878}}.
- **Pullovers for chest.** "Best complement to the bench" and a chest/lat builder {{AFK9x4EZh2Q@127}} {{IYobXlRVLd0@746}} vs "mediocre for chest" and "not a chest lift" {{cUkWvnY8AJI@3324}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@507}}.
- **Leg press.** 2021: dislikes it, causes lower back pain {{8mGHG5C-EPE@497}} vs 2022/2024: a fine leg builder and the pick for people with lower back problems {{SPicD3F686s@1123}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@2094}}.
- **Seal vs Pendlay rows.** Seal rows are among the best rows {{4ORCb_i7MxY@185}} vs he prefers Pendlay and hates a chest pad {{QfHfxLYRu88@2515}}.
- **Kroc rows.** Slightly inferior to DB rows, explosive without negative control {{Cbq-vxVba4k@882}} vs "incredibly underrated, unmatched for traps" {{6p2x4kDDjzs@2584}}. These may just be different muscles judged (lats vs traps).
