# Strength

**Bottom line**
- For a bodybuilder, strength is a means, not the goal: it drives tonnage and progressive overload. You must get stronger to grow, but chasing strength numbers (1RMs, big-three standards, powerbuilding) costs size.
- Size and strength are correlated within one person but are different qualities. Between individuals, leverages, skill, nervous system and bodyweight blur the link, so you can't read strength from size or the reverse.
- Neural/technical gains on a practised lift are mostly done within ~1 month to 1 year (~6 months of weekly practice). After that, strength added at stable bodyweight and technique = muscle. Strength gained from fat gain is leverage and vanishes on a cut.
- Never test 1RMs. Build rep strength (a 10RM tracks muscle better than a 1RM) and use low-rep work only as a small, focused, unsupersetted dose. By 2024-25 his own heavy work had moved to rare deadlifts and squats, with RDLs, dips and weighted chins carrying the load.

## Positions

### Strength vs size
- **Strength ≠ size; they are different qualities with cross-pollination, and training for one is not training for the other** — P; ~15 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "share a wall but not the same room". - **But you can't grow without getting stronger: size and strength both rise, one faster than the other** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "your goal is not strength, but you will have to get stronger" {{k8k1PycHKDI@0}} {{k8k1PycHKDI@220}} {{JgAoGDmM4n4@497}} {{jUMmu583w34@1030}}
- **Within one person, strength gains on long-practised lifts reflect muscle**: if your legs go from 20 to 25 in, your squat went up. Small size increases give surprisingly large strength increases. Between people, leverages dominate (20 in legs squatting ~400 vs 22 in legs squatting 500) — P/D; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{VVckf4vyYPE@260}} {{-1dECrWYeFk@230}} {{7XqE7GhDsgc@938}} {{LxGdGChvk2c@3076}}
- **Strength athletes train functions; bodybuilders train muscles** (a powerlifter squats to train his squat, a bodybuilder to train quads, glutes and hamstrings). - **Specialising in one costs the other; no program maximises both.** No natural bodybuilder holds powerlifting records, and at equal bodyweight bodybuilders are weaker and powerlifters stronger, which in his view is due to training, not genetics — P/O; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; "pizza and sushi" restaurant analogy {{-banYSTnkq4@63}} {{JgAoGDmM4n4@310}} {{kAVZsNLPNQ4@2519}} {{loDNjTHZjG4@2967}}
- **Bodybuilders have "accidental strength"**: weaker than powerlifters, much stronger than average. ### Neural adaptation vs muscle
- **Neural/technical gains are front-loaded and capped.** After ~6 months of weekly practice of a new lift (later "a month to a year"), you sit at 95-100% technical mastery, and further strength at the same technique = muscle. Saying all strength gains could be neural is "wrong and demoralizing" — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: high {{7XqE7GhDsgc@938}} {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@519}} {{ISLtO-xJbYo@252}} {{AeIKc3A2FiY@270}}
- **Rotating or learning lifts gives artificial progress**: his SSB box squat went 185→365 in under a year, mostly relearning. The box lets you think you're stronger at the squat. A second +100 lb on the bench brings far more muscle than the first — P/A; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@399}} {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@450}} {{k3O630Nz4B8@188}} {{bTin_tBGoVo@395}}
- **Strength is largely skill**: motor-unit recruitment in a pattern, needing a fresh CNS. Specific frequency-manipulation programs (daily squatting, Bulgarian/Wisconsin-style) raise one lift without much muscle — P/O; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium; he never prescribes them for size {{bJK7L5fb-eI@125}} {{ISLtO-xJbYo@252}} {{u10c43TRDb0@880}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@250}}
- **A big % strength gain with no muscle adaptation is biologically impossible past the novice stage; strength that jumps with no visible change is a red flag** (+100 lb on chins, dips and OHP should visibly change you) — P; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{xn8RN9807Ns@129}} {{U1zVxzMVM2E@2420}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1139}}
- **Strength detrains faster than size**: a month without a gym barely changed his legs but cost ~60 lb on lifts and work capacity. Presses, especially OHP, regress fast when not practised — A/O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{VVckf4vyYPE@815}} {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@822}} {{AeIKc3A2FiY@340}}

### Bodyweight, fat and leverage
- **Mass moves mass, but fat-driven strength isn't real progress**: +100 lb of mostly fat raises absolute but not pound-for-pound strength. +20 lb on bench with +20 lb bodyweight = no real progress, and fat-driven strength vanishes on a cut — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{VVckf4vyYPE@322}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@242}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1580}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1139}}
- **Bodyweight helps pressing via leverage (shorter ROM), much less hinges**: he benched 315 at ~220 and 335 when fatter, and regressed when the fat came off. "Put 50 lb on your frame, add ~100 lb to your bench." In a deficit, hinges barely suffer while bench and OHP drop a lot — A/P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{so6MbvvtM1w@902}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@616}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@668}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@316}}
- **For pure strength, carry as much mass as possible, preferably muscle** (400 lb at 10% beats 400 at 20%). Getting strong requires letting bodyweight rise, so "otter mode" means never getting really strong or reaching full size — P; ~2 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: medium {{mUAHGlseeyk@502}} {{HOgwZO70z_M@878}}
- **You can get stronger without gaining weight**: all his lifts rose at ≤210 lb (2020), and in 2024 he beats his old 240 lb lifts at 220 except the bench — A; ~2 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{7XqE7GhDsgc@314}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@2385}}
- **Pound-for-pound matters**: Wilks-type scoring favours light lifters, and big frames that bulk would otherwise dominate. Lighter is easier for calisthenics, and his handstand push-ups were easy at 160, impossible at 220 — P/O; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{f0vdsC2yyhY@1325}} {{WmH326eoF04@1477}} {{ZN5ohmPPfjM@630}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@1770}}
- **Leverages dominate lower-body numbers**: long femurs or torso make squats hard and deadlifts easier, and arms past the knees would make a deadlift world-record holder. Tall lifters have more absolute strength and shouldn't compare with short ones, but short lifters keep a squat advantage via ROM. Bigger structure (wide hips, big joints) = more strength potential — P; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{aiVrEt5ziHQ@63}} {{3Mzk6FLh5UI@310}} {{Ox6TQAC9cW0@253}} {{3nyuEtmqL-I@1060}}

### Strength work inside a bodybuilding program
- **Strength work is the backbone and pillar of hypertrophy training, never removed, year-round** (not just in a block). It is little tonnage but high-intensity peaks that drive progression, and "the strength hook is the one cornerstone you never cut" — P; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium-high (strong early, less emphasised later) {{65rLZe9cz_0@251}} {{oLm1CDuY9zg@64}} {{RgGkGiaGqks@1510}} {{_2M6FhWuIvs@314}}
- **Strength work opens the session; a day with zero strength work is bad** (occasional exceptions). Pre-activation must be light, and an explosive opener (KB swing short of failure) potentiates while a leg-curl opener fatigues. A small dose fires the nervous system for the accessories — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{-banYSTnkq4@230}} {{zCo31HKOsIY@317}} {{62OBPecQPnM@440}} {{nqzVqvMvQ1s@1196}}
- **Strength work is a minority of the tonnage**; the rest is hypertrophy, and strength exists only to drive tonnage. Bouncing between strength and hypertrophy phases is "like living with divorced parents" — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{7ZS-mGePFXw@340}} {{7ZS-mGePFXw@90}} {{_2M6FhWuIvs@480}}
- **Do strength sets as solo sets; never superset or giant-set heavy squats, deadlifts or top sets** (at most neck or calves alongside). His bench depends on focus, though bench and rows can be supersetted at 4x6-8 — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{l3tsyzKEktQ@440}} {{Z-D9kRwK90I@63}} {{XncJr9uMpWo@550}} {{nqzVqvMvQ1s@438}}
- **Plan rep and set evolution more carefully for strength work than volume work**, since it opens the session and affects everything after — P; 2 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{65rLZe9cz_0@752}} {{65rLZe9cz_0@628}}
- **Strength blocks are unnecessary for bodybuilders**: "build a strength base first" is a lie because strength grows on a bodybuilding program anyway. Bodybuilders don't peak, so peaking practices don't transfer; speed work at 40% 1RM is junk volume — O; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: high (later view) {{RgGkGiaGqks@187}} {{Rz4uzjM068M@631}} {{JgAoGDmM4n4@686}} {{SZEjCsuclx8@1968}}
- **"Get strong" advice must specify which lifts**: unless the answer is every lift for every muscle, ignore it. Trying to get as strong as possible on every movement for every muscle with good form *is* bodybuilding — P; ~3 videos, 2023; confidence: medium {{elk4uYvjXos@1701}} {{elk4uYvjXos@1321}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@1383}}
- **A bodybuilding program progresses strength more slowly** (effort spread over more muscles; a different goal) but develops every body part. Powerlifting maximises strength while bodybuilding stabilises it for steady tonnage — P/O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{YcHkXwsIOI8@1406}} {{7ZS-mGePFXw@90}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@318}}
- **The 85% rule deliberately doesn't maximise strength gains**, because that would minimise volume accumulation — P; 1 video, 2022; confidence: low {{z2uzpl_smSg@1574}}
- **Train weak points, not variations that flatter your strengths** (his deadlift weak point is the top, not the floor) — P; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low {{_iaU9_rM1QQ@438}}
- **Approach the bar certain the programmed weight goes up**; since adopting this he has never failed a squat or deadlift in his notebook — P/A; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low {{xxOQxGwiX4I@1197}}

### Rep schemes for the main lifts
- **Deadlift: 1-3 sets of 2-5 reps, never more than 5; often a single set of 5 is enough at high intensity.** 3x3 is "amazing" for deadlift if you leave reps in the tank, and a 3x5 is a good volume for floor pulls. Back-offs: 4x3 with a comfortable triple, or 3x3 submax — R; ~14 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{t_RZT-AkjhE@127}} {{sCMGXCXKVjs@1130}} {{9M7QVlvFdQY@1150}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1131}}
- **Novice deadlift: sets of 6 are fine (2x6 = 12 reps)**; don't do 12-rep maxes on conventional, where cardio and structure limit you before muscle — R; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{4aThavRpYLw@314}} {{mbtAKsbDz9I@350}} {{8mGHG5C-EPE@440}}
- **Squat: 4-8 reps, never singles, doubles or triples** (to avoid valgus and the back tipping forward); 3x4-8 on one heavy day, or 3-6/3-8. Single sets of 5 at RPE 6, 7, 8 as a ramp to a top set beat 3 sets at the same intensity — R; ~4 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{t_RZT-AkjhE@127}} {{cGzlMRCxvxo@968}} {{mbtAKsbDz9I@373}} {{YcHkXwsIOI8@316}}
- **Bench: never below 4 reps; 4-8 for capricious shoulders** (3x4-6 flat barbell, 4x6-8; 8-10 fine). Paused bench 6-10, Spoto 8-12, close-grip 4x6-10 (his favourite, progressed very fast) — R; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{t_RZT-AkjhE@64}} {{jSUmn33lmlI@563}} {{D3mJh0ZA5nc@524}} {{6sFaB3rAY1A@1317}}
- **Overhead press as strength work: 2-5 reps, 3-6 sets (4x4-6 also given), never superset**; 12-15 is too many when it opens the day. It's safe enough for 1-2RMs (2020), but 3x3 lacks the volume he wants for OHP — R; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{t_RZT-AkjhE@127}} {{cGzlMRCxvxo@406}} {{niD0UuwTUvg@129}} {{-anA4CV2SVU@440}}
- **3x3 is a favourite strength scheme** (little room to sandbag, very high intensity) for bench, squat, pull-ups and deadlift. Don't use it for tonnage lifts like rows or RDLs — R/O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{hYFO2eKn-m4@126}} {{hYFO2eKn-m4@189}} {{hYFO2eKn-m4@150}}
- **Weighted pull-ups 3x3-5** (very low volume, intense; after leg day they decompress the spine). His 2025 numbers: chin-ups +60 lb x8, regular-grip pull-ups +115 lb x3-5 — R/A; ~2 videos, 2023 → 2025; confidence: medium {{DA7QvIAyptw@1136}} {{0O0nG2jLadU@132}}
- **Mix rep ranges; strength isn't only low reps.** Successful powerlifters train varied ranges, and switching high→low reps spikes strength short-term, but long-term progression is what matters — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2025; confidence: medium {{Vw9MhD5Amfw@132}} {{7XqE7GhDsgc@1188}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@1325}} {{ACFKXT44BzM@1694}}
- **Reps at a given %1RM are individual** (2 vs 4 reps at 90%), so online %/RPE calculators mislead — P; 1 video, 2022; confidence: low {{z2uzpl_smSg@631}}

### Max testing
- **Never test 1RMs as a bodybuilder**: maxing is a powerlifting skill for peaking, with no hypertrophy benefit. 1RMs don't even build strength (2-3RMs or 1 RIR might), and every max-out costs muscle-building capacity — P/O; ~7 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{T_DyRlu3Y7I@317}} {{CTMwuqNsY9Q@498}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@763}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@1262}}
- **Estimate your max from rep performance instead (e.g. 200x10)**; he hadn't tested a 1RM in ~4 years by 2021 and programs by intensity. A double just under max beats a single (485x2 vs 500x1: double the tonnage, less risk and fatigue) — R; 1 video, 2021; confidence: medium {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@1054}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@665}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@922}}
- **Constant max testing "smashes the piggy bank"**: testing a deadlift 1RM every 2 weeks kept him at 465-485 lb for a year — P/A; 1 video, 2024; confidence: medium {{Ft039H8jzXU@1822}}
- **2-rep max deadlifts (2020-21) worked for him**, with zero back pain and more progress than any other lift. A 2RM done for hypertrophy counts as hypertrophy training — A/O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: low (superseded, see changes) {{2hwj-NEebj8@378}} {{mbtAKsbDz9I@330}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1810}}

### Carryover between lifts
- **Specificity wins: the best way to bench more is to bench, and the best deadlift accessory is more deadlifting** — P; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{9M7QVlvFdQY@1150}} {{8I6IPBr2_z0@317}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@2622}}
- **Bench carryover**: close-grip bench yes; skull crushers almost none; push-ups and ring push-ups don't build bench strength (he was advanced at push-ups yet benched ~145 the first time); dips carry over a lot (a lifter hit a 4-plate bench after 6 weeks thanks to dips). Slingshot → raw is minimal, and only slingshotting for 2 months could cost raw strength — P/O; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{K4UBm1dV6WM@947}} {{-anA4CV2SVU@504}} {{3z75cYOdZ74@186}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@1644}}
- **Squat/deadlift carryover**: front→back squat is real but smaller than thought, pause squat more; front squat barely helps the conventional pull (only upper-back rigidity). A stronger squat helps the deadlift up to a point. The SSB box squat in deadlift stance may be the best conventional-deadlift accessory. Sumo→conventional carries better than the reverse — P/O; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{7C5gtGrWYM0@378}} {{pHNNbwju0GU@495}} {{pHNNbwju0GU@669}} {{lG5SZbRdOjA@1512}}
- **Pause reps build strength but force lighter weights and can't replace touch-and-go**; use them as variations. Even top lifters use variations, so pure minimalism fails — P; ~3 videos, 2021; confidence: medium {{MP01uQbcoLc@124}} {{MP01uQbcoLc@868}} {{w4-2c3iWvDQ@1255}}
- **Partial ROM can help the full lift, but partials and odd lifts mask true strength** (1000 lb rack pull vs 500 deadlift; his 500 lb front-rack holds vs Olympic lifters' 700) — P/D; ~3 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{-wk3jSpm434@445}} {{IgxJijW3MSQ@125}} {{sQSM1JiaN80@2533}}
- **Machine numbers don't transfer** (a 500 lb leg press usually means a ~300 squat). Leg presses at 500-600 lb are the kind of load legs should handle — O; ~2 videos, 2020 → 2023; confidence: low {{GClcGq2tIDU@1450}} {{_IBSNO56zuE@1062}}
- **Squats are more taxing than deadlifts** with proper technique; big deadlift-to-squat ratios come from sloppy pulls and timid squats — O; ~2 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{4aThavRpYLw@376}} {{GClcGq2tIDU@1134}}
- **Where you fail**: in hinges and squats you fail on the positive or reversal, never the negative. Many "mid-squat" fails are a failure to build speed out of the hole. The fishing-rod (rounded) position makes lockout harder — P/O; ~3 videos, 2020; confidence: low {{3IMgRqcRN4Y@1503}} {{N8Dp4inzHog@631}} {{iY2uzM3vlBA@188}}
- **Mixed grip can beat straps for some** (him: double overhand caps him at ~330 single vs 470 mixed). Powerlifters should give hook grip a real try first, and thick-grip training makes a normal bar feel easier — P/A; ~4 videos, 2020; confidence: medium {{sT8z3ENnqAQ@189}} {{eU_f4lGi9vA@377}} {{-GYV9s5Ad8s@820}} {{m-DWvBP_ncU@63}}

### Strength standards
- **Strength standards are C-tier and he ignores them unless you compete**: they rest on unverified self-reports or powerlifter data and round plate numbers, and they ignore leverages, experience, nervous system and timeline. They compare you to weaklings and turn hypertrophy training into number-chasing. If you compete, use your sport's competition standards — O/P; ~10 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{9M7QVlvFdQY@300}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@1275}} {{LxGdGChvk2c@1513}} {{SxqjF2oPasg@440}}
- **Size-from-strength standards are illogical** ("a 200 lb OHP gives big shoulders"; invert it to "20 in arms means a strong curl"). A 3-plate bench goal can keep a bodybuilder's chest small — O/P; ~4 videos, 2022 → 2023; confidence: medium {{LxGdGChvk2c@2004}} {{9kG_0Y7UYnw@253}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@754}}
- **1/2/3/4 (135 OHP / 225 bench / 315 squat / 405 deadlift)** roughly holds for novices because loads are low, but beyond novice leverages make fixed numbers meaningless. OHP 135→225 is far harder than the others, and a 3-plate bench is much easier than a 2-plate OHP — D/O; 1 video (several claims), 2020; confidence: medium {{3Mzk6FLh5UI@0}} {{3Mzk6FLh5UI@123}} {{3Mzk6FLh5UI@249}} {{3Mzk6FLh5UI@500}}
- **Minimum baselines he does endorse**: a trained adult male should never drop below 20 push-ups. The average man can't do a pull-up or deadlift bodyweight, and every able-bodied man should. A year of training should exceed a few push-ups and a 60 lb OHP at 140 lb, and an untrained man pulls ~200 lb while any lifter can reach ~400 — O/P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{EElvKR4K_HE@355}} {{yr4zj-WbG4I@1520}} {{ZN5ohmPPfjM@630}} {{uKOuj3Mzc9c@1570}}
- **"Natural can't bench 400" and idolising round numbers create plateaus in your head** — O; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low {{-cPcWQqMRC8@800}}

### Powerlifting, powerbuilding, strength sports
- **Train like a powerlifter and you'll look like one**: bottom-heavy (~70-80% lower-body volume) and not aesthetic. Lifters chasing the big three often stay skinny-fat (the "Starting Strength problem"), and most powerlifters wrongly expect aesthetics. A 700 lb deadlift isn't needed for a big back — O/P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{-1dECrWYeFk@127}} {{nqzVqvMvQ1s@753}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@250}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@2344}}
- **Rejects powerbuilding**: heavy big-three plus high-rep accessories usually delivers neither. It trains one narrow strength (1RM on three lifts) that must dominate and drains energy, and most powerbuilders never compete. Fix = drop 1RM training and you're just bodybuilding (silver/bronze era) — O/P; ~6 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: high; he debated Dr. Milo Wolf on it {{UgkxSVRSWZapd_B7fnXIOyXduIuEundS2DZf@0}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@63}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@629}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@1325}}
- **The big three are ~1/10 of the body's strength potential**: big naturals may be weak on them but strong on isolations and back work. The big three were maybe 5% of Arnold's volume, and they should express strength, not pigeonhole you — O; ~3 videos, 2023 → 2024; confidence: medium {{hEuy__sCbZA@1830}} {{loDNjTHZjG4@3404}} {{loDNjTHZjG4@3217}}
- **"Strength copers"**: lifters who chose strength because they couldn't build a physique, or who claim "the best way to get big is to get strong", are lying to themselves — O; ~3 videos, 2022 → 2024; confidence: medium {{GeMs1MAEK60@1438}} {{9kG_0Y7UYnw@1640}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@2649}}
- **What bodybuilders can learn**: powerlifters who stall add volume and run hypertrophy blocks because strength eventually needs bigger fibres, and they often have huge quads. Their peak-drop-peak cycle works for strength, not tonnage — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{N8Dp4inzHog@63}} {{k8k1PycHKDI@249}} {{7dqBu5KLIoQ@189}} {{_2M6FhWuIvs@420}}
- **Strength sports are minimalist by default and bodybuilding maximalist**; the more specific the need, the more minimalist the method. Bodybuilding and powerlifting are "cousins who shouldn't mix" — P/O; ~3 videos, 2021; confidence: medium {{w4-2c3iWvDQ@709}} {{w4-2c3iWvDQ@795}} {{cNGPCn9Uupc@250}}
- **Strongman is a better strength test than powerlifting**; a strength athlete who never competes isn't one. Former strongmen and powerlifters in their 30s-40s fit well into bodybuilding — O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2025; confidence: low-medium {{qdPmmahWTVA@253}} {{eaypnfdz3J0@3019}} {{J9_udWdk6ZM@1030}}
- **Modern strength athletes recomp to lighter classes and carry lean muscle**; a fluffy lifter loses to leaner lifters in his class — O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: low {{so6MbvvtM1w@1191}} {{WmH326eoF04@1539}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@668}}

### Verifying lifts
- **Unverified lifts don't count**: no video, witness or record; bands, chains, boxes, cambered bars or above-parallel squats; unweighed plates (Rogue technical plates can fake ~100 lb). Weigh and load plates on camera, ideally at a sanctioned meet, and claim records only in competition. A deadlift-bar, deadlift-only event inflates pulls (+20-30 lb at 600) — O/D; ~9 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{g4mX62QGzTo@1251}} {{RYkGN4SOkX0@1319}} {{U1zVxzMVM2E@1807}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@509}}
- **Strength claims are meaningless without bodyweight** (a 260 lb lifter mocking a 200 lb one; IFBB pros at 250-270 pulling ~600 is weak in his view) — O; ~3 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium {{g4mX62QGzTo@1627}} {{GeMs1MAEK60@2381}} {{loDNjTHZjG4@3092}}

### Strength for athletes and fighters
- **Lifting doesn't make you a fighter, but hypertrophy carries to fighting while fight training does nothing for size.** Strength matters more for grappling than striking. ## How his view changed
- **Deadlift practice**: 2020-21 he pushed the deadlift hardest with 2RMs and saw the most progress there (495x2 PR) {{mbtAKsbDz9I@330}} {{twEeL-z4Zug@891}}. 2024: frequent max testing had stalled him at 465-485 for a year, so he now deadlifts every ~40 days with RDLs, good mornings and hypers doing the work {{Ft039H8jzXU@1822}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1884}}. Later view = low-frequency deadlifts, no max testing.
- **Strength blocks and powerbuilding**: 2020 says most bodybuilding "is really powerbuilding", and 1-2 mesocycles of a powerlifting program to raise strength is "a good use" {{PYmr91aELnE@413}} {{YcHkXwsIOI8@1406}}. 2022-24 rejects powerbuilding and strength blocks, and calls "build a strength base first" a lie {{JgAoGDmM4n4@686}} {{SZEjCsuclx8@1968}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@63}}. - **Size and strength moving apart**: 2020 rejects that strength can rise while muscles shrink, saying size can't rise while strength stays flat {{TIsnQ03FegQ@338}} {{k8k1PycHKDI@220}}. 2022 cites Candito losing size while his total rose and says you can trade one for the other {{LxGdGChvk2c@3139}} {{LxGdGChvk2c@3076}}. - **Role of big-lift strength work**: 2020-21 "a day with zero strength work is bad", strength work is the backbone, never cut {{-banYSTnkq4@230}} {{_2M6FhWuIvs@314}}. 2024-25: high-bar squat and bench only 3-5x/year, he "doesn't care about strength", and his heavy work moved to dips and weighted pull-ups {{0O0nG2jLadU@572}} {{-7bzbmL-x3Q@748}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@4816}}.
