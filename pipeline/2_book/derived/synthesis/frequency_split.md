# Frequency & Split

**Bottom line**
- Frequency is the variable naturals ignore most and the one that matters most. Spread a muscle's weekly sets over 2-3+ sessions instead of piling them into one. Training a muscle once a week is suboptimal unless you are deliberately coasting on it.
- Bro splits (one muscle per day, once a week) are the worst template for naturals. His favourite is the "gentleman split": an upper/lower skeleton with body-part emphases and synergistic pairings, with supersets and flexible boundaries. Late on he also rates full body 2-3x/week and PPL (pull-push-legs order) as good splits.
- Default spacing: ~48 h minimum between hard sessions for the same muscle. Upper body is trained ~3x/week, legs every 2 days (until 2024). In late 2024, as a stronger lifter, he moved legs to 2x/week with 72-96 h between sessions and said "never sacrifice intensity for frequency".
- 4 days/week is his favourite sustainable frequency for most people, 3 is fine, and 2 (full body) suits busy lifters. He himself ran 6 days on an 8-day rotation (2020-2024), then dropped to 5 days in late 2024 (3 upper including an arm day, 2 lower).
- Within the week, never put heavy squats and heavy deadlifts on the same day or on consecutive days. Alternate taxing and easy days, start the week with upper, and put the heaviest lower day right before the rest day. Upper days are long and lower days short.
- Within a session, use the "pyramid of priority": heavy strength work first, never supersetted. Then non-competing supersets and giant sets. Put work you tend to skip (neck, abs, calves) early, as supersets.

### Spacing between sessions for the same muscle
- **~48 h is the default and minimum for hard work on the same muscle. Do not hit a muscle hard two days in a row.** — P; ~8 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high. 2020 reasoning: muscle protein synthesis runs about 48 h for naturals. 2024: benching two days in a row is bad even with different variations, and "48 hours is a bare minimum". Back sessions also need ≥48 h, even if that means two back exercises on the same day. Alternate pull-up and chin-up variations 48 h apart. {{m_IGaiqrCvA@255}} {{tZ9CSu2DMIE@186}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1327}} {{7uBT8vp0lNw@1386}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@257}}
- **Lighter back-to-back hits are allowed: full recovery matters for peak performance, not for hypertrophy.** — P; ~6 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium. Advanced lifters can add a light blood-flow movement 24 h after a heavy day. You can hit lats lightly one day and destroy them the next, or stop short of full fatigue and finish the muscle 1-2 days later. Consecutive days at 90/80/70% Mon-Wed then rest until Saturday is a valid alternative to one big session. Small doses (a few pull-ups) can be repeated daily. {{RqS8AxVE28I@62}} {{RqS8AxVE28I@125}} {{ADLnarIVojI@315}} {{p-kR3RfGBHw@0}} {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1141}}

### Split templates
- **The bro split (one muscle per day, once a week) is the worst template for naturals.** — O/P; ~15 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high. Its frequency is too low and its per-session volume too high, so you pile in junk volume to justify the trip. It breeds hatred of leg day and small legs (the "skip leg day" meme). - **Keep rotations simple (A/B) and within the same movement pattern, and keep strength work regular, not rotated. If alternatives do not share a pattern, the missing work must appear elsewhere.** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium-high. Too much rotation (15 squat variations) means each lift is practised ~3 times a year. Full body and A/B/C upper rotations can be too much, and each added rotation complicates things. {{8I6IPBr2_z0@190}} {{cGzlMRCxvxo@1294}} {{7n8RMhsxEB4@437}} {{7n8RMhsxEB4@880}} {{G7rVTseabFU@1330}}
- **A good program is flexible. If moving or dropping one exercise breaks it, it is a bad program. Since 2024 he runs a "template, not a program": upper or lower days filled from a memorised list of exercises. {{ehxjCB_MdKc@0}} {{JnWWva91QUM@63}} {{itnjfi6RYtY@1631}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@125}} {{JW73oft8Q60@930}}
- **Stick with a good program for years, as an endless repeating cycle, rather than jumping every 6 months. Programs that change too much produce artificial PRs.** — P; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low-medium. ### Special populations
- **Fighters and athletes: lifting is secondary. Purists get 2 minimal, intense sessions/week (~1.5 h each, ~3 h/week) plus an optional conditioning day. An upper-dominant split (4 upper, 2 lower) suits fighters because lower lifts tax the CNS and tendons more. ## How his view changed
- **His own split.** 2020-2023: 6 days/week on an 8-day rotation of themed day pairs, Sunday off, so a full cycle takes 9 days {{m_IGaiqrCvA@0}} {{o3RTXWAia4A@65}} {{itnjfi6RYtY@188}} {{43F9unmSc_I@187}}. 2024-03: upper/lower 6x/week {{SV5rA-iMHRE@379}}. 2024-09: went from 7 to 6 to 5 days: 3 upper (2 torso, 1 arms) + 2 lower {{JW73oft8Q60@1090}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@5023}}. 2024-12: upper 3x, lower 2x {{qvWxIzhT1QU@759}}. 2025-01: 3 home + 2 commercial gym sessions + 2 backyard carry sessions {{0O0nG2jLadU@824}}.
