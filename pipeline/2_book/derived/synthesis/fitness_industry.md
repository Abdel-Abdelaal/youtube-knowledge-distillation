# Fitness Industry

**Bottom line**
- The fitness industry, YouTube fitness included, exists to sell. Hard work can't be sold, so it sells supplements, programs, coaching, gimmicks and drug-built physiques. In his view, whoever profits from a product should have their integrity questioned.
- Lifting knowledge is free. Learn to program for yourself. Don't buy cookie-cutter programs. Coaching is mostly pre-digested free knowledge. From late 2024 he runs a paid Ko-fi tier anyway, framed as teaching independence.
- Judge a teacher by whether he can demonstrate what he teaches now (natural size or strength), or has verifiable proof or credentials, and by character. Don't judge by fame, subscriber count, the "science-based" label or charisma. Stay in your lane: take hypertrophy advice from natural bodybuilders.
- Enhanced and fake-natural influencers are the core rot: unrelatable role models who sell products off drugs. By 2025 he sees honest enhanced influencers who normalise PEDs as the bigger problem. Starve them of attention instead of making natty-or-not and drama content.
- YouTube fitness is powerlifting-centric, then science-centric, and swings like a pendulum between extremes. Novices are the renewable target. Avoid parasocial fandom and guru-hopping, and be your own teacher.
