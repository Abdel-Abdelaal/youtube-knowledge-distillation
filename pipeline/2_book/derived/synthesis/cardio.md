# Cardio

**Bottom line**
- A bodybuilder's cardio has one job: your heart and lungs should never end a set before your muscles do. Getting winded on squats, deadlifts or even curls means poor conditioning, and that costs tonnage and size.
- Cardio done right doesn't kill gains. It raises work capacity, pumps and recovery. Its main cost is calories, which matters for skinny or low-appetite lifters.
- Since late 2023 his prescription is 5-15 min (usually ~10) of hard cardio after lifting or on separate days, never before lifting, plus low-intensity walking, hiking, swimming or weighted carries. His own: stair machine on tiptoes with an 80 lb backpack, a self-powered treadmill mile, and 20-30 min of outdoor carries on off days.
- Giant sets and high-rep lower-body work serve as built-in GPP. His top cardio picks are fartlek, StairMaster, rucking, rowing machine, crawling, sandbag and rebounding. He rates treadmill, long-distance running and under-desk steppers worst.

## Positions

### Purpose: conditioning for lifting
- **Never end a squat or deadlift set out of breath with reps left in the muscles. Gassing out (even being winded after one rep, on curls or after a bench set) means poor cardio. Fix it with conditioning or slower tempo** — P/O; ~16 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high; he squats 8 reps to failure and deadlifts 5 heavy without breathing hard {{LCpa0h89TJM@380}} {{ckDmNEFcwW8@189}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@62}} {{ANvvVrMqF7Y@1880}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@887}}
- **Poor conditioning means unfinished sets, lost tonnage and ultimately lost size. You can't build high lower-body tonnage without stamina, and gassing after four reps means never getting big. Many people fail intermediate programs this way** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high {{LCpa0h89TJM@128}} {{3dLu3VQinL8@757}} {{YcHkXwsIOI8@689}} {{XAcv427WvMw@1501}}
- **Balance cardiovascular and muscular endurance. Excess conditioning won't hinder growth, but being out of breath while lifting means you prioritise cardio over muscle and should fix cardio or methods** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{LCpa0h89TJM@445}} {{boPKiKE26Rw@381}} {{bJ7gfILR6RA@1316}}
- **Puking in training means low work capacity, not a good session. If 10 squats make you vomit, improve conditioning** — P; 1 video, 2024; confidence: medium {{Eta-sxecx9E@505}}
- **People who call anything over 5 reps "cardio" lack work capacity, and avoiding reps reinforces it. High reps for legs are a requirement** — P; 2 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{pUpQypTV3Ck@249}} {{CTMwuqNsY9Q@123}}
- **Lifting isn't metabolically demanding (you rest as long as you like), so you must actively seek breathlessness. Machines, mostly upper body, barely tax the heart. Older machine-only lifters should do lower-body work** — P; 2 videos, 2021 → 2022; confidence: medium {{BBqiphYfqMA@629}} {{O5xVdqgt8Uo@2290}}
- **Fitness is separate from hypertrophy: big, lean or vascular ≠ fit. Stamina and cardiovascular health are separate too (diet and lifestyle). ### Does cardio kill gains?
- **Cardio doesn't kill gains or testosterone. Biking 30 min each way to the gym didn't hurt his squat/deadlift. Low-intensity running 2x/wk for 20-30 min doesn't hurt. Good cardio boosts gains via more volume, better pumps and faster recovery** — P; ~7 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: high {{ANvvVrMqF7Y@1691}} {{9y2s6kyFkPc@2128}} {{d1eLAd5WvIA@1630}} {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@189}} {{-OjJEzZTXtQ@1134}}
- **Its real cost is calories: cardio doesn't cannibalise muscle but uses calories meant for recovery. How much you can do depends on intake. Skinny men prioritising muscle should avoid cardio-focused activity. Long slow distance limits growth only via calorie shortfall** — P; ~5 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{3dLu3VQinL8@757}} {{6sFaB3rAY1A@2008}} {{WyxeMHovPJ8@125}} {{THV7B11oalk@1266}}
- **Cardio isn't needed to be lean or recomp. It burns calories but can raise appetite, so weigh it. With his low appetite he never did cardio to burn calories (2020)** — O; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium (superseded by his 2023+ advocacy) {{ckDmNEFcwW8@378}} {{EP1Qw9aEKOU@1490}} {{XASWAdsSu0M@1144}}
- **Cardio also loads joints and muscles (2 h/day swimming tires shoulders, back and legs), so factor it into frequency and recovery. Weighted cardio has a recovery cost but is time-efficient and synergistic** — P; 2 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{3dLu3VQinL8@757}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@710}}
- **Low-intensity cardio barely touches lifters' recovery, but heavy leg training can hurt endurance, so fighters must program legs carefully** — P; 1 video, 2021; confidence: low {{ANvvVrMqF7Y@1818}}

### How much and when
- **Current prescription: 5-15 min of high-intensity cardio after every session, replacing pump finishers (2023-11). 10-15 min HIIT plus low-intensity hikes/swims (2024-11). His own is 10 hard min after the gym plus daily walks, swims or hikes** — R; 3 videos, 2023 → 2024; confidence: high {{SxqjF2oPasg@1952}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@887}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@3467}}
- **Never do cardio before lifting; it lowers lifting intensity. Do it after lifting, on another day or at another time. Warm up by ramping the lift (wear layers), not with cardio** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{PxMpAWWtcrg@444}} {{THV7B11oalk@761}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@3344}}
- **On a cut, do cardio separately (e.g. morning) because energy is too low next to lifting. On a bulk, right before/after lifting is fine** — R; 1 video, 2024; confidence: medium {{wBct3Pl8OY4@9732}}
- **Program sprints like deadlifts: intense, well warmed up, 1-2x/week ("already a lot"), far from heavy squat/deadlift days. Hamstring strain risk → warm up and do weighted stretches** — R; 3 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{6sFaB3rAY1A@1506}} {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@442}} {{u10c43TRDb0@1444}}
- **Placement with leg days: 2020, sprints/longer cardio 2-3x/wk on days off or after leg day before a push day, or on light days. 2022, avoid intense leg cardio on leg days. 2024-12, hard cardio on top of leg training boosts recovery and conditioning** — P; 3 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: medium (see changes) {{u10c43TRDb0@1444}} {{9y2s6kyFkPc@2128}} {{-OjJEzZTXtQ@1134}}
- **His 2024 routine: cardio 2x/week (a hard threshold mile on a non-motorised treadmill, or 10 min stair stepper on tiptoes with an 80 lb backpack); 20-30 min outdoor weighted carries on his two days off** — A/R; 4 videos, 2024; confidence: high {{-OjJEzZTXtQ@1197}} {{-OjJEzZTXtQ@1261}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@569}} {{qvWxIzhT1QU@948}}
- **Joggers typically need 3-4 runs/week to benefit** — D; 1 video, 2024; confidence: low {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@506}}

### Cardio inside lifting (GPP)
- **Supersets and giant sets of non-interfering movements, done non-stop, are a bodybuilder's GPP. They tax the heart without separate cardio or hurting strength, and conditioning rises with working weights. Giant sets are "B tier, passive cardio with almost no cost" if not turned into a circuit** — P/A; ~12 videos, 2020 → 2024; confidence: high {{Z-D9kRwK90I@190}} {{LCpa0h89TJM@255}} {{QfHfxLYRu88@128}} {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@1011}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@887}}
- **Examples: 10 reps of a lower-body move → weighted carry → ab move; start with a squat then easy exercises; end upper days with push-ups + lateral raises + face pulls until quality drops; pull-ups + dips for novices. If a pull-up/face-pull superset kills performance, your conditioning is poor** — R; ~5 videos, 2021 → 2023; confidence: medium {{BBqiphYfqMA@660}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@740}} {{ZkQZPnTVKpg@315}} {{XncJr9uMpWo@1106}} {{DA7QvIAyptw@1199}}
- **Lower-body lifts build work capacity best (heavy high-rep squats/deadlifts are "compressed HIIT"). High-rep lower body plus giant sets give enough moderate conditioning. If you skip legs, add supersets to compensate** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{87CMjYjX2jY@1504}} {{ckDmNEFcwW8@691}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@441}} {{A55c7v1yMn4@875}}
- **Specificity: high-rep squats carry over to breathing under heavy squats better than sprints. Find your most cardio-taxing lift and make it easier over time (variation, shorter rests, giant sets). Strongmen use farmer's walks or hikes; powerlifters use squats or rack/isometric pulls** — P; ~4 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{LCpa0h89TJM@255}} {{ckDmNEFcwW8@755}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@600}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@819}}
- **Even so, he doesn't recommend doing cardio only through weight training. Add an activity you like (swimming, hiking)** — P; 1 video, 2020; confidence: low (he himself did only weights then) {{ckDmNEFcwW8@691}} {{ckDmNEFcwW8@755}}
- **AMRAP compound sets (30-40 rep squats) are C tier: limited by heart/lungs, hard on joints, use sparingly. 3 sets of paused squats beat 30 min of treadmill for leg size and strength** — O; 2 videos, 2021 → 2024; confidence: medium {{BBqiphYfqMA@690}} {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@883}}
- **GPP for lifters: pull-ups, push-ups, ab work, plate carries, traps, bodyweight hyperextensions, sandbag carries/tosses, sled drags. Nothing that fries you for the next session** — R; 3 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: medium {{b4rMVrWFVEg@1383}} {{eaypnfdz3J0@821}} {{QfHfxLYRu88@472}}

### Athletes and fighters
- **Athletes' sport already covers conditioning, so lifting should target the fibre damage the sport leaves. You can do plenty of health cardio, but you can't be both a competitive endurance athlete and a high-level bodybuilder** — P; 2 videos, 2020 → 2021; confidence: medium {{3dLu3VQinL8@695}} {{cNGPCn9Uupc@1193}}
- **Combat conditioning (2022): 30-40 min sessions of intense pockets with rest, not long runs. Optional conditioning day of 35-40 min (mostly cardiovascular fatigue, safer than a third lift day). Boxing: 3-5 dashes of 3 min at max sustainable pace, progress by distance. MMA: 5-min dashes or sled drags. Strikers: 10x10 m sprints. Grapplers: rope climb (3x 5 m) or sled pull 15 m, pull-ups, dead hangs. Each day combines cardio, thoracic extension, abs, posterior chain and carries/pulls** — R; 2 videos, 2022; confidence: medium {{v-F3QHSM2hg@1939}} {{3JNwlqx3Z6w@842}} {{3JNwlqx3Z6w@2291}} {{3JNwlqx3Z6w@1498}} {{3JNwlqx3Z6w@2688}}

## How his view changed
- **No cardio → cardio advocate.** 2020-2021: he "does not do cardio in the conventional way", hadn't done cardio in 3 years, did all cardio with weights and never used ellipticals or steppers. Cardio was "not necessary", and 30 min lifting beats 30 min cardio {{ckDmNEFcwW8@0}} {{LCpa0h89TJM@380}} {{BBqiphYfqMA@504}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@1394}}. From 2023-11: cardio is "S tier", 5-15 min after every session, and it "makes you a better bodybuilder". He now does the stair machine, treadmill miles and carries {{SxqjF2oPasg@1952}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@887}} {{-OjJEzZTXtQ@1197}}. Later view: do cardio.
- **Calories framing.** 2020: cardio kills gains indirectly through calories, and skinny lifters should avoid it {{6sFaB3rAY1A@2008}} {{WyxeMHovPJ8@125}}. 2024: good cardio boosts gains {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@189}}.
- **Leg-day cardio.** 2022: avoid intense leg cardio on leg days {{9y2s6kyFkPc@2128}}. 2024-12: hard cardio on top of leg training boosts recovery and conditioning {{-OjJEzZTXtQ@1134}}. Later view: it's fine.
- **Stepper/jump rope.** 2020: prefer stepper or jump rope over treadmill {{ckDmNEFcwW8@441}}. 2021: never steppers {{BBqiphYfqMA@504}}. 2024: StairMaster is S tier and jump rope is poor for bodybuilders {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@1769}} {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@757}}.
- **Running.** 2020: uphill running "unmatched" for cardio {{ZKgM-tX_ODU@62}}. 2024: running C tier, long distance D {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@316}} {{ryWJV6yN3ZE@379}}. Sprints and fartlek stay highly rated.

## Misfiled but useful
- Nose breathing gives control of oxygen intake and builds stamina; coaches punished his mouth breathing with 15 push-ups {{cRLiTTlzXxk@253}}.
- Fat adds weight without vascularity, hurting endurance {{so6MbvvtM1w@1013}}. Former athletes from conditioned sports build lifting work capacity easily {{LCpa0h89TJM@192}}.
- Losing walking ability signals declining health and too much mass; keep calisthenics. Only squatting left his feet and ankles sore {{THV7B11oalk@1138}} {{XAcv427WvMw@1439}}.
- No-gym periods: keep work capacity with very high-rep, short-rest band good mornings and band squats {{9y2s6kyFkPc@126}}.
- Stiff-legged deadlift + row supersets are too taxing on lower back and cardio for 99% of people {{ckDmNEFcwW8@691}}.
- Swimming strokes are upper-body driven: leg joints lack propulsive range and big leg muscles use up to 3x more oxygen {{oytzIPcqih4@62}}.
