# Intensity & failure

**Bottom line**
- Intensity, meaning effort relative to your max, is in his later view the main driver of natural growth: "hard sets are king". Volume is a later lever, and pump, pain or screaming are not intensity.
- Train mostly at ~60-95% of 1RM, mostly 6-12 reps (all work 3-15, never 20+). Never do true 1RMs for hypertrophy, and don't fill a program with singles, doubles or triples.
- Take isolation, upper-body and other low-risk lifts to failure on every set. Keep 1-2 reps in reserve on squats, deadlifts, good mornings and other risky lower-body compounds. Failure is a calibration tool, not the goal; proximity to failure is the point.
- Failure means the muscle stops (organic). Cardio, lactic burn, pump or structure giving out (artificial or mechanical failure) don't count. Never actually miss a rep; a missed rep means the program is miscalculated.
- Sets must be repeatable. A set you can only recover from in a week or more is not a good hard set. Rep schemes that force sandbagging (5x5, 10x10, fixed reps) are bad; use evolving rep ranges.
- Since 2023-25 his preferred intensity technique is rest-pause (10-20 s, +2-5 reps, ~+10-50% volume), used on top of a quality set. Drop sets, forced reps and beyond-failure work are minor tools.

### Failure: definitions
- **The target is muscular (organic) failure, where the target muscle is the limiting factor; cardiovascular, mechanical/structural or pump-driven stops don't count** — P; ~14 videos, 2020 → 2022; confidence: high; novices mistake cardio fatigue for failure; getting winded, pumped or uncomfortable isn't failure; on cables you can keep moving with body English after the muscle stops; most failed lifts are structural (e.g. thoracic rounding); conditioning must never be the limiter; cardio or mechanical failure signals a bad window, either too light or so heavy form breaks at once {{UQguwgxTC9E@319}} {{T_ZMmSNmLbs@0}} {{K-iofHQxDsA@248}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@850}}

## How his view changed
- **Failure on every working set → selective failure.** 2020: every working set goes to failure, "no point not going to failure" {{UpbamnOFaZw@124}} {{MIHPylz3f7Q@300}}, though even then compounds kept a rep in the tank {{LYBdXCcmOO0@250}}. From 2022: failure on everything misallocates resources, "always training to failure is stupid", proximity is the point; failure only on isolation and upper body {{qrBLykvU_EE@188}} {{SxqjF2oPasg@503}} {{z2uzpl_smSg@2903}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@10175}}. Later view: selective.
- **RIR size on lower-body compounds.** 2020-11: exactly 1 RIR, never 2 or more {{JmowZI5YiAk@189}}. 2022-2024: 2 RIR on squats/deadlifts/SSB squats, 1-2 RIR on hinges {{yoROLGGUPXU@1660}} {{d1eLAd5WvIA@3755}} {{JW73oft8Q60@740}}. Later view: 1-2 (often 2).
- **Rest-pause.** 2020-21: skeptical, ranked last, leaks tonnage, a disguised three sets {{RqS8AxVE28I@697}} {{A55c7v1yMn4@375}}. 2023-25: his favourite technique, preferred over drop sets, worth +10-50% hyper-intense volume {{-OYtq2AHGX8@1833}} {{qvWxIzhT1QU@696}} {{waeSP7DBmig@160}}. Later view: pro rest-pause.
- **Upper rep limit.** 2020-02: 20-35 reps as the upper limit {{uTHCzF7Exck@760}}. 2020-09: ~15 {{p6WcFTOBq3s@436}}. 2022-12: 15-20 extremes {{z2uzpl_smSg@757}}. 2024: "never 20+", all work 3-15 {{bRKQIOYo2u4@7914}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@2535}}. 2025 adds that true failure is reachable at 12-15 {{ACFKXT44BzM@1063}}.
- **Personal skew from volume to intensity.** 2020-21: he loves volume, doesn't respond well to intensity, calls himself volume-skewed {{RaGX9rPQr4k@1294}} {{p-kR3RfGBHw@150}}. 2023-24: intensity drives growth and volume is a last resort; "these days intensity" {{7uBT8vp0lNw@382}} {{wBct3Pl8OY4@3680}}.
- **Hip thrusts.** 2020: never to failure {{du7JYCA2pa4@744}}. 2022: failure is safe, as long as ROM isn't cut {{vlnnV8BdWCE@845}}.
- **Mentzer/HIT tone.** 2020: HIT is a cult {{6sFaB3rAY1A@2448}}. 2022-24: keep Mentzer's hard-set understanding; Mentzer was right on intensity but went too far {{lmhW2W2UQto@1514}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@14205}}.
- **AMRAP.** 2020: he does pull-ups as AMRAPs and likes it {{np9LdwTPfNs@636}}. 2021: AMRAPs only for finishers and unweighted calisthenics {{P89o8FBSWDI@232}}. 2024: drop AMRAP sets from bodybuilding programs {{6p2x4kDDjzs@5100}}, although bodyweight pull-up AMRAPs still appear in 2023 {{7uBT8vp0lNw@447}}.

## Contradictions
- **RIR on compounds: sandbagging or smart?** He keeps 1-2 RIR on squats and hinges {{d1eLAd5WvIA@3755}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@4770}}, yet says leaving strength reps in reserve is a strength-athlete tool and bodybuilders who sandbag "train for strength" {{GNJkAcgUJ_0@5710}}, and that progressing 4-6 with RIR "is just sandbagging" {{ACFKXT44BzM@563}}. The 2025 remark targets low-rep HIT-style work, but the tension isn't resolved.
- **Percentages vs no percentages.** He recommends % of an estimated 1RM over RPE {{8ELQ2XZgpls@2320}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@540}}, but also says "no percentages, push every set to failure" {{HVD5j7hOobI@186}}, that he dislikes percentages {{y_lPAdWi4sU@318}}, and that he doesn't know his own 1RM {{UQguwgxTC9E@126}}.
- **Heavy/medium/light days.** He dislikes light/heavy days and volume/intensity days {{niD0UuwTUvg@318}} {{pvpf7Yuayco@106}}, but full body "needs heavy, medium and light days for each muscle" {{G7rVTseabFU@530}}. Possibly split-dependent.
- **Steady vs swinging intensity.** "Steady 85-95% beats swinging between 99% and 60%" {{7ZS-mGePFXw@480}} vs "intensity can swing (95% one day, 65% the next)" around an 85% average {{z2uzpl_smSg@820}}.
- **Can intensity exceed 100%?** No; forced reps only shift the window {{6sFaB3rAY1A@2510}}. Yes, via overloads like high block pulls {{T0QddSpwwdc@317}}.
- **NO and failure.** NO at ~15% "still reaches muscular failure" {{ZXFj-taKBis@64}} vs "never reach failure on NO, always ≥5 reps left" {{LNggSj0WBd8@502}}.
- **Meaning of "mechanical failure".** In 2020-21 it is structural breakdown that doesn't count as failure {{UpbamnOFaZw@124}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@2253}}. In 2023 he "defines failure as mechanical failure", meaning stop when body English appears {{0X2PxuT3tuE@380}}. Elsewhere it also means pump/lactic "artificial" failure {{fC5KHhv4_bI@64}}. This is a terminology clash, not a change of substance.
- **Flies to failure.** Avoid ranges that push flies to mechanical failure; failure there is pointless and risky {{eaXDVkHjsZQ@242}} vs flies with loads "to failure and beyond" {{WKy2C8JoltU@696}} and cable flies safe to push to failure {{eFhwWirR70o@739}}.
- **One set vs many.** One-set trainees are lazy {{6sFaB3rAY1A@2635}} vs one hard low-rep set can replace three chest exercises with better results {{BnzVYV9_IQc@190}} {{QL9Dh9CGOBQ@260}}.
- **Grinding.** Grinding drains and injures; never grind {{QiOh8SF-UxQ@128}} {{T_ZMmSNmLbs@381}} vs grinding is where the muscle works hardest and good lifts let you grind {{DAZRYoex8_w@1206}} {{CGhPZ9AxBJE@696}}. Likely compounds vs isolations, but he never states that.
- **Back-off loads.** He uses reduced-weight back-off sets {{-OYtq2AHGX8@1957}}, but also says he never takes weight off the bar because that is regression {{BJXFeQWGUuY@1389}}.
- Script flags checked and rejected as false alarms: "failure = one rep short / never fail a rep" variants (same position); low reps under 5-6 vs "low reps can be high intensity" (compatible); artificial vs organic failure lines (same idea); isolation-only failure vs rest-pause order (compatible); 0 RIR iso / 1-2 RIR compounds in 2022 vs 2023 (identical); pre-fatigue raising intensity (same); "no need to cycle intensity" vs rep/intensity myth (unrelated); "screaming isn't intensity" (same); 20-rep artificial failure (same); 1 RIR vs 0-1 RIR (same rule).

## Misfiled but useful
- Alsruhe's intensity categories, as he reports them: light 60-70%, medium 65-75%, heavy 70-80%, brutal 75-85%, in waves so two lifts are never brutal the same week {{OWbmOI3cuNs@1269}}
- In his view lifting in truly challenging ranges gives the biggest hormonal (testosterone) boost {{smAsuyqAQOo@188}}
- 10 sets of 1 heavy is quality tonnage but impractical and risky; 3 sets at 70-85% give similar tonnage more safely {{Vw9MhD5Amfw@380}}
