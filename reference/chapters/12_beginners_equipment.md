# 12. Beginners, training levels & equipment

This chapter covers how he defines novice, intermediate and advanced lifters, what he wants a novice to do, and his views on gear, straps and building or choosing a gym. On beginners, his stance is that "anything works", so a novice should play the long game: a minimal, written full-body program with isolation from day one, free weights and calisthenics, few hard sets, and adherence above all. On equipment, he says straps are a must whenever grip limits a pulling set and most other gear is optional. His gym advice moved from home-gym evangelism (2020-2021) to a hybrid home/commercial view (2023-2025).

## Why the novice phase matters

He treats the novice phase as a foundation for a long career rather than a race, and novice results as proof of nothing.

### BEG-01 · Anything works, so choose for the long term
- **Statement:** Almost any program works for novices. That should free them to pick what sets up the rest of their career, not justify random programs, and novice results prove nothing about a program, coach or channel.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** novices; it becomes "a game of inches" only later
- **Confidence:** high — ~18 videos, 2020→2025
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is the novice phase judged by skill, habits and base built rather than by how fast the numbers rise?
- **Sources:** {{QpTHtmI8nrU@0}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@442}} {{9mnLRQsrs9k@2514}} {{J9_udWdk6ZM@1050}}

### BEG-02 · Build a base and habits, not performance
- **Statement:** Novice status is short but you are an intermediate or advanced lifter for life, so use the novice period to build a base, prevent injuries and form habits rather than to chase numbers.
- **Numbers:** noob gains may be only 3-4% of your lifting time
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{d7441LfRS9s@63}} {{Tw8qA93Gf58@1196}} {{o9uDgF5J89U@313}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@0}}

### BEG-03 · Don't rush out of the novice phase
- **Statement:** Treat the novice phase like childhood and prolong it. The noob-gain "poison" and the "noob" stigma make people rush.
- **Numbers:** 1-2 years as a novice "doesn't matter" over a 20-year career; 6-12 months costs nothing; strength novice status can last up to 2 years
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — see BEG-10 (2020-04 "no more than 4-6 months" → 2020-11/2021 prolong to 1-2 years → 2024 done within a year for many)
- **Sources:** {{gWdAfpJIXbc@497}} {{9mnLRQsrs9k@916}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1006}} {{LxGdGChvk2c@3951}}

### BEG-04 · Noob gains are a quantity, not a window
- **Statement:** Noob gains are an amount of development, not a time window that closes. A bad program wastes time but not potential.
- **Numbers:** they last ~6 months to ~2 years; 6-8 months on machines is followed by fast growth after switching to compounds
- **Scope:** anyone untrained without a hormonal deficiency
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{xe1s_--iA98@0}} {{xe1s_--iA98@126}} {{d7441LfRS9s@127}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@660}}

### BEG-05 · Novices need only the bare minimum
- **Statement:** Novices are built to grow from very little, so do the bare minimum and don't add volume you don't need. "Optimal" means adding complexity only once the simpler thing stops working.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** novices; minimalism would hurt an advanced lifter like him

- **Status:** current

## Defining training levels

He classifies lifters by experience, programming ability and overload options, never by strength standards.

### BEG-06 · Novice is about experience, not strength numbers
- **Statement:** Novice status depends on experience, not strength. Strength standards are worthless for classifying lifters.
- **Numbers:** he dislikes the 225/315/405 x5 rule, though any healthy male should eventually reach it; some bench 225 lb after two weeks, others take two years; he knew a near-4-plate bencher (5-plate squat, 6-plate deadlift) who was "still a novice"

## Frequency, session length and volume

Novices lack work capacity, so he keeps their frequency moderate, sessions short and hard sets few, but not easy.

### BEG-11 · Three full-body days a week by default
- **Statement:** The novice default is 3 full-body days a week, 48 h apart (Mon/Wed/Fri); 2-3 days works.
- **Numbers:** 4 days is the high end; 5-6 days is "very hard to justify"; 2024: "tremendous results" on 2-3 days of ~1 h; floating rest days are for advanced lifters
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** high — ~14 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** contradicted — elsewhere he prefers "full body every day" with one heavy compound per day and says novices can recover from daily calisthenics, while also saying full body needs programming knowledge novices lack
- **Check:** Does the novice train 2-3 (at most 4) full-body days a week?
- **Sources:** {{4aThavRpYLw@250}} {{gpFdh0eBMGc@375}} {{G7rVTseabFU@720}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@1975}}

### BEG-12 · Build frequency before sets, sets before weight

## Program structure and content

His novice template: a written full-body program with supersets, 2-3 compounds a day, isolation for the rest, every movement pattern, and no powerlifting bias.

### BEG-23 · Novices need written, rigid structure
- **Statement:** Novices need a written, fixed structure; flexibility is earned. They can't be trusted with lift rotations or optional exercises, and should start on an established program because they lack the data to program for themselves.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** novices; training by template or instinct comes later
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{nWHewU0tAwQ@160}} {{4aThavRpYLw@1508}} {{43F9unmSc_I@377}} {{yZC3Nsmmzb8@8488}}

### BEG-24 · Leave some room for choice and mistakes
- **Statement:** Within that structure, leave some room for choice (rep ranges rather than fixed numbers, a small choice of lifts) and for first mistakes, which can't be catastrophic. His novice program is "deliberately open-handed".
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** novices

### BEG-25 · Full body is the best novice split
- **Statement:** Full body is the best novice split; a novice wanting 3 days is better served by full body than by half a PPL. No gentleman splits.

## Reps, loading and progression

Novices start light, build skill before load, and learn overload methods beyond adding weight.

### BEG-40 · Start light and accumulate quality reps
- **Statement:** Start light, even with the empty bar, and accumulate quality reps without struggling or compromising the next session. Repeat a hard weight before adding more; no PR is worth an injury.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{_4wWgin1dVY@317}} {{9mnLRQsrs9k@2885}} {{QKpAtCK6oPQ@563}}

### BEG-41 · Fixed schemes first, evolving ranges on accessories
- **Statement:** Fixed schemes (5x5, 3x5) are fine at first; use evolving ranges on accessories, then spread them. If every lift uses evolving ranges, tonnage collapses when all hit their low point at once.
- **Numbers:** evolving ranges 6-10, 8-12; turning a 3x3 into a 3x3-5 takes ~2 weeks to learn
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** contradicted — "evolving ranges are not suitable for beginners" vs "teach them early so they become second nature"
- **Sources:** {{65rLZe9cz_0@63}} {{65rLZe9cz_0@502}} {{2QnD9k0SbwA@0}} {{ulwUVDK7RUk@314}}

### BEG-42 · Use rep ranges where form stays clean
- **Statement:** Novices should use higher reps in a range where form stays clean; back work that loads the lower back and hamstrings needs at least 6-10 reps.
- **Numbers:** ≥6-10 reps; 3x6 rows get butchered; 4x8 squats are risky because of grinding and ugly reps
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{QKpAtCK6oPQ@438}} {{4aThavRpYLw@631}}

### BEG-43 · Early bar progress is mostly neural
- **Statement:** Early strength gains are mostly neural and technical, not muscle.
- **Numbers:** 5-10 lb per session for a long time; +10 lb/week is mostly neural; 60 → 200 lb squat in year one is "a given"; adding 200 lb in 6 months is pointless
- **Scope:** novices
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{k3O630Nz4B8@126}} {{AeIKc3A2FiY@250}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@610}} {{gWdAfpJIXbc@247}}

## Exercise selection for novices

Novice-specific choices; general exercise selection is in ch. 5 and body-part detail in ch. 6.

### BEG-47 · Free weights and bodyweight before machines
- **Statement:** Novices should learn on free weights and bodyweight; machines, cables and the Smith are for advanced lifters. Novices on machines "stay small for years", and a machine-and-cable novice program is a bad sign.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** novices; exception from 2024: the Smith vertical press, which handles stabilisation

### EQP-53 · How to build the platform
- **Statement:** Plan on paper and do a mock assembly; buy plywood in person (online stock is warped). - **Numbers:** plan 1-2 months ahead; top wood ~3x the price; glue dries ~40 min; swapping a damaged piece ~$30
