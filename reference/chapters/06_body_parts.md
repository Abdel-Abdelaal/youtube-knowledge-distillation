# 6. Body-part playbook

This chapter goes through his rules one body part at a time: what each muscle needs, his rep and frequency numbers, and the exercises he prefers for it. His main point is that every muscle you want big has to be trained directly. For naturals, compounds leave the arms, forearms, abs, calves, neck, side and rear delts and the triceps long head small, while quads, glutes and hamstrings can grow mostly from compounds. His latest prescriptions (2023-2024) are more moderate than his 2020 high-volume "nucleus overload" (NO) phase: weighted, progressively overloaded isolation in roughly the 6-15 rep range.

## Chest

He treats the chest as one muscle built by a press you actually feel. Bench pressing is optional, and dips and ring push-ups do just as well.

### CHE-01 · No separate upper chest; build the whole chest
- **Statement:** There is no separate "upper chest" to chase; bench and dips develop it, and people who obsess over it usually have no chest. Splitting the chest into regions is an "isolationist mindset".
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters; novices especially shouldn't worry about it. Multi-height cable flies are gimmicky.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{fycepPpxN_k@444}} {{WKy2C8JoltU@883}} {{wLx5BRx8AaU@100}} {{62OBPecQPnM@64}}

### CHE-02 · A stubborn chest needs a press you feel
- **Statement:** Grip, arch and dominant delts or triceps can steal bench work ("225 on the bar isn't 225 on the chest"). If you don't feel a press in your chest, switch to push-ups, dumbbells or dips for a while, or superset flies or band work between bench sets.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Lifters whose chest lags despite a decent bench.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** If the chest lags, is the main press one the lifter actually feels in the chest (or has it been swapped)?
- **Sources:** {{K4UBm1dV6WM@499}} {{HrUPp573ki0@771}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@385}} {{vLAhljbROlk@0}}

### CHE-03 · Bench press is not mandatory
- **Statement:** Dips, push-ups and ring push-ups can replace the bench with no other change to the program. Choose by what agrees with you, and swap the bench if it doesn't hit your chest or keeps injuring you. His chest first grew from home push-ups, and by 2024 he had dropped the bench himself.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters with a muscle goal; not powerlifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Eh0fnXIyDMc@125}} {{k9_dDbGLFZQ@498}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@821}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@1205}}

### CHE-04 · Weighted dips are top tier
- **Statement:** Weighted dips ("squat of the upper body") are top tier for chest, triceps and shoulders. They give more pec and shoulder stretch than bench and may stress the pecs more. The shoulder risk is slightly higher, with no safety net.
- **Numbers:** He does 3 plates + 2×10 lb for 5.
- **Scope:** Those whose shoulders and ribcage tolerate them; growing young men should watch for sternum or rib pain.
- **Confidence:** high — ~9 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — in 2020 he would not recommend dips over bench; later he ranks them equal or better for hypertrophy.
- **Sources:** {{MIHPylz3f7Q@1489}} {{cUkWvnY8AJI@813}} {{K4UBm1dV6WM@313}} {{wBct3Pl8OY4@3304}}

### CHE-05 · Ring push-ups are among the best chest builders
- **Statement:** Ring push-ups, especially weighted (backpack) and decline with the rings to the armpits, free the wrists and elbows, have no floor limit, let the scapulae retract, and bias the upper chest when declined. He ranks them first in his hypertrophy tier list.
- **Numbers:** About 5 sets of deep ring push-ups.
- **Scope:** All levels; load them once bodyweight is easy.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{9M7QVlvFdQY@1600}} {{43F9unmSc_I@1697}} {{K4UBm1dV6WM@1762}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@5111}}

### CHE-06 · Push-ups aren't only for novices
- **Statement:** Loaded, or placed at the end of a press day, push-ups stay effective for advanced lifters.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All levels.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020: high-rep push-ups too easy for the advanced and weighted push-ups more risk than reward; 2024: he regrets dropping push-ups because "YouTube said they were for noobs".
- **Sources:** {{9M7QVlvFdQY@1340}} {{vyKzL85QwY4@65}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1508}} {{K4UBm1dV6WM@499}}

### CHE-07 · Flies and ring work add chest volume while sparing the shoulders
- **Statement:** The less the shoulder is taxed, the more chest you can stimulate. Use flies, band flies and ring push-ups to grow the chest past dominant shoulders.
- **Numbers:** Band flies 8-12 between bench warm-ups; cable flies 3-4x12; ~5 sets deep ring push-ups.
- **Scope:** Especially shoulder-dominant lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{xoLo7fptNA8@377}} {{vLAhljbROlk@63}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@6535}} {{K4UBm1dV6WM@1762}}

### CHE-08 · Flies are finishers with a bent-arm stretch
- **Statement:** Use flies for high reps and supersets, not heavy single sets. Top picks: low-to-high cable fly, bent-arm pec deck, fly press, barrel press, and ring flies (build up from neutral ring push-ups first). Straight-arm DB flies and the straight-arm pec deck are mediocre and risky.
- **Numbers:** Low-to-high cable fly 4x15.
- **Scope:** Accessory after the main press.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020: "scrap the pec deck"; later the bent-arm pec deck is a top choice.
- **Sources:** {{6kRRzKx7lKs@722}} {{cUkWvnY8AJI@561}} {{0X2PxuT3tuE@192}} {{-OYtq2AHGX8@826}}

### CHE-09 · One king press, maybe a second, maybe one isolation
- **Statement:** The chest needs one big press, perhaps a second press or accessory and perhaps one isolation; more is pointless unless you specialize in chest.
- **Numbers:** 1 main press + 0-1 secondary press + 0-1 isolation.
- **Scope:** Non-specialists.
- **Confidence:** medium — 4 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{k9_dDbGLFZQ@372}} {{uRWgz3nhqds@314}} {{6sFaB3rAY1A@570}} {{BiVfpZEchws@62}}

### CHE-10 · Rotate horizontal presses, including shoulder-friendly variations
- **Statement:** Use more than one horizontal press over time, including variations that unload the shoulder: paused, close-grip, slingshot, dumbbell, football/cambered bar, pin or Spoto press. Replace one bench day per week with a paused variation.
- **Numbers:** One paused-variation day per week.
- **Scope:** Intermediate and up.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{IahVc0kbGOU@251}} {{xoLo7fptNA8@186}} {{cUkWvnY8AJI@1628}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@819}}

### CHE-11 · The dumbbell press is underrated
- **Statement:** Start with dumbbells if you don't feel the bench in your chest; dumbbells beat a barbell for incline.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Especially lifters who don't feel the barbell bench.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** contradicted — in 2020 he also said shifting flat-bench volume to dumbbells likely costs gains.
- **Sources:** {{4ORCb_i7MxY@747}} {{cUkWvnY8AJI@1879}} {{PYmr91aELnE@726}} {{fSOiGuUPqBc@881}}

### CHE-12 · The incline press is a chest/shoulder hybrid
- **Statement:** The steeper the incline, the more shoulder. Treat the incline as a priority lift and don't stack it right after a heavy flat press. Dumbbell or Smith incline and weighted incline ring push-ups are his favourites.
- **Numbers:** 3x8-10 or 6-10.
- **Scope:** All levels.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — early on he disliked the barbell incline (front-delt heavy, injury-prone, "incline for upper chest" is bad advice); by 2023-2024 DB/Smith incline are favourites.
- **Sources:** {{4ORCb_i7MxY@58}} {{cUkWvnY8AJI@1253}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@1455}} {{jUMmu583w34@540}}

### CHE-13 · Low-value chest movements
- **Statement:** Floor press (no stretch; 3 years of DB floor press gave him a poor chest), plate/Svend press, standing cable press, pullovers for chest, chain dips, Hindu and one-arm push-ups, finger push-ups.
- **Numbers:** —

### CHE-14 · Pair the press with a complement
- **Statement:** Horizontal press then vertical press (his favourite bench day), bench supersetted with pullovers, or a heavy horizontal press then ring push-ups.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Press days.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{hT4zgNcMOBY@752}} {{vyKzL85QwY4@510}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@3513}} {{AFK9x4EZh2Q@127}}

### CHE-15 · A big chest doesn't need a 3-plate bench
- **Statement:** Long-armed lifters bench less but may build bigger chests.
- **Numbers:** 3-plate (315 lb) bench is not a requirement.
- **Scope:** All, especially long-armed lifters.

### CHE-16 · Uneven chest: fix the bar path, add one-arm DB work

- **Confidence:** low — 1 video, 2020

### CHE-17 · Strong chest, weak shoulders: bench once a week
- **Statement:** A chest-dominant lifter with lagging shoulders should cut bench to one low-rep session a week (see SHO-06).

- **Scope:** Chest-dominant, shoulder-lagging lifters.

## Back, lats and traps

The upper back is where he sees the most untapped potential in naturals. A complete back needs a hinge, a vertical pull and a horizontal pull. Lats come from pull-ups and pullovers, not deadlifts, and traps come from heavy load held in the hands.

### BAK-01 · The upper back has the most growth potential in naturals
- **Statement:** The upper back is the most underdeveloped area in naturals and the slowest to mature; the best natural backs belong to lifters in their 30s-40s. After 10 years of training, adding upper-back volume made his grow "like weeds".
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Natural lifters, all levels.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{4aThavRpYLw@1385}} {{3JBjFKI4PmQ@0}} {{qDH088UUiMA@13}} {{4t72r8Q0vXQ@1130}}

### BAK-02 · The upper back tolerates the most volume and frequency
- **Statement:** The upper back is a "glutton for volume"; train it almost every session. Its limits are the shoulder, biceps and elbow tendons and lower-back fatigue, so favour rows and pulls that spare the lower back. 4 sets of pulldowns won't build big lats.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Intermediate and up.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{3oZD6Hk525A@0}} {{eaXDVkHjsZQ@751}} {{THV7B11oalk@1576}} {{JnWWva91QUM@1900}}

### BAK-03 · Push/pull balance: from "pull more" to "many need more front"
- **Statement:** He now sees many lifters as back-dominant from the "pull twice as much as you push" advice; they need less back and more front volume. Everyone should still do plenty of back work.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Judged per physique.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020: do more pulling than pushing; 2024: many reviewees need more pushing.
- **Sources:** {{CFlRdyLiWOo@313}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@2500}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@2347}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@1335}}

### BAK-04 · A complete back needs a hinge, a vertical pull and a horizontal pull
- **Statement:** The back is several areas, each needing its own pattern; vertical and horizontal pulls are not interchangeable. Deadlifts alone give a thick "fridge" back with no width; pull-ups were the only difference between his flat and his detailed back at the same 210 lb. 6-7 back exercises is "bro-split garbage".
- **Numbers:** Masterplan: 1 hinge + 1 vertical + 1 horizontal pull on different days, then +1 pull or shrug; a new hinge alternates weekly (A: deadlift 3x3, B: RDL 3x8-12); 1-2 exercises per area.
- **Scope:** All lifters wanting a full back.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does each week include a hip hinge, a vertical pull and a horizontal pull?
- **Sources:** {{3IMgRqcRN4Y@812}} {{HrUPp573ki0@1803}} {{7uBT8vp0lNw@1198}} {{DA7QvIAyptw@1261}}

### BAK-05 · Open the back day with a heavy pull
- **Statement:** Open with a floor pull/hinge, a heavy row or a weighted pull-up, never a light pulldown or machine. Don't burn out on the opener; it doubles as a warm-up.
- **Numbers:** 2021: ~90% of people should open pull day with a pull from the floor.
- **Scope:** Back or pull days.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** changed — 2020-2021: a floor pull; later a heavy row or weighted pull-up is also allowed.
- **Sources:** {{pey1Ni6HPlY@476}} {{qDH088UUiMA@76}} {{bJ7gfILR6RA@628}} {{BiVfpZEchws@187}}

### BAK-06 · Back thickness comes from hinges as well as rows
- **Statement:** The upper back works isometrically to stabilize hinge loads, so heavy hinges can replace one or two back exercises; the deadlift's weighted stretch at a 45° torso grows the mid back. Pull-ups alone leave you "flat as paper" from the side.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~4 videos, 2021→2025
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{qDH088UUiMA@752}} {{4t72r8Q0vXQ@251}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@3609}} {{waeSP7DBmig@830}}

### BAK-07 · The deadlift's role for the back shrank
- **Statement:** The deadlift is now only mediocre for lats, builds a thick back with no V-taper, and ignores the small upper-back muscles.
- **Numbers:** 2020: it gave ~85% of his back thickness.
- **Scope:** Hypertrophy goal.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020: "mandatory" for the back; 2022-2024: mediocre for lats, "fridge back".
- **Sources:** {{cGzlMRCxvxo@1844}} {{Cbq-vxVba4k@190}} {{4t72r8Q0vXQ@568}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@190}}

### BAK-08 · "Legless" pulls as the bread and butter of upper-back growth
- **Statement:** Cable, seal, DB and chest-supported rows and hanging pulls should carry upper-back growth; he expects bigger backs from this approach.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Upper-back hypertrophy.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2021→2022
- **Status:** changed — in 2021 he still called deadlifts and pull-ups the bread and butter.
- **Sources:** {{dVssxcEh1UQ@1592}} {{dVssxcEh1UQ@1727}} {{1MOLCv6ojgw@200}} {{qDH088UUiMA@497}}

### BAK-09 · Pull-ups are the king of lat and upper-back builders
- **Statement:** Weighted pull-ups when possible; nearly every natural with big lats built them with pull-ups. They decompress the spine, barely tax the CNS, and pair well with presses and small-muscle work. Weighted beat bodyweight, which beat machines.
- **Numbers:** Add weight after 6-8 clean reps (2021) or once you reach 20+ bodyweight reps (2024).
- **Scope:** All naturals who can do them.
- **Confidence:** high — ~20 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{OATtf3kPtdI@875}} {{6YWa3qOu6BY@441}} {{ABTeMrXdgI4@540}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@6664}}

### BAK-10 · Deadlifts and rows don't build lats; vertical pulls and pullovers do
- **Statement:** At a 500-520 lb deadlift he had "essentially no lats" and wrongly blamed his insertions; the tonnage went to the rhomboids and mid traps. Slow pull-ups and pullovers then grew them. Only a few people with particular leverages grow lats from deadlifts.
- **Numbers:** 500-520 lb deadlift, no lats.
- **Scope:** Most lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{RaGX9rPQr4k@914}} {{QwoBxHI7NBU@187}} {{kkbfHkyQ_q0@2340}} {{J9Y9VZhnZ5M@66}}

### BAK-11 · Do pullovers every week, short of failure
- **Statement:** Pullovers transformed his lats and ribcage and are his favourite lift; put them in every program, on push or upper days. Dumbbell or machine is best; the cable version is a nightmare to set up. They supplement pull-ups and pulldowns but can't replace them.
- **Numbers:** Start 1x/week, then medium-heavy 2-3x/week at 1-2 RIR; 2024: about 95 lb, slow negative, dumbbell nearly to the floor.
- **Scope:** All lifters; not for most women (barrel-chest look).
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Are pullovers done at least weekly, stopping 1-2 reps short of failure?
- **Sources:** {{J9Y9VZhnZ5M@383}} {{dVssxcEh1UQ@2489}} {{Cbq-vxVba4k@1132}} {{ZRNSc-xBVnc@1955}}

### BAK-12 · Pullover rep ranges
- **Statement:** Keep pullovers in moderate reps; very high reps shift the work to the triceps long head.
- **Numbers:** 2020: 4-12, preferably 5-7, 3-8 sets; 2022 NO: a 25RM weight, 3-4x7-10, 2-3x/week for a year; 2023: 3x8-12; 2024: 6-10 experienced, 8-12 novices, 12-20 too high.
- **Scope:** Lats and ribcage.
- **Confidence:** high — 5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — preferred range moved from 5-7 (2020) to 6-10/8-12 (2024).
- **Sources:** {{ZXFj-taKBis@754}} {{LNggSj0WBd8@2463}} {{ZDWFfYLYVNs@605}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@4294}}

### BAK-13 · Pull-up prescriptions for lats
- **Statement:** Big lats come from heavy weighted pull-ups plus volume, not light pulldowns. Bodyweight pull-ups can be done daily with varied grips.
- **Numbers:** Weighted pull-ups 3-5, 4-8 or 5-8; weighted chin-ups ~6-10, 1x/week, not fully extended (biceps protection); 2020 NO: 40-100 pull-ups a day, 6 days a week.
- **Scope:** Lats.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{3oZD6Hk525A@89}} {{2edX06SQDnU@125}} {{6p2x4kDDjzs@1386}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@5639}}

### BAK-14 · Lat pulldowns: from "replace them" to a top pull-up substitute
- **Statement:** Some pulldowns are top tier and the pulldown is the best pull-up substitute, worth rotating in even if you can do pull-ups. Best: close-grip neutral, wide supinated, and the diagonal/iliac pulldown heavy for 8-10 to failure. Wide pronated and behind-the-neck are only decent; the standing cable pulldown is bad.
- **Numbers:** Diagonal pulldown 8-10 to failure; women's program 4x10-15.
- **Scope:** All levels.
- **Confidence:** high — ~20 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020-2021: replace pulldowns with pull-ups, never as the main lift or opener; 2022-2024: some pulldowns are top tier.
- **Sources:** {{eobG2o3Ww4M@500}} {{Cbq-vxVba4k@567}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@1729}} {{jUMmu583w34@1390}}

### BAK-15 · Pull-up grips have different flavours; vary them
- **Statement:** Chin-up = lower/front lat plus biceps and core; pronated = more upper/mid back; neutral = best stretch, most shoulder-friendly; wide = lower lat/V-taper, more traps. The sternum (Gironda) pull-up is the "best of the best" but needs about 10 chest-to-bar reps first.
- **Numbers:** ~10 chest-to-bar reps before sternum pull-ups.
- **Scope:** All levels.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Cbq-vxVba4k@378}} {{DQo0DGn_wI4@400}} {{C4CnYbHCCGo@756}} {{mMA3KF3qmlQ@378}}

### BAK-16 · Lat mind-muscle connection comes from stretch and slow tempo
- **Statement:** Very slow, fully stretched pull-ups, one-arm cable rows and wide snatch-grip rows to the sternum build the lat connection. Wide overhand grip is best for him; Pendlay rows lack the bottom stretch.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Lifters who don't feel their lats.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2020
- **Status:** current
