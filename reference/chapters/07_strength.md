# 7. Strength & the big lifts

This chapter covers how strength relates to size, where strength work sits in a bodybuilding program, his rep schemes for the main lifts, max testing, carryover between lifts, strength standards, powerlifting and powerbuilding, and strength for other athletes. His stance: for a bodybuilder, strength is a means, not the goal. You must get stronger to grow, but chasing 1RMs, big-three numbers or standards costs size. His own lift numbers over time are in ch. 1 and Appendix C and are not repeated here.

## Strength vs size

Strength and size are linked but separate qualities. Within one person they move together, but between people leverages, skill and bodyweight blur the link.

### STR-01 · Strength and size are different qualities
- **Statement:** Strength and size are different qualities with some crossover, and training for one is not training for the other. Two lifters who add the same amount to a lift end up different sizes, and the one focused on volume and tonnage is bigger.
- **Numbers:** example: two lifters taking a squat from 100 to 300 lb, or reaching a 250 lb bench
- **Scope:** all lifters, and especially bodybuilders choosing what to train for
- **Confidence:** high — ~15 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is the program built to add muscle (volume, tonnage) rather than to raise lift numbers for their own sake?
- **Sources:** {{-1dECrWYeFk@0}} {{k8k1PycHKDI@340}} {{V4aBuYpdY34@300}} {{z2uzpl_smSg@1702}}

### STR-02 · You can't grow without getting stronger
- **Statement:** Strength is not the bodybuilder's goal, but you will have to get stronger to grow. Size and strength both rise, one faster than the other.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** bodybuilders at every level
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does the program expect loads or reps on its lifts to go up over time?
- **Sources:** {{k8k1PycHKDI@0}} {{k8k1PycHKDI@220}} {{JgAoGDmM4n4@497}} {{jUMmu583w34@1030}}

### STR-03 · Within a person, strength reflects muscle
- **Statement:** For one person on long-practised lifts, strength gains reflect muscle gains (bigger legs mean a bigger squat), and small size increases bring surprisingly large strength increases. Between people, leverages dominate, so you can't read strength from size.
- **Numbers:** legs from 20 to 25 in means the squat went up; between people: 20 in legs squatting ~400 vs 22 in legs squatting 500
- **Scope:** comparing yourself over time; not for comparing different lifters
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** changed — 2020: he rejected the idea that strength can rise while muscles shrink, and said size can't rise while strength stays flat; 2022: citing Candito losing size while his total rose, he said you can trade one for the other. Later view: correlated within a person, but tradeable.
- **Sources:** {{VVckf4vyYPE@260}} {{TIsnQ03FegQ@338}} {{LxGdGChvk2c@3139}} {{LxGdGChvk2c@3076}}

### STR-04 · Strength athletes train functions, bodybuilders train muscles
- **Statement:** A powerlifter squats to train his squat, and a bodybuilder squats to train quads, glutes and hamstrings. In his bucket picture, powerlifters drain the muscle buckets to fill the big nervous-system bucket, and bodybuilders do the reverse.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** choosing how to perform and judge a lift
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2021→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{w4-2c3iWvDQ@1107}} {{PuIp9cGKMHo@1331}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1875}}

### STR-05 · Specialising in one costs the other
- **Statement:** No program maximises both size and strength. At equal bodyweight bodybuilders are weaker and powerlifters stronger, which he puts down to training, not genetics, and no natural bodybuilder holds powerlifting records.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** anyone tempted to chase both goals at once
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024

### STR-06 · Bodybuilders have "accidental strength"
- **Statement:** Bodybuilders are weaker than powerlifters but much stronger than average. Powerlifters are small for their strength and imbalanced: big legs, chest, front delts and traps, with small arms and parts of the back.
- **Numbers:** —

### STR-07 · Strong lifters need a higher threshold before size shows
- **Statement:** Lifters gifted for strength have to get much stronger before size shows. They should use that strength to add sets and reps, not to max out. For a naturally weak lifter like him, strength gains turn into size.
- **Numbers:** e.g. a strength-gifted lifter's OHP of 195 lb for reps vs his 135
- **Scope:** strength-gifted lifters; naturally weak lifters (the opposite case)
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2023

### STR-08 · Prefer rep strength over 1RM strength
- **Statement:** A 10RM tracks muscle mass better than a 1RM, which mostly reflects the nervous system, so bodybuilders should focus on rep strength. 1RM calculators built from strength athletes don't apply to them.
- **Numbers:** 10RM vs 1RM
- **Scope:** bodybuilders
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2023

## Neural adaptation vs muscle

Early strength gains on a lift are mostly skill. Once the skill is learned, strength added at the same technique and bodyweight is muscle.

### STR-10 · Neural gains are front-loaded and capped
- **Statement:** Neural and technical gains on a new lift mostly arrive early. After that you are at 95-100% technical mastery, and further strength at the same technique is muscle. Saying all strength gains could be neural is "wrong and demoralizing".
- **Numbers:** ~6 months of weekly practice (2020); "a month to a year" (2021); 95-100% technical mastery
- **Scope:** any lift practised regularly, past the learning phase
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current — the window was refined from ~6 months (2020) to a month to a year (2021), with the same conclusion
- **Check:** Once a lift has been practised for ~6-12 months, is further strength at the same technique and bodyweight treated as muscle gain?
- **Sources:** {{7XqE7GhDsgc@938}} {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@519}} {{ISLtO-xJbYo@252}} {{AeIKc3A2FiY@270}}

### STR-11 · Learning or rotating lifts gives artificial progress
- **Statement:** Progress on a new or rotated lift is mostly relearning, not muscle. A box lets you think you are stronger at the squat. A second +100 lb on the bench brings far more muscle than the first.
- **Numbers:** his SSB box squat 185→365 lb in under a year, mostly relearning
- **Scope:** judging progress on new variations
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@399}} {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@450}} {{k3O630Nz4B8@188}} {{bTin_tBGoVo@395}}

### STR-12 · Strength is largely skill
- **Statement:** Strength is largely the skill of recruiting motor units in a pattern, and it needs a fresh nervous system. High-frequency programs (daily squatting, Bulgarian or Wisconsin-style) raise one lift without much muscle, and he never prescribes them for size.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** specialised strength programs
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{bJK7L5fb-eI@125}} {{ISLtO-xJbYo@252}} {{u10c43TRDb0@880}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@250}}

### STR-13 · Big strength jumps without visible change are a red flag
- **Statement:** Past the novice stage, a big percentage strength gain with no muscle gain is biologically impossible. Strength that jumps while nothing visibly changes is a red flag.
- **Numbers:** +100 lb on chin-ups, dips and OHP should visibly change you
- **Scope:** past novice; judging your own or others' claims
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{xn8RN9807Ns@129}} {{U1zVxzMVM2E@2420}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1139}}

### STR-14 · Strength detrains faster than size
- **Statement:** Time off costs strength and work capacity well before it costs size. Presses, especially the OHP, regress quickly when not practised.
- **Numbers:** a month without a gym: legs barely changed, ~60 lb lost on lifts
- **Scope:** breaks and returns to training
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{VVckf4vyYPE@815}} {{D1_tXh-Kp9Y@822}} {{AeIKc3A2FiY@340}}

## Bodyweight, fat and leverage

Bodyweight makes you stronger, but strength that comes from fat is leverage, not progress.

### STR-15 · Fat-driven strength isn't real progress
- **Statement:** Mass moves mass, but strength gained by adding mostly fat raises absolute strength, not pound-for-pound strength. It is leverage, and it disappears on a cut.
- **Numbers:** +20 lb on the bench with +20 lb bodyweight = no real progress; +100 lb of mostly fat raises absolute strength only
- **Scope:** judging progress during bulks
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is strength progress judged at a stable bodyweight (or alongside lean gain), not credited when it only follows fat gain?
- **Sources:** {{VVckf4vyYPE@322}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@242}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1580}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@1139}}

### STR-16 · Bodyweight helps presses far more than hinges
- **Statement:** Extra bodyweight helps pressing through leverage (a shorter range of motion) and helps hinges much less. In a deficit, hinges barely suffer while bench and OHP drop a lot.
- **Numbers:** "put 50 lb on your frame, add ~100 lb to your bench"; he benched 315 at ~220 lb, 335 when fatter, and lost it when the fat came off
- **Scope:** pressing vs hinging lifts across bulks and cuts
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{so6MbvvtM1w@902}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@616}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@668}} {{e3KPnpnfbxc@316}}

### STR-17 · Pure strength needs mass, preferably muscle
- **Statement:** For pure strength, carry as much mass as possible, preferably muscle. Getting really strong means letting bodyweight rise, so staying very lean ("otter mode") means never getting really strong or reaching full size.
- **Numbers:** 400 lb at 10% body fat beats 400 lb at 20%
- **Scope:** lifters whose goal is maximal strength
- **Confidence:** medium — ~2 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{mUAHGlseeyk@502}} {{HOgwZO70z_M@878}}

### STR-18 · You can get stronger without gaining weight
- **Statement:** Strength can keep rising at a stable bodyweight. He uses his own lifts as proof.
- **Numbers:** all his lifts rose at ≤210 lb (2020); in 2024 he beat his old 240 lb lifts at 220 lb, except the bench
- **Scope:** lifters at stable bodyweight
- **Confidence:** medium — ~2 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{7XqE7GhDsgc@314}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@2385}}

### STR-19 · Pound-for-pound matters
- **Statement:** Relative strength matters. Wilks-type scoring favours light lifters, and without it big frames that bulk would dominate. Being lighter also makes calisthenics easier.
- **Numbers:** his handstand push-ups were easy at 160 lb, impossible at 220 lb
- **Scope:** comparing lifters; calisthenics
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{f0vdsC2yyhY@1325}} {{WmH326eoF04@1477}} {{ZN5ohmPPfjM@630}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@1770}}

### STR-20 · Leverages dominate lower-body numbers
- **Statement:** Long femurs or a long torso make squats hard and deadlifts easier, and arms past the knees would make a deadlift world-record holder. Tall lifters have more absolute strength and shouldn't compare themselves with short ones, but short lifters keep a squat advantage through range of motion. A bigger structure (wide hips, big joints) means more strength potential.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** comparing squat and deadlift numbers between people
- **Confidence:** high — ~7 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{aiVrEt5ziHQ@63}} {{3Mzk6FLh5UI@310}} {{Ox6TQAC9cW0@253}} {{3nyuEtmqL-I@1060}}

## Strength work inside a bodybuilding program

Strength work is a small, focused part of a bodybuilding program. It opens the session, is done as solo sets, and serves the tonnage. His later practice moved the heavy work to less risky movements.

### STR-21 · Strength work is a year-round pillar
- **Statement:** A small dose of heavy strength work is the backbone of hypertrophy training and stays in all year, not only in a block. It is little tonnage, but its high-intensity peaks drive progression.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** all bodybuilding programs
- **Confidence:** medium — ~10 videos, 2020→2021 (strongly stated early, less emphasised later)
- **Status:** changed — 2020-21: the backbone, "the one cornerstone you never cut"; 2024-25: high-bar squat and bench only 3-5 times a year, he "doesn't care about strength", and his heavy work moved to dips and weighted pull-ups. Later view: keep getting stronger, through less risky movements.
- **Check:** Does the program keep some heavy, low-rep work all year rather than only in phases?
- **Sources:** {{65rLZe9cz_0@251}} {{_2M6FhWuIvs@314}} {{0O0nG2jLadU@572}} {{-7bzbmL-x3Q@748}}

### STR-22 · Strength work opens the session
- **Statement:** Strength work comes first, and a day with zero strength work is bad, with occasional exceptions. Pre-activation must be light: an explosive opener taken short of failure (a kettlebell swing) primes you, while a leg-curl opener only fatigues. A small dose fires up the nervous system for the accessories.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** session structure
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does the heavy strength work come first in the session, after only light warm-ups?
- **Sources:** {{-banYSTnkq4@230}} {{zCo31HKOsIY@317}} {{62OBPecQPnM@440}} {{nqzVqvMvQ1s@1196}}

### STR-23 · Strength work is a minority of the tonnage
- **Statement:** Strength work is a small share of total tonnage, and the rest is hypertrophy work. Strength exists to drive tonnage. Switching back and forth between strength and hypertrophy phases is "like living with divorced parents".
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** bodybuilding programs
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is heavy strength work a minority of weekly tonnage, with most work in hypertrophy ranges?
- **Sources:** {{7ZS-mGePFXw@340}} {{7ZS-mGePFXw@90}} {{_2M6FhWuIvs@480}}

### STR-24 · Strength sets are solo sets
- **Statement:** Never superset or giant-set heavy squats, deadlifts or top sets. At most pair them with neck or calves. His bench depends on focus.
- **Numbers:** exception: bench and rows can be supersetted at 4x6-8
- **Scope:** heavy compound and top sets
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** contradicted — he also says the bench is the one lift you can get away with not treating as strict strength work, and he allows bench/row supersets, while elsewhere it needs full focus and no supersets
- **Check:** Are heavy squats, deadlifts and top sets performed as solo sets, not in supersets or giant sets?

- **Statement:** Plan how reps and sets evolve more carefully for strength work than for volume work, because it opens the session and affects everything after it.

### STR-26 · No strength blocks for bodybuilders
- **Statement:** Bodybuilders don't need strength blocks. "Build a strength base first" is a lie, because strength grows on a bodybuilding program anyway. Bodybuilders don't peak, so peaking practices don't transfer, and speed work at 40% 1RM is junk volume.

- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2023 (later view)

## Rep schemes for the main lifts

His rep ranges for the main strength lifts. A summary table follows the principles.

### STR-34 · Deadlift: few sets, never more than 5 reps
- **Statement:** Deadlift for 1-3 sets of 2-5 reps, never more than 5, and a single heavy set of 5 is often enough. 3x3 works very well if you leave reps in the tank.
- **Numbers:** 1-3 x 2-5; often 1x5; 3x3 with reps in reserve; 3x5 is a good volume for floor pulls; back-offs 4x3 at a comfortable triple or 3x3 submax
- **Scope:** heavy floor pulls, intermediate and up
- **Confidence:** high — ~14 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Are heavy deadlift sets kept at 5 reps or fewer, for only a few sets?
- **Sources:** {{t_RZT-AkjhE@127}} {{sCMGXCXKVjs@1130}} {{9M7QVlvFdQY@1150}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1131}}

### STR-35 · Novice deadlift: sets of 6, no high-rep maxes
- **Statement:** Novices can deadlift sets of 6. Don't do 12-rep maxes on the conventional deadlift, where cardio and structure give out before the muscles.
- **Numbers:** 2x6 (12 reps)
- **Scope:** beginners
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{4aThavRpYLw@314}} {{mbtAKsbDz9I@350}} {{8mGHG5C-EPE@440}}

### STR-36 · Deadlift rarely; let other hinges do the work
- **Statement:** His later practice is to deadlift from the floor rarely and get most posterior-chain volume from RDLs, good mornings and hyperextensions.
- **Numbers:** 2024: deadlifts about every 40 days, with a PR each time; ~95% of posterior-chain volume from RDLs, good mornings and hypers
- **Scope:** advanced lifters (his own practice)
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020-21: he pushed the deadlift hardest with 2-rep maxes, said they gave zero back pain and more progress than any other lift, and called a 2RM done for hypertrophy hypertrophy training; 2024: low-frequency deadlifts and no max testing. (His 2RMs also sit uneasily with STR-44; they are consistent only if a 2RM is not a 1RM.)
- **Sources:** {{mbtAKsbDz9I@330}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1810}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1822}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@1884}}

### STR-37 · Squat: 4-8 reps, never singles to triples
- **Statement:** Squat in the 4-8 rep range, never singles, doubles or triples, to avoid knee valgus and the back tipping forward. Ramping single sets of 5 to a top set beats 3 sets at the same intensity.
- **Numbers:** 4-8 reps; 3x4-8 on one heavy day; 3-6 or 3-8; ramp 1x5 at RPE 6, 7, 8
- **Scope:** lifters wanting big legs
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Are heavy squat sets kept at 4 reps or more?
- **Sources:** {{t_RZT-AkjhE@127}} {{cGzlMRCxvxo@968}} {{mbtAKsbDz9I@373}} {{YcHkXwsIOI8@316}}

### STR-38 · Bench: never below 4 reps
- **Statement:** Never bench below 4 reps, and keep to 4-8 if your shoulders are temperamental. Close-grip bench is his favourite variation and progressed very fast.
- **Numbers:** 3x4-6 flat barbell; 4x6-8; 8-10 fine; paused 6-10; Spoto press 8-12; close-grip 4x6-10
- **Scope:** bench and its variations; shoulder-friendly benching
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Are bench press sets kept at 4 reps or more?
- **Sources:** {{t_RZT-AkjhE@64}} {{jSUmn33lmlI@563}} {{D3mJh0ZA5nc@524}} {{6sFaB3rAY1A@1317}}

### STR-39 · Overhead press: low reps, never supersetted
- **Statement:** As strength work, the overhead press is done for low reps and never supersetted. 12-15 reps is too many when it opens the day, and 3x3 gives less volume than he wants.

## Max testing

Bodybuilders build strength and estimate it from reps. They don't test it.

### STR-44 · Never test 1RMs as a bodybuilder
- **Statement:** Maxing out is a powerlifting skill for peaking and brings no hypertrophy benefit. 1RMs don't even build strength (2-3RMs or 1 rep in reserve might), and every max-out uses up muscle-building capacity.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** bodybuilders
- **Confidence:** high — ~7 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does the program avoid 1RM testing?
- **Sources:** {{T_DyRlu3Y7I@317}} {{CTMwuqNsY9Q@498}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@763}} {{x9GNS5HBZ_4@1262}}

### STR-45 · Estimate your max from reps
- **Statement:** Estimate your max from rep performance and program by intensity. A double just under your max beats a single: double the tonnage, with less risk and fatigue.
- **Numbers:** e.g. 200x10; 485x2 vs 500x1; by 2021 he hadn't tested a 1RM in ~4 years
- **Scope:** bodybuilders
- **Confidence:** medium — 1 video, 2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@1054}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@665}} {{UrFS3Gs5zh0@922}}

### STR-46 · Constant max testing stalls progress
- **Statement:** Testing your max often "smashes the piggy bank": it spends what you have built instead of building more.
- **Numbers:** testing a deadlift 1RM every 2 weeks kept him at 465-485 lb for a year
- **Scope:** anyone who tests maxes frequently
- **Confidence:** medium — 1 video, 2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Ft039H8jzXU@1822}}

## Carryover between lifts

The best way to improve a lift is to do that lift. Variations help, but partials, machines and odd lifts can hide your real strength.

### STR-47 · Specificity wins
- **Statement:** The best way to bench more is to bench, and the best deadlift accessory is more deadlifting.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** improving a specific lift
- **Confidence:** medium — ~3 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{9M7QVlvFdQY@1150}} {{8I6IPBr2_z0@317}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@2622}}

### STR-48 · What carries over to the bench
- **Statement:** Close-grip bench carries over and skull crushers barely do. Push-ups and ring push-ups don't build bench strength, but dips carry over a lot. A slingshot carries over little to raw benching, and using only a slingshot for 2 months could cost raw strength.
- **Numbers:** he was advanced at push-ups yet benched ~145 lb the first time; a lifter hit a 4-plate bench after 6 weeks thanks to dip strength
- **Scope:** bench accessories
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{K4UBm1dV6WM@947}} {{-anA4CV2SVU@504}} {{3z75cYOdZ74@186}} {{2LoP6-KdEx4@1644}}

### STR-49 · Dips as the pressing strength lift
- **Statement:** He believes not everyone can reach a 4-plate bench, but everyone can reach a 4-plate dip. Dips don't hurt him, so far heavier dips feel achievable.
- **Numbers:** 4-plate dip for all; 5-7 plates feels achievable to him; "get to a 10-plate dip" before complaining about human limits
- **Scope:** pressing strength, especially for those whose bench stalls or hurts
- **Confidence:** medium — ~2 videos, 2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{ZLheQPgEfoA@4816}} {{lyj1BFZ85Dg@8891}}

### STR-50 · What carries over to squat and deadlift
- **Statement:** Front squats carry over to the back squat less than people think, and pause squats carry over more. Front squats barely help the conventional pull beyond upper-back rigidity. A stronger squat helps the deadlift only up to a point. The SSB box squat in deadlift stance may be the best conventional-deadlift accessory, and sumo carries to conventional better than the reverse.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** squat and deadlift accessories
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2021

### STR-51 · Pause reps are a variation, not a replacement
- **Statement:** Pause reps build strength but force lighter weights and can't replace touch-and-go reps, so use them as variations. Even top lifters use variations, so pure minimalism fails.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** strength lifts

### STR-52 · Partials and odd lifts mask true strength

## Strength standards

He mostly dismisses strength standards, with a few minimum baselines he does endorse.

### STR-57 · Strength standards are C-tier
- **Statement:** He ignores strength standards unless you compete. They rest on unverified self-reports or powerlifter data and round plate numbers, and they ignore leverages, experience, the nervous system and timeline. They compare you to weak people and turn hypertrophy training into number-chasing. If you compete, use your sport's competition standards.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** non-competing lifters; competitors use their sport's standards
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current

- **Statement:** The 1/2/3/4-plate standard roughly holds for novices because the loads are low, but beyond novice, leverages make fixed numbers meaningless. The OHP step is far harder than the others.

## Powerlifting, powerbuilding and strength sports

He sees powerlifting and bodybuilding as cousins that shouldn't be mixed, and he rejects powerbuilding.

### STR-63 · Rejects powerbuilding
- **Statement:** Heavy big-three work plus high-rep accessories usually delivers neither size nor strength. It trains one narrow kind of strength (1RMs on three lifts) that has to dominate and drains energy, and most powerbuilders never compete. The fix is to drop the 1RM training, and then you are just bodybuilding the silver/bronze-era way. He debated Dr. Milo Wolf on this.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** bodybuilders tempted by powerbuilding
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2022→2024
