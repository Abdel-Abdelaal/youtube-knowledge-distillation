# 11. Bulking, cutting & cardio

This chapter covers how NH thinks a natural should manage bodyweight (bulking, cutting, recomposition, body-fat ranges) and how cardio fits into bodybuilding. His default is a very slow lean bulk on a small surplus, judged by gym progress, waist, mirror and calisthenics rather than the scale. He cuts only if you start fat or compete, and he calls dirty bulks, crash cuts and yo-yo cycles his own biggest mistake. On cardio he moved from "not necessary" (2020-21) to advocate (2023+): short hard sessions after lifting so that your lungs never end a set before your muscles do.

## Who should bulk, cut or recomp

His starting question is where you are now: skinny, skinny-fat, lean but small, or fat. Over time he widened the group he tells to bulk.

### BLK-01 · Default for naturals: small permanent surplus
- **Statement:** Men who are not very over- or underweight should stay in a small surplus for their whole career ("perma-bulk"), unless they start out fat; overshooting body fat once ruins it.
- **Numbers:** "permabulk from day one for 15 years"
- **Scope:** Natural men not at a bodyweight extreme; fat starters cut first (BLK-04).
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2024, still said in 2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is the default plan a small, continuous surplus rather than repeated bulk/cut phases?
- **Sources:** {{VAblRcVMjqw@248}} {{VpPf9o5923k@379}} {{mUAHGlseeyk@566}} {{yZC3Nsmmzb8@6575}}

### BLK-02 · Who should bulk: from extremes only to most
- **Statement:** In 2020 only extremes should bulk or cut (very skinny bulk, very fat cut, everyone else near maintenance). By 2024 lean, small and formerly chubby lifters must lean bulk, and maintaining or cutting is a mistake for them; moving past the lean "auto mode" physique requires an awkward bulking phase.
- **Numbers:** example lean-but-small lifter: ~15% at 190 cm/81 kg (2024)
- **Scope:** Naturals who are lean but not big, including escaped skinny-fat and formerly chubby lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020: only very skinny people should bulk; 2024: lean/small lifters must bulk.
- **Sources:** {{VpPf9o5923k@63}} {{U2WB_cYv2po@124}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@6639}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@10484}}

### BLK-03 · Skinny naturals must gain weight, slowly
- **Statement:** Skinny naturals should slow lean bulk on a small surplus, never dirty or aggressively (they just turn skinny-fat). You must gain weight to gain muscle; building muscle at maintenance at 130 lb is a meme. Skinny women need to gain too.
- **Numbers:** a skinny novice transforms in 2-3 years
- **Scope:** Skinny naturals, men and women.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** changed — 2022: "you do not need a caloric surplus" (he had gained at maintenance); 2022-2024: for skinny/lean lifters a surplus is required and "your best weapon".
- **Sources:** {{74XVh8ghvs4@2198}} {{RryibwmuOWE@1596}} {{h3U30oRVrMU@63}} {{yZC3Nsmmzb8@7595}}

### BLK-04 · Fat people: lift while you cut

- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Overweight/obese lifters, especially novices.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current

### BLK-05 · Skinny-fat: recomp at maintenance, never cut
- **Statement:** Skinny-fat is not a body type but metabolic distress. Stay at maintenance (or a very slight surplus or deficit) and put the effort into training; cutting skinny-fat is a mistake.

### BLK-08 · Formerly very fat: stop cutting, then bulk

## Surplus size

The body can only turn so much food into muscle; past a small surplus the rest is fat.

### BLK-12 · Keep the surplus small
- **Statement:** Bulk on a small surplus; a few hundred calories is enough and more is pointless for a natural past beginner.
- **Numbers:** +200-400 kcal (2020); under 10%, "4% beats 40%" (2021); his own +300 (2022); ~500 kcal gets ~95-98% of possible gains (2022); 200 kcal "enough" (2024-09); 300 "plenty", 500 "pointless" (2024-12)
- **Scope:** Naturals past the beginner stage; see BLK-15 for the hardgainer exception.
- **Confidence:** high — ~9 videos, 2020→2024, still said in 2024
- **Status:** changed — the advised surplus shrank: 10-15% ceiling (2020) and ~500 kcal near-optimal (2022) → 200-300 kcal, 500 "pointless" (2024).
- **Check:** Is the bulking surplus around 200-300 kcal rather than 500+?
- **Sources:** {{vC9cKh9tFxs@1253}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@318}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@8320}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@8625}}

### BLK-13 · Big surplus = fat; big deficit = muscle loss
- **Statement:** A big surplus doesn't build more natural muscle, only fat; a big deficit burns muscle as well as fat. Protein synthesis is limited, your capacity to get fat is not.
- **Numbers:** no muscle benefit above ~10-15% surplus (2020); 1000-2000 kcal does not double muscle (2022)
- **Scope:** Naturals.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{NpIo8tFRESg@700}} {{VAblRcVMjqw@248}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@941}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@318}}

### BLK-14 · Novices start at maintenance
- **Statement:** Novices should eat at maintenance and raise calories slowly as appetite grows; a big surplus is a waste.
- **Numbers:** +500 kcal or more is a waste
- **Scope:** Novices.
- **Confidence:** medium — 1 video, 2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1670}}

### BLK-15 · Hardgainer exception: eat more
- **Statement:** Very skinny, fast-metabolism lifters who stall on a small surplus should eat more aggressively.
- **Numbers:** perhaps double the surplus
- **Scope:** Very skinny lifters with fast metabolisms who have stalled.
- **Confidence:** medium — 1 video, 2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{h7D6CB4p1oU@2212}}

### BLK-16 · Raise calories slowly, then hold
- **Statement:** Raise calories slowly and hold them steady until the bulking intake becomes your new maintenance (proof you gained muscle), then raise again. Constant swings in intake disrupt training.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — 2 videos, 2021→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{U2WB_cYv2po@567}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@694}}

### BLK-17 · Small daily surpluses beat huge days
- **Statement:** A steady small surplus beats occasional huge eating days, which are mostly stored as fat. Force-feeding makes you lethargic and diet-fixated.
- **Numbers:** a 3,000 kcal surplus day is mostly fat; +1000 kcal/day force-feeding criticised
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{6nKKGsCCqxo@695}} {{2sIS1fZYqrc@128}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@445}}

### BLK-18 · His own method: bare-minimum surplus
- **Statement:** He bulks on the smallest surplus that still moves the scale; for him an extra bowl of rice is enough because his metabolism is slow. He admits this instinctive method may not suit hardgainers.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Him; slow-metabolism lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2022→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{uYRIWAKlA0c@127}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@943}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@3039}}

## Rate of gain

He measures a bulk by how slowly the scale moves, and the advised rate tightened over the years.

### BLK-19 · Gain about 0.5-1.5 lb per month
- **Statement:** Bulk slowly; past the beginner stage, more than a pound or so a month is mostly fat.
- **Numbers:** under 4 lb/month (2020-08); 1-2 lb/month (2020-12); 0.5-1 lb/month or ~10 lb/year (2023-07); ~1 lb/month (2023-10); 1.5 lb/month "good" on a lean bulk (2024-11)
- **Scope:** Intermediate and advanced naturals; true beginners can gain faster (BLK-21).
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 1-2 lb/month (2020) tightened to 0.5-1 lb/month after his own too-fast 2023 bulk (20-30 lb in a year); 1.5 lb/month called good in 2024.
- **Check:** Is the target gain about 0.5-1.5 lb per month (less if advanced)?
- **Sources:** {{mUAHGlseeyk@566}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@865}} {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1317}} {{fPU3nRIH8sM@9349}}

### BLK-20 · The more advanced, the slower
- **Statement:** The more advanced you are, the slower the bulk must be. An advanced lifter gaining 2 lb a month is just gaining fat.
- **Numbers:** he gained 5 lb in a year from 210 (<0.5 lb/month); 10 lb lean mass in 2-3 months at 200 lb is impossible
- **Scope:** Advanced naturals.
- **Confidence:** high — 3 videos, 2023→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1193}} {{43F9unmSc_I@2136}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@9103}}

### BLK-21 · Judge gain relative to bodyweight
- **Statement:** Judge monthly gain as a percentage of bodyweight; fast gain is justified only for true beginners filling out.
- **Numbers:** at 180 lb, several lb/month is too much to be muscle for an intermediate
- **Scope:** Intermediates vs true beginners.
- **Confidence:** medium — 1 video, 2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@248}}

### BLK-22 · Spread gains over years
- **Statement:** Gain your mass over years, not months.
- **Numbers:** over 5 years rather than 8-10 months (2020); 20 lb over 3 years beats 20 lb in 1 year (2023); 14 kg in 4 years is good (2024); 50 lb in 3 years too fast unless dangerously skinny (~20 lb of it avoidable fat)
- **Scope:** All naturals.
- **Confidence:** high — 4 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{a02c10s3Pjk@443}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@1569}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@12773}} {{yZC3Nsmmzb8@8627}}

### BLK-23 · Fast fat-free gains are a lie
- **Statement:** Never try to gain 20 lb fast "without fat", even if skinny; anyone claiming 20 lb of muscle with no fat is lying, and no one bulks 100 lb and gets 90% muscle.
- **Numbers:** 20 lb; 100 lb / 90%
- **Scope:** All naturals.
- **Confidence:** medium — 3 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{VAblRcVMjqw@0}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@598}} {{GsRa8YsVo00@126}}

### BLK-24 · Slower gain is more likely muscle
- **Statement:** The same weight gained faster may give the same strength increase, but the slower gain is far more likely muscle.
- **Numbers:** 10 lb in 2 months vs 5-6 months; an inch on the arms in 3 months is fat
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — 2 videos, 2021→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{-T8qWCI4_No@1012}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@953}}

### BLK-25 · Each bulk more conservative
- **Statement:** Each bulk after the first should be more conservative ("first bulk is like a first love"); lifters tend to do the opposite.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Lifters on their second or later bulk.
- **Confidence:** low — 1 video, 2024
- **Status:** current

### BLK-26 · Teens have time
- **Statement:** Teens can slow bulk for up to 10 years. Forcing large gains (e.g. for a sport season) backfires: it is mostly fat and fosters eating disorders.

## Training drives the bulk

Calories only fuel what the training creates.

### BLK-27 · Training dictates diet
- **Statement:** Calories can't become muscle by themselves. Training creates growth and the surplus only fuels recovery, so extra calories must coincide with rising volume, workload and intensity, or they are stored as fat.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~9 videos, 2021→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Are calorie increases matched by rising volume, workload or intensity in the gym?
- **Sources:** {{9mnLRQsrs9k@2836}} {{h7D6CB4p1oU@126}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@1507}} {{TxDUQPe3B0E@880}}

### BLK-28 · A bulk must raise work capacity
- **Statement:** A bulk should raise work capacity and volume; if volume regresses on a bulk, there's no reason to bulk. Eat to fuel progression, not to gain weight.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~4 videos, 2022→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{yUecX6qONXg@252}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@628}} {{6KA80BQ9W3k@1193}}

### BLK-29 · Progressing? Don't add calories
- **Statement:** If you are progressing without a surplus, don't add calories. Raising his surplus while still progressing was his "first mistake" in 2023.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Anyone still progressing at current intake.
- **Confidence:** medium — 2 videos, 2023→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@186}} {{okm6bZHKHI4@8257}}

### BLK-30 · Bulking helps but isn't required
- **Statement:** Novices and intermediates can gain without bulking; a surplus only makes progression easier, and its main advantage is faster visible gains, which help adherence.

## Bulk checks: muscle or fat?

Rising scale and bar weight do not prove muscle; he uses calisthenics, waist, mirror and the ratio of strength to bodyweight.

### BLK-32 · The calisthenics check
- **Statement:** If bench rises while push-ups regress, or rows rise while pull-ups drop, you're gaining fat: slow down or stop the bulk. Keep calisthenics in while bulking.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — 3 videos, 2021→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Do push-ups and pull-ups hold or improve while the bulk continues?
- **Sources:** {{HrUPp573ki0@1684}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@565}} {{Ft039H8jzXU@502}}

### BLK-33 · Strength in proportion to bodyweight is a bad trade
- **Statement:** Strength rising only in proportion to added bodyweight is a bad trade and mostly not muscle; heavier scale and heavier bar don't equal muscle ("mass moves mass", better leverage).
- **Numbers:** +20 lb bench for +10 lb bodyweight = bad trade
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters, especially strength-focused ones.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{TgPYObguEM0@63}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@2870}} {{RryibwmuOWE@2006}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@312}}

### BLK-34 · Watch the waist
- **Statement:** Rising lifts and bodyweight with a growing waist means fat, a trap for bodybuilders using powerlifting principles. A rapidly growing waist means visceral fat, and every inch there looks bad.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~4 videos, 2021→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is the waist staying roughly stable while bodyweight rises?

### BLK-35 · Mirror as often as the scale

## Dirty bulking and GOMAD

### BLK-41 · Dirty bulking is mostly fat
- **Statement:** Dirty bulking adds mostly fat and leverage, not muscle. Junk food is low in protein and micronutrients, so you feel sluggish and bloated; whatever muscle you keep would have come from any surplus. Big naturals got big in spite of it.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Naturals.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** contradicted — 2020: dirty bulking builds less muscle because junk lacks protein; 2021: the muscle difference is "negligibly small, if it exists".
- **Check:** Does the bulk avoid dirty bulking and force-feeding?
- **Sources:** {{a02c10s3Pjk@65}} {{a02c10s3Pjk@256}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@524}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@7106}}

### BLK-42 · There is always more fat than you think
- **Statement:** After a dirty bulk you must cut and lose the "size and strength" you thought you had. Everyone who cuts finds more fat than expected, and you can't predict your shredded weight by subtracting fat. Some people never lose the love handles.
- **Numbers:** fat you think is 20 lb is really 40; plan 20 lb, need 40-60
- **Scope:** Lifters who over-bulked; anyone planning a cut.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{so6MbvvtM1w@714}} {{Cn38kgCdOhw@380}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@901}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@7106}}

### BLK-43 · GOMAD is stupid
- **Statement:** A gallon of milk a day overshoots into a large surplus with no room to manage calories, and brings too much milk fat and protein. At most it suits very skinny or very active athletes, and it can promote dysmorphia.
- **Numbers:** a gallon of milk/day
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters; possible exception for very skinny or very active athletes.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{E783zHvVUdk@0}} {{E783zHvVUdk@380}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@1170}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@4295}}

### BLK-44 · Excess fat is anti-anabolic
- **Statement:** Excess fat lowers testosterone, so the fatter you get the less anabolic you are; obese naturals might even lose muscle. Visceral fat and the tendon load of extra bodyweight aren't fully reversed by leaning out.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Naturals.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~5 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{a02c10s3Pjk@568}} {{so6MbvvtM1w@1266}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@950}} {{-T8qWCI4_No@1012}}

### BLK-45 · Force-feeding harms your relationship with food
- **Statement:** Force-feeding can trigger eating disorders the way crash diets trigger anorexia. If you hate your food, the bulk fails.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bulking lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{TgPYObguEM0@63}} {{NpIo8tFRESg@630}} {{aFENxCkDXQM@3200}} {{XK9KjVP-5Wo@1003}}
