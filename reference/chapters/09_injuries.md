# 9. Injuries

This chapter covers why lifters get hurt, how to read pain, how to treat tendinitis, how to program to avoid injury, and joint-by-joint rules for the back, knees, shoulders, elbows, wrists and other sites. His stance: injuries are almost never bad luck. They come from mismanaged load, fatigue, volume and repetition, so they are the lifter's responsibility. Tendinitis is treated by training around the pain, not by rest or drugs. For a natural, staying injury-free is the best way to keep growing.

## Why injuries happen

He puts nearly every injury down to variables the lifter controls: load and fatigue, grinding reps, and repeating the same pattern.

### INJ-01 · Injuries are preventable, not bad luck
- **Statement:** Injuries come from variables you failed to control, so they are preventable; calling them bad luck is lazy and irresponsible.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters. Only things like being tackled mid-lift are truly out of your control; skipping a warm-up or slipping on ice still counts as yours.
- **Confidence:** high — ~14 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{rsbqqfM6ZFM@0}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@492}} {{yoROLGGUPXU@64}} {{iU-oYc8fbEM@632}}

### INJ-02 · Volume/intensity mismanagement is the top cause
- **Statement:** Ego, too much load and forcing the extra rep cause most injuries; together with poor positioning they account for the "vast majority". Perfect form cannot save poor volume/intensity management.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{rsbqqfM6ZFM@62}} {{pUpQypTV3Ck@124}} {{W-s1ft_HqYE@1061}} {{iU-oYc8fbEM@632}}

### INJ-03 · Injury = load and fatigue exceeding tissue capacity
- **Statement:** An injury is pressure exceeding what the tissue can handle, a failure to adapt to a cycle of stress. Risk depends on load and accumulated fatigue together.
- **Numbers:** 50% of max is "baby weight"; 5 reps at 80% to failure every day raises risk daily as fatigue builds.
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~4 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Z90VsPDV__U@423}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@189}} {{iCWUcBy7Vxs@62}} {{sFMQj4Lflk0@1320}}

### INJ-04 · The "red zone" is where injuries happen
- **Statement:** Acute injuries happen where the negative reverses into the positive; almost nobody gets hurt at lockout. Negative-first lifts (squat, bench) are the most dangerous at high intensity, and a controlled negative is injury prevention.
- **Numbers:** "99%" of injuries in the red zone (2020); 95-98% (2021).
- **Scope:** All lifts, especially heavy squat and bench.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{2hwj-NEebj8@126}} {{6kRRzKx7lKs@1600}} {{UzTfF1VduxM@382}} {{8ELQ2XZgpls@2531}} {{9kIIW251uiI@2140}}

### INJ-05 · Grinding the last rep causes most injuries
- **Statement:** Most gym injuries come in the last grindy reps of heavy sets. With no grinding, staying in the rep range and no last-rep chasing, risk is "almost zero". All his own past injuries came from grinding reps he wasn't capable of.
- **Numbers:** Cutting 3-4 grinder squat reps gave him 3 years with no squat injury.
- **Scope:** All lifters, heavy compound sets.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does the program avoid grinding ugly reps and chasing the extra rep on heavy sets?
- **Sources:** {{QiOh8SF-UxQ@300}} {{rsbqqfM6ZFM@125}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@253}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@568}} {{9kIIW251uiI@692}}

### INJ-06 · Overuse comes from repetition, not frequency
- **Statement:** Overuse injuries come from repeating the same movement, angle, stance and load. Frequency itself is not the cause. Back-off sets in the same style as the main lift, minimalist HIT and Big-3-only training all feed tendinitis.
- **Numbers:** A quad can be trained every 48 h if the stance varies.
- **Scope:** All lifters; see INJ-46 for the rotation rule.
- **Confidence:** high — ~14 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{hYFO2eKn-m4@374}} {{7dqBu5KLIoQ@128}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@941}} {{29XrRmOrCMs@569}} {{IR-YS-TGGx0@125}}

### INJ-07 · Muscle should give out before tendon
- **Statement:** Mechanical failure with weak technique shifts tonnage onto skeleton and tendons and causes tendinitis. Maximize stress on the muscle and minimize it on tendons; tendons are never fully spared, but there is a sweet spot.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~7 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{T_ZMmSNmLbs@444}} {{3J-zADEbtqU@376}} {{rIngKQqTJMs@564}} {{ZScs2L9rero@1330}}

### INJ-08 · Never high volume, frequency and intensity together
- **Statement:** You can combine two of high volume, high frequency and high intensity, never all three; the trifecta "summons injury".

- **Statement:** An injury is a gap in tonnage, which means no growth, plus a mental-health hit; preventing injury is the best growth strategy.

## Reading pain

He separates muscle pain from tendon/joint pain, separates "hurt" from "injured", and judges a lift by how it feels the next day.

### INJ-23 · Never train through joint or tendon pain
- **Statement:** Joint/tendon pain is a warning; pushing through turns minor issues into lifelong ones, and joint pain never goes away by itself. If a movement hurts, stop and re-check form; if form changes don't fix it, drop the lift, since there is "always a way around".
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters; applies to tendon/joint pain, not muscle soreness (INJ-24).
- **Confidence:** high — ~14 videos, 2020→2025
- **Status:** changed — 2020: absolute "never lift through pain; if it hurts, stop". 2021: graded pain scales where minor pain can be pushed aside (INJ-26). 2022-2025: tendon/joint pain is still a stop signal.
- **Check:** Is any lift that causes joint or tendon pain modified or dropped rather than pushed through?
- **Sources:** {{1R42snjAX4I@0}} {{Eh0fnXIyDMc@382}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@0}} {{waeSP7DBmig@370}}

### INJ-24 · Muscle pain is fine, tendon/joint pain is an alarm
- **Statement:** There are two kinds of pain: pump and muscle soreness (fine) versus tendon, ligament or joint pain (alarm). Tendons have more nerve endings because they repair poorly. Tension in the muscle is "more painful than dangerous"; pain at the tendon insertion is not.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Eh0fnXIyDMc@0}} {{WyxeMHovPJ8@290}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@377}} {{9kIIW251uiI@3}}

### INJ-25 · Hurt is not injured
- **Statement:** Pain is not injury and doesn't tell you the degree of damage; judge by function, not sensation. Most times lifters think they're injured it is nothing, and an unnecessary break hurts them.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{1R42snjAX4I@254}} {{-GYV9s5Ad8s@631}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@603}} {{d1eLAd5WvIA@1503}}

### INJ-26 · Graded pain scales: occurrence and aftermath
- **Statement:** He grades pain during the lift (occurrence) and after the session (aftermath), each with a matching response; see the table below.
- **Numbers:** Level 1 in-set pain: one more rep; if it persists, stop, walk 2 min, do another set ("99%" gone). Aftermath level 3+ is usually injury.
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** medium — 1 video, 2021
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Z90VsPDV__U@1528}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@1665}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@1760}} {{Z90VsPDV__U@2024}}

| scale | level | sign | response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occurrence | 0 | noise | nothing |
| Occurrence | 1 | pain on a rep | do one more rep; if it persists, stop, walk 2 min, do another set {{Z90VsPDV__U@1528}} |
| Occurrence | 2 | pain all workout | small strain or nocebo; push through or go home {{Z90VsPDV__U@1590}} |
| Occurrence | 4 | shift + pain | walk it off; if still there, likely injured {{Z90VsPDV__U@1665}} |
| Occurrence | 5 | shift + pain + snap | injury |
| Occurrence | 6 | structural failure | freak accident, avoidable {{Z90VsPDV__U@1760}} |
| Aftermath | 0 | soreness | nothing {{Z90VsPDV__U@1915}} |
| Aftermath | 1 | pain only lifting heavy | change angle or go lighter that day |
| Aftermath | 2 | pain with any weight | change movement/ROM, keep moving {{Z90VsPDV__U@1962}} |
| Aftermath | 3 | pain walking | usually injury, often tendinitis |
| Aftermath | 4-6 | pain to the touch / at rest or waking you / can't move | structural damage {{Z90VsPDV__U@2024}} |

## Tendinitis: diagnosis and treatment

Tendinitis is his most-discussed injury. His treatment is to keep training in a pain-free range, change angles, push blood to the tendon and build the surrounding muscle, with little rest and no painkillers.

### INJ-32 · Tendinitis is a spectrum ending in rupture
- **Statement:** Tendinitis is the most common lifting injury, ranging from occasional pain to deep all-day pain. Ruptures (biceps, patellar, hip labrum) usually follow months or years of ignored tendinitis.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{9c7O0jjeR5Q@126}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@64}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1398}} {{yoROLGGUPXU@0}}

### INJ-33 · Rest does not cure tendinitis
- **Statement:** Rest only seems to cure tendinitis; it returns because the movement cause is unchanged. Immobility "fossilizes" the tendon and atrophies the muscle that shares its load. Take at most 1-2 days off when pain is bad, mainly to get over the emotional hump; longer builds a fear association.
- **Numbers:** Max 1-2 days off; 3-4 weeks is far too long; 2 weeks off still re-flares.
- **Scope:** Any tendinitis.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is tendinitis handled with at most 1-2 days off followed by modified training, rather than weeks of rest?
- **Sources:** {{3dLu3VQinL8@1386}} {{vHf2ZoHWHQs@312}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@583}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@1533}}

### INJ-34 · Avoid painkillers and anti-inflammatories
- **Statement:** They mask the signal you need, and in his view anti-inflammatories weaken tendons and block adaptation ("not medical advice"). If you are on them anyway, avoid max singles and stay high-rep. He claims a powerlifter on painkillers squatted through months of hip pain until the labrum tore.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Lifters with tendon or joint pain.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{vHf2ZoHWHQs@160}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@852}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@641}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@978}}

### INJ-35 · Train in the pain-free "valley"
- **Statement:** Train between inactive moderate pain and acute pain; this widens the pain-free window. End the session before pain turns acute. Warm-up blood flow eases pain, but if it returns mid-session you are re-aggravating it. Rehab is never painless; the pain becomes dull.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Tendinitis rehab.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1084}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1335}} {{vHf2ZoHWHQs@815}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@1533}}

### INJ-36 · Tendinitis is "a game of angles"
- **Statement:** Find which movements load the painful tendon and switch to variations that work the muscle without pain. Reintroduce the painful movement unloaded; if it always hurts, ban it, because there are always equal alternatives.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Tendinitis rehab.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2025
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1019}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1148}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1272}} {{waeSP7DBmig@370}}

### INJ-37 · Blood flow and surrounding muscle fix tendons
- **Statement:** Send blood to the tendon with light, high-rep pump work, and build the surrounding muscles and the antagonist, since an aggravated tendon is doing too much work. Only corrective work that stresses the tendon enough to adapt ends tendinitis for good. A pump matters more for tendon health than for growth.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Tendinitis rehab and prevention.
- **Confidence:** high — ~9 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{3dLu3VQinL8@1448}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@470}} {{P7ADeDVghxg@1120}} {{KzXUgd-SnMw@1618}}

### INJ-38 · Fix the environment first
- **Statement:** For tendon recovery, fix stress, sleep and badly managed rest in the program first. He also mentions MSM powder and more water.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Tendinitis.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~2 videos, 2020→2021

- **Sources:** {{KzXUgd-SnMw@239}} {{VXwYO2qG9ZY@446}}

- **Statement:** Use a reusable gel pack with a thin cover, never directly on skin. Build duration gradually, then stay still as long as you iced; never ice before or during activity.

- **Confidence:** high — ~4 videos, 2020→2024

## Programming for prevention

Most of his prevention is programming: rotating variations, keeping risky lifts at higher reps, ordering the session, balancing push and pull, and ramping in changes slowly.

### INJ-46 · Rotate variations, grips and stances
