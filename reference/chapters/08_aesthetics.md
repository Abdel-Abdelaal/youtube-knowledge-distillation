# 8. Aesthetics & the gym look

This chapter covers what NH thinks a physique should look like and how to judge one: body fat, proportions, which muscles matter most to the eye, frame, measuring and the social side of looking muscular. His stance is that bodybuilding is an art of illusion aimed at beauty. Every muscle should be trained with aesthetic intent, imbalances should be deliberate, and a natural should carry a moderate body fat over a lot of muscle. He also holds that big limbs, a developed core, a thick neck and upper back count for more than stage leanness. How to train each muscle is in ch. 6; bulking and cutting are in ch. 11.

## What bodybuilding is for

His starting point: looks are a legitimate goal, they come from function that you point toward appearance, and the goal is personal as long as it is chosen on purpose.

- **Statement:** Bodybuilding pursues beauty through illusion, not a pile of mass or big measurements; flow, insertions and posing beat raw size.

### AES-02 · Train with aesthetic intent or get an accidental physique
- **Statement:** Pure strength, big-three-only or compound-only training gives an "accidental" physique: big trunk and quads, small arms (spider, fridge, frog). A minimalist strength routine won't build an aesthetic body. His own 2020 big-three-only experiment confirmed it.

- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current

## Body fat and leanness

He wants lots of muscle at a moderate body fat. Fat, muscle size and fat pattern all change how lean someone looks.

### AES-11 · Natural ideal is about 12-18% body fat
- **Statement:** A natural should carry about 12-18% body fat with lots of muscle; stage condition is pointless for naturals. Old-school naturals and physique competitors 100 years ago were in this range and looked tremendous.
- **Numbers:** 12-18% (old-school naturals); 10-16% (era competitors); abs look good at 10, 15 or 17%; 15% looks good; 6-8% "achievable but not reasonable"; 5% is masochism; 8% standards make men look like skeletons
- **Scope:** Naturals training for looks, not stage competitors.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is the target condition in the 12-18% range rather than stage leanness (5-8%)?
- **Sources:** {{Cn38kgCdOhw@1389}} {{oAXzDQIwvLg@1562}} {{oAXzDQIwvLg@1621}} {{q1XhaAN9M5k@1877}}

## Proportions and his ideal physique

He frames ideals through Greek statues and the classical era, prefers a limb-heavy Herculean look for himself, and insists that neighbouring muscles grow together.

### AES-20 · Apollonian V-taper vs Herculean X-taper
- **Statement:** The Greek tradition has two branches: Apollonian (wide shoulders and lats, small waist, smaller legs, low fat) and Herculean/Farnese (thicker waist, big limbs, legs and core). He prefers the Herculean, ancient "bear mode"; the Farnese Hercules is closer to his ideal.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Goal-setting; he has written programs for both.
- **Confidence:** high — ~7 videos, 2020→2022
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{4IhOKe7lo_s@0}} {{4IhOKe7lo_s@189}} {{Zt6bvNqnJmA@516}} {{IYobXlRVLd0@309}}

### AES-21 · His "Berserker" ideal: big limbs, strong core, upper back
- **Statement:** Massive arms, forearms and legs slightly out of proportion with the trunk, a strong core, developed upper back and smaller shoulders. Big limbs pull attention from the torso, an illusion.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** His personal goal.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** changed — 2020-22 the trunk and chest stay comparatively small (2021 "Buddha physique": big limbs, small shoulders and chest, very developed core); 2023 the Berserker includes a big chest; 2025, being narrow, he chases a Bizu-style physique with huge traps.
- **Sources:** {{NTMGQPjpP00@126}} {{PYICG0hBlVo@446}} {{43F9unmSc_I@0}} {{waeSP7DBmig@270}}

### AES-22 · Arms should look bigger than the shoulders
- **Statement:** He dislikes big round delts on smaller limbs; the arm should dominate the shoulder.

- **Statement:** A classical look has smaller legs and posterior chain, a tight waist and a V-taper from shoulders and lats; the silver era didn't chase glutes or hamstrings. Use upper/lower plus arm/upper hybrids instead of two lower days.

For him the limbs are what separate a bodybuilder from someone who is merely big.

- **Numbers:** —

### AES-30 · Isolate the arms or become a "spider"

- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024

## Core and waist

### AES-43 · The core ties the physique together
- **Statement:** The eye goes to the midsection first, and weak abs make everything look worse; without his abs, he says, his physique would look terrible. Never skip core, especially with a wide waist.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All, especially shirtless looks and wide waists.
- **Confidence:** high — ~7 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020-23 the core is key (2021: the most important thing for aesthetics); 2024 tier list puts abs bottom tier for everyday clothed looks, while he still says never skip core. A refinement by context (clothed vs shirtless).
- **Check:** Is the core trained directly?
- **Sources:** {{rGt7TWcWD60@0}} {{f-XiVNy25tw@595}} {{7KDB5wzgY4c@0}} {{bRKQIOYo2u4@1343}}

### AES-44 · Waist width is mostly bone structure
- **Statement:** Ab and oblique training won't ruin a V-taper; it only thickens the wall a little. Bodybuilders with thick obliques often had fat there first, and waist fat goes when you cut.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** General.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2021→2024
