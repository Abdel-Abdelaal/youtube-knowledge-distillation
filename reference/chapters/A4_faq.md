# Appendix D. FAQ

The questions viewers asked most often in NH's Q&As, merged and answered from the principles in this book.

## Volume and intensity

### How many sets per muscle per week should I do?
A weekly set count means little on its own, and ±1-2 sets don't matter. If you need a number, 8-14 hard sets per muscle per week; at three sessions a week and high intensity, 9-15 is enough to keep growing. Almost no one needs 16-20. → VOL-19, VOL-20, VOL-22

### How do I know if my volume is too high or too low?
Lingering soreness or failing to repeat last session's performance means too much; if you are doing high weekly sets with slow strength gains, try less volume and more intensity. No progress means either too little stimulus or too much. When unsure, err slightly high but add conservatively. → VOL-17, FAT-61, VOL-14

### Is one set to failure (HIT) enough?
No. Mentzer was right to focus on intensity but went too far: one set every 6-8 days is too little for a natural. Both extremes fail; naturals grow in the middle, with several hard sets. → INT-62, VOL-12

### Should I train to failure?
Take isolation, upper-body presses, rows and other low-risk lifts to failure; keep 1-2 reps in reserve on squats, deadlifts and good mornings. Failure is a calibration tool, not the goal, and you should never actually miss a rep. → INT-28, INT-30, INT-27

### When should I use reps in reserve?
On low-rep work and heavy, risky compounds, where the last grinding reps aren't worth the risk; 0-1 RIR on 10-20-rep sets is pointless. RIR only means something once you have trained to failure many times, so learn failure first. → INT-34, INT-35, INT-36

### What rep range builds muscle?
Intensity isn't tied to a rep range. Bodybuilding work sits mostly at 6-12 within an overall 3-15; choose 6-10, 8-12 or 10-15 by preference and by what lets you progress. Low reps build size too. → INT-12, INT-17, INT-22

### Are sets of 20+ reps worth doing?
Rarely. Above about 15-20 reps each extra rep adds little intensity, and cardio, burn or technique stop you before the muscle does. → INT-18, INT-17

### How long should I rest between sets?
About 2-3 minutes, never more than 5: as little as possible while the muscle returns to baseline. Two exercises for the same muscle still need full rest between them. → RST-01, RST-02, RST-03

### Do supersets hurt performance?
Pairing unrelated or antagonist muscles costs little or no performance, only some cardio capacity, so superset all accessory and isolation work. Don't superset the opening strength work or two lifts for the same muscle. → RST-09, RST-10, RST-06

### Are drop sets and rest-pause useful?
Rest-pause is his preferred technique: at failure, rest briefly and add reps that are the hardest of the set. Drop sets on every set are pump-chasing junk; use them only to reach target reps once rest-pause fails. Both go on top of a quality set, never in place of it. → INT-56, INT-57, INT-55

### How long should a session be?
About 60-90 minutes is normal and 90 is pushing it. There is no fixed cap: train until the day's stimulus is done, then leave. → VOL-57, VOL-56

## Progression and plateaus

### How do evolving rep ranges work?
Give each lift a fixed rep range, start near the bottom and add reps each week. When the first set reaches the top of the range, add weight and stay in the same range. If the first set misses after a jump, return to the previous weight. → PRG-20, PRG-21, PRG-25

### How can I progress besides adding weight?
Reps, sets, frequency, rest, variation and range of motion are all overload. But reps-only or sets-only progression stalls too, so bar weight must stay in the mix. → PRG-11, PRG-13, PRG-12

### What should I do when a lift stalls?
First change rep ranges, widen the range, add some volume or frequency, not the exercise. Don't deload and re-run the same progression. If truly stuck, add a same-pattern variation as back-offs or on another day. → PRG-73, PRG-74, PRG-76

### Should I swap a lift when it plateaus?
No. Swapping a stalled lift dodges the plateau, the "progress" on the new lift is neural relearning, and stalling is exactly when the body must build muscle. → PRG-75, EXS-20

### Do I need to log every set?
Yes: weights and reps for every set of every exercise, curls included. Not counting reps is the easiest way to make no progress. → PRG-109

### Do bodybuilders need periodization, mesocycles or training blocks?
No. The whole year is a hypertrophy block; variation lives inside the program through rotations and evolving ranges. Muscle never needs resensitising, and a good program runs indefinitely. → PRG-88, FAT-10, FRQ-78

## Recovery, deloads and sleep

### Do I need deloads?
Not scheduled ones. Needing a deload means you have been doing too much; trim the program yourself instead, which loses less tonnage. If deloads genuinely help you, he says keep them. → FAT-01, FAT-03, FAT-11

### Do I need rest days for my CNS?
Most people can't reach real CNS burnout. Strength loss blamed on the CNS is usually a calorie deficit, poor sleep, stress or a bad day. → FAT-27, FAT-28

### Is soreness a sign of growth?
No. Strength and tonnage over time are the signal. Soreness should fade with training age and frequency; constant soreness means poor adaptation or too little frequency. → SLP-42, SLP-43, FRQ-09

### How much sleep do I need?
Most adults need 7-8 hours, and hypertrophy training raises the need. Cutting an hour to be productive backfires; between sleep and entertainment, always sleep. → SLP-02, SLP-03, SLP-04

### Should I train when sick or short on sleep?
Slightly unwell or underslept: still train, but avoid heavy work. If kept up very late, sleep instead of training. → SLP-21, SLP-20

### How do I come back after a long break?
Restart at about a third of your usual tonnage with short, low-volume sessions of favourite exercises, and ramp up stepwise; muscle returns fast, but jumping straight to old loads causes injuries. → FAT-57, BEG-89, INJ-60

## Frequency and splits

### Is training a muscle once a week enough?
For a natural it is always suboptimal unless you are deliberately coasting on that muscle. The fix for a bro split is to hit each muscle about twice a week. → FRQ-03, FRQ-37

### Can I run push-pull-legs on 4-5 days?
PPL is a good split: run it about 6 times a week or 3 times a week, and for 4-5 days just cut a day. Order it pull-push-legs so pull day's hinges don't tire your legs before squats. → FRQ-41, FRQ-40

### I'm very busy. What's the minimum?
Two or three full-body days, or upper/lower twice with isolation in short gaps. A physique built on 5 days a week can be maintained on 2. → FRQ-35

### Do I need an arm day?
Only if big arms are a goal, and only once legs are trained at least twice a week with plenty of upper-back work. It adds to arm work on other days and should be low-stress. → FRQ-67, ARM-37, FRQ-66

## Exercises and body parts

### Do I need direct ab work?
Yes. Compounds and bracing don't build a six-pack; ab isolation is "non-negotiable". Diet only reveals abs. → ABS-01, ABS-02

### Will oblique training make my waist wider?
No. Waist width is hip bone structure and love handles are fat; bigger obliques tighten the side and make love handles look smaller. → ABS-13, AES-44

### Is the bench press necessary? Are dips better?
The bench is not mandatory: dips, push-ups and ring push-ups can replace it. Weighted dips are top tier for chest, triceps and shoulders, with slightly higher shoulder risk. → CHE-03, CHE-04

### Overhead press or lateral raises for shoulders?
Both, with pressing first: vertical pressing is the bread and butter of delt size, and raises support it rather than replace it. → SHO-01, SHO-02

### Do I need forearm isolation?
Yes. Heavy pulling doesn't build big forearms, and going strapless won't either; direct isolation does. → FOR-01, FOR-02

### Should I use straps?
Yes, whenever grip limits a back or pulling set; letting grip limit the back is "criminal", and straps don't hurt forearm growth. Skip them on warm-ups and where grip isn't the limit. → EQP-01, EQP-02, EQP-04

### What's the best pull-up substitute?
The lat pulldown, especially close-grip neutral, wide supinated or the diagonal pulldown taken heavy for 8-10 to failure. → BAK-14

### Are machines and the Smith machine okay?
Yes, for stable, hard, joint-friendly sets and extra volume. But free weights should stay most of a natural's training. → EXS-51, EXS-50, EQP-61

### How often should I train my neck?
One or two sessions a week, ideally supersetted with the first lower-body lift, already gives great results; more than 3-4 is pointless. He calls the sleep-apnea worry a myth. → NCK-05, NCK-06, NCK-01

### Are small calves just genetics?
No. Calves stay small because they aren't trained, or are trained inconsistently with poor exercise choices. Insertions limit absolute size, not looking beefy. → CLF-01, GEN-50

### Should I stretch?
Not statically before lifting. Lifting through full range builds mobility. The one stretch he kept is a hip-flexor stretch after leg days. → INJ-59, TEC-87, INJ-66

## Injuries

### How do I train around tendinitis?
Never push through joint or tendon pain. Find which movements load the painful tendon and switch to variations that work the muscle without pain. Rest doesn't cure it, because the cause is unchanged. → INJ-23, INJ-36, INJ-33

### Are deadlifts dangerous for the back?
Done properly the deadlift is one of the safest lifts; deadlifting with bad form is the problem. Next-day low-back pain means a style you aren't built for or improper pulling. → INJ-68, INJ-69

## Nutrition and bulking

### How much protein do I need?
Far less than commonly sold. About 0.8 g/lb is near optimal but more than he needs; 2 g/kg, 2 g/lb or 200 g a day is wasted. → NUT-16, NUT-17, NUT-18

### Should I bulk, cut or maintain?
Most men who aren't very over- or underweight should stay in a small permanent surplus. Fat lifters cut while lifting; skinny-fat lifters recomp at maintenance. → BLK-01, BLK-04, BLK-05

### How fast should I gain weight?
Slowly: about 0.5-1.5 lb a month, and slower the more advanced you are. Past the beginner stage, more than about a pound a month is mostly fat. → BLK-19, BLK-20

### How big should my surplus be?
Small. A few hundred calories is enough; a big surplus only builds fat. → BLK-12, BLK-13

### Is weight loss just calories in, calories out?
Energy balance governs weight change, but food choice decides what kind of weight you gain or lose, and the source of calories matters. → NUT-08, NUT-09

### Can I train fasted or eat one meal a day?
He never trains fasted; eat a carb-rich meal 2-3 hours before. Meal timing is "D tier" and OMAD isn't inherently bad for hypertrophy. → NUT-37, NUT-35

### How long should a recomp last? Can I recomp forever?
Commit at least a year. When it stalls, don't cut deeper: run a very slow bulk, then recomp again. A recomp that only holds mass is wasted time. → BLK-57, BLK-63, BLK-64

### I can't gain weight. What do I do?
"Can't gain" means not eating enough. Use scheduled meals, more of them, and drink calories through homemade shakes. → BLK-52, NUT-85, NUT-86

### Which supplements are worth it?
Almost none matter for muscle. In 2024 he named whey, creatine, multivitamins and vitamin D as the only ones worth anything. Take creatine daily without loading or cycling. → SUP-01, SUP-09, SUP-12

## Natural lifting

### Doesn't testosterone crash after 30?
No. Natural men peak in their 30s-40s, and most muscle is built between your 20s and 50s. You lose muscle by stopping training and diet, not by turning 40. → NAT-34, SLP-49

### Should I plan on TRT when I'm older?
He says no: men in their 50s-60s do fine without it, and any exogenous hormone ends natural status. → NAT-50, NAT-46

### Can a natural reach 20 or 21-inch arms?
19-20-inch cold arms are possible naturally; 21 inches is doubtful. → NAT-39

### Is there a natural limit?
Not a locatable one. Progress slows but continues as long as weight, volume and intensity keep rising; most people who claim a limit stopped finding the work worth it. → GEN-11, GEN-12

### How long until I stop looking like I don't lift?
With average genetics it took him about 6 years to look trained and 10+ to look jacked; changes show in months and people notice in years. → JRN-06, AES-79, GEN-20

## Beginners

### How often should a novice train?
Three full-body days a week, about 48 hours apart. → BEG-11, FRQ-80

### Is StrongLifts 5x5 a good beginner program?
It works only because anything works for novices. It is a powerlifting program that lacks isolation and pushes beginners into weights they shouldn't handle. → BEG-33, PRG-51

### When should I write my own program?
You stop being a novice when you want to, and can, write your own. Start by modifying the novice program you run: copy its rep ranges and tonnage and stay minimalist. → BEG-07, BEG-63, MND-45

### Can kids and teens lift?
Children can start conservatively around 10 (goblet squats, pull-ups, push-ups), heavy squats and deadlifts at 15-16. Teens should prioritise form, work capacity and enjoyment, with no ego lifting. → BEG-83, BEG-84

### Does cardio kill gains?
No. Done right it helps through more volume and faster recovery; its real cost is calories. Never do it before lifting. → CAR-09, CAR-10, CAR-15
