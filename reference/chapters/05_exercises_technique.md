# 5. Exercise selection & technique

This chapter covers how Natural Hypertrophy picks exercises in general (compounds vs isolation, how many lifts, rotations, order, equipment) and how he wants lifts performed (form, spine, tempo, range of motion, cheating, warm-ups) plus the technique of the main lifts, with a cue table at the end. His stance: a small, stable roster of compounds plus serious isolation, chosen to fit your build and trained for months. Every rep should be controlled, aimed at the target muscle and the same from first to last. Over time he moved toward stretch-heavy, stable lifts and strict reps. Which exercise to pick for each body part is in ch. 6; strength-specific programming of the big lifts is in ch. 7.

## Compounds and isolation

Compounds give the tonnage and isolation fills what compounds miss. He counts both as required.

### EXS-01 · Compounds plus isolation, neither alone
- **Statement:** A hypertrophy program is compounds for most of the tonnage plus isolation for whatever the compounds miss. Compounds are the "shotgun", isolation the "sniper", and neither works alone.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All lifters training for size.
- **Confidence:** high — ~25 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does the program combine compound lifts for most of the tonnage with isolation for the muscles they miss?
- **Sources:** {{QwoBxHI7NBU@312}} {{g4N7gpzHm5k@126}} {{nmgidpn3DNM@1516}} {{jUMmu583w34@1420}}

### EXS-02 · Isolation is as serious as compounds
- **Statement:** Isolation gets the same priority and effort as compounds, not a token "3 sets of curls" tacked on at the end where it gets skipped. It taxes the nervous system less, so be generous with it.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All; aimed at strength-template followers with small arms.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2021→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is isolation work programmed with real sets and effort rather than as optional leftovers?
- **Sources:** {{w4-2c3iWvDQ@1703}} {{d1eLAd5WvIA@313}} {{NPlGK0-7h0A@1937}} {{Z-hGdKz28RY@1320}}

### EXS-05 · The dominant muscle takes the work
- **Statement:** In a compound the strongest muscle does most of the work and leverages decide activation, so some "targeted" muscles barely grow. ## How many exercises, variations and rotations

Variety should come from how a few lifts are trained, not from many lifts. Rotations swap close variations for a reason.

### EXS-13 · Keep the exercise list short
- **Statement:** Variety comes from what you do with a few lifts, not from many lifts. Too many exercises means each is trained too rarely, technique and tonnage become unstable, and you become "a technician of new tricks rather than a master".
- **Numbers:** 20, 45 or 55 lifts named as too many
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** high — ~15 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Is each muscle trained with a small set of lifts done often enough to progress?
- **Sources:** {{uRWgz3nhqds@127}} {{Zkhc-j-6ptU@735}} {{dyeeKOQJhrM@620}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@4087}}

### EXS-14 · Exercise and variation counts by level
- **Statement:** The number of exercises per muscle and variations per lift grows with training age but stays small.
- **Numbers:** 2020: one method keeps a list of 15-20 lifts per body part to choose from. 2022: he rotates 6-7 favourites per muscle; biceps: beginner 1 exercise, NH 4-6. 2023: variations per lift novice 0-1, intermediate 1-2, advanced 3-5, max 5 "hard" variations per pattern. 2024: novices 1-2 exercises per muscle, never more than 4-5; small variations unlimited.
- **Scope:** Novice → advanced.
- **Confidence:** medium — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does a novice program use 1-2 exercises per muscle (never more than 4-5)?
- **Sources:** {{wJTax5ziJUY@125}} {{BW-32tYoNxw@759}} {{1TKqwXCge9E@1960}} {{ZLheQPgEfoA@1974}}

### EXS-15 · Hard vs soft variations
- **Statement:** A soft variation keeps the pattern and changes the implement, grip, pause or rep range (near-full carryover, unlimited). A hard variation is a different pattern (pull-up vs pulldown, squat vs leg press), is a new skill, and should be capped. Rotate soft variations to prevent overuse without changing the pattern.
- **Numbers:** max ~5 hard variations
- **Scope:** All, especially intermediate+.
- **Confidence:** high — ~5 videos, 2023→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{1TKqwXCge9E@1899}} {{1TKqwXCge9E@2273}} {{43F9unmSc_I@695}} {{c7hl9u3h_s0@6814}}

### EXS-16 · Change a lift only for a reason
- **Statement:** Add or swap a variation only for a reason: a stall despite changing rep ranges, technique, overuse or injury. Never for boredom-driven novelty or to "shock the muscle" (he rates that D tier); constant switching keeps you relearning.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does every exercise change have a stated reason (stall, overuse, injury, technique) rather than "muscle confusion"?
- **Sources:** {{FtwMg4iM5kA@0}} {{5ANLeP4QvX8@1767}} {{1TKqwXCge9E@1516}} {{yUecX6qONXg@990}}

### EXS-17 · Rotation options must hit the muscle the same way
- **Statement:** Rotations alternate close variations in one slot (week A / week B). Good pairs: Kroc/Meadows rows, RDL/stiff-leg, V-bar/rope pushdowns, bench/close-grip, dips/pause dips. Bad pairs: bench vs OHP, floor press vs paused bench, deadlift vs RDL, close-grip vs skull crusher, sumo vs conventional deficit. An "or" in his programs means pick one or rotate weekly, never both in one session.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** high — ~15 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{hi-kNbZnXz0@127}} {{z9MDwMO8K4Y@509}} {{BW-32tYoNxw@633}} {{DA7QvIAyptw@1199}}

### EXS-18 · Train a lift at least every 10-12 days
- **Statement:** A lift practised less often than every 10-12 days doesn't belong in the program; heavy rotation that drops a lift to once a month is too rare. Big compounds should stay one variation; isolation can rotate more freely.
- **Numbers:** every 10-12 days minimum; weekly rotation only after 6-12 months of training
- **Scope:** All; weekly rotation for lifters with 6-12+ months.
- **Confidence:** medium — 3 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{6sFaB3rAY1A@632}} {{Rc9jhSVGoxM@570}} {{gpFdh0eBMGc@623}}

### EXS-19 · When to rotate: scheduled or by need
- **Statement:** He has said both that rotations must be scheduled and that they should only happen "when the muscle needs it". The 2023 version (scheduled for most, autoregulated for advanced) may reconcile them.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** medium — 3 videos, 2020→2023

- **Statement:** Rotating a lift out at every plateau teaches you to quit and resets you to beginner neural gains; grind through instead. Rotating back exercises every two months means never breaking through.

## Choosing and dropping lifts

No lift is mandatory; lifts are judged by fit, enjoyment, stretch, stability and stimulus-to-fatigue over months of use.

### EXS-23 · No lift is mandatory
- **Statement:** For a bodybuilder every lift, including squat, bench and deadlift, can be replaced by another in the same pattern. If a lift hurts after a fair try, plenty of others do the job.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bodybuilders; powerlifters must do competition lifts.
- **Confidence:** high — ~12 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{Eh0fnXIyDMc@188}} {{0RoULYC_tao@188}} {{sFMQj4Lflk0@756}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@883}}

### EXS-24 · Fit the lifts to your build
- **Statement:** Leverages, height and mobility decide which lifts suit you; the program adapts to the lifter. Drop lifts that don't suit you instead of clinging to them (sunk cost).
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All; tall lifters often do better with sumo, low-bar to parallel, RDL/stiff-leg, block pulls, OHP.
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Were the main lifts chosen to suit the lifter's build and pain-free history?
- **Sources:** {{iCWUcBy7Vxs@187}} {{S0qBQppUtTQ@740}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@816}} {{pt4mbNb7NJ0@8505}}

### EXS-25 · Enjoyment beats theoretical optimality
- **Statement:** A lift that is 75% optimal and that you actually do beats a 100% optimal lift you skip. Remove exercises you dislike; advanced lifters especially should refuse them.
- **Numbers:** 75% vs 100% optimal
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current

### EXS-26 · Judge a new lift after about three months
- **Statement:** Start a new lift light and judge it only after training it seriously for about three months, by progress rather than feel; allow a tuning phase. Only program lifts you have done regularly for 3+ months. Keep a ranked list of lifts done, a "to try" list and an "exercise graveyard".
- **Numbers:** ≥3 months trial; tuning phase up to 6 months
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** high — ~6 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Sources:** {{gWdAfpJIXbc@790}} {{rlWIogS2Qho@129}} {{DAZRYoex8_w@950}} {{qrBLykvU_EE@753}}

### EXS-27 · What makes a good hypertrophy lift
- **Statement:** His criteria: stretch under load, high stability, good stimulus-to-fatigue (judged per target muscle), low cardio cost, quick setup, and progressable. A lift without a stretch can't rank above "decent"; if form breaks as soon as you load it, it's useless for hypertrophy.
- **Numbers:** setup under ~3 min or it gets skipped
- **Scope:** All, 2023-24 framework.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Do the main lifts load the target in a stretch, stay stable under load and set up in a few minutes?
- **Sources:** {{jUMmu583w34@1280}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@569}} {{6yW3GSskl8g@1391}} {{9kG_0Y7UYnw@821}}

### EXS-28 · Prefer lifts with a negative
- **Statement:** Most lifts should have a controlled eccentric. Positive-only or explosive lifts (Olympic lifts, Pendlay rows, dumped deadlifts) are weaker for hypertrophy; safely loading a lengthened muscle gives the best gains.

## Order and structure of the session

### EXS-41 · Open with the heaviest compound
- **Statement:** Always open with the heaviest, most demanding compound or strength lift, never with isolation (laterals, calf raises, leg extensions, leg curls, pulldowns, shrugs). You are freshest at the start, and the last exercises get skipped, so order by importance.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** All.
- **Confidence:** high — ~15 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** current
- **Check:** Does each session open with its heaviest compound rather than an isolation lift?

## What good form is

Technique is how tonnage gets directed into the target muscle. Form rules exist, but they adapt to the individual.

### TEC-01 · Bodybuilding form: target, pain-free, reproducible
- **Statement:** Good bodybuilding form targets the intended muscle, is pain-free and is reproducible every rep; how much you lift is not a criterion. It may mean lifting less at first, but it recovers better and correlates with long-term progress.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Scope:** Bodybuilders.
- **Confidence:** high — ~8 videos, 2020→2023
- **Status:** current

- **Statement:** Execution is about half of results and programming the other half; a great program executed badly won't deliver. Form is "mandatory" to lift for years without setbacks.
