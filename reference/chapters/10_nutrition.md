# 10. Nutrition & supplements

This chapter covers what NH says about food, energy balance, protein and macros, meal timing, fasting, food quality, individual food choices, drinks, the psychology of eating, and supplements. His stance: for naturals, training drives growth and diet only fuels it, though diet is the top factor for long-term health. He eats mostly whole, home-cooked, carb-heavy food with modest protein, tailors it to his own body and ancestry, and treats supplements as "D tier", taking only creatine, MSM, bee pollen and a little whey. Bulking, cutting and cardio strategy are in ch. 11.

## Where diet ranks

He ranks training above diet for building muscle, but diet above everything for health and longevity. Once a diet works, it should run on autopilot.

### NUT-01 · Training matters more than diet for naturals
- **Statement:** For a natural lifter, training drives muscle growth and diet only fuels it; the "diet is 70-80%" line is nonsense that comes from drug users. Nutrition only matters once training is on point.
- **Numbers:** Training is "at least 50%" (2020), "80% or more" (2023)
- **Scope:** Naturals, all levels; training's share grows as you advance
- **Confidence:** high — ~15 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — 2020: training is at least 50%; 2023: training is 80% or more
- **Check:** Does the program treat training, not diet, as the main driver of muscle gain?

## Protein

He thinks protein needs are heavily overstated and eats about half of the usual recommendation.

### NUT-16 · Protein needs are vastly overrated
- **Statement:** He eats far less protein than commonly recommended and still grows; eating much more gave him health problems and no extra gains.
- **Numbers:** 120-140 g/day at 95 kg (2020-11, 140 only on heavy days); 50-70 g at 210 lb (2021-04); ~70 g max (2022-03); ~100 g ≈ 0.5 g/lb at 215-220 lb (2024-03); 100-120 g (2024-12)
- **Scope:** Naturals; him at ~210-220 lb
- **Confidence:** high — ~10 videos, 2020→2024
- **Status:** changed — his own intake fell from 120-140 g (2020) to 50-70 g (2021-22), then rose to ~100-120 g (2024); the view that high protein is unnecessary never changed

- **Statement:** Fear of muscle loss from fasting is a myth created by supplement companies: muscle is expensive and preserved, and true muscle breakdown comes only deep into starvation. Muscle lost during fasts comes from training performance dropping.
